**For:** Dale Grant's planning agent (SOT v8.2 holder). **Date:** 30 Sep 2026. **Status:** recommendation, evidence-backed, ready to fold into the SOT.
**Evidence base:** 17 research notes (≈1,000 traced URLs), 81 priced UK listings + a 54-listing South East servicing scan, Companies House accounts for 21 scale-up cases, HMRC trade data (198 tariff queries), and a 20,000-path Monte Carlo model. ## 1. The decision in one paragraph

Buy a small contracted-servicing business in the South East in 2028–29 (electrical compliance testing / critical-power maintenance first choice, HVAC servicing fallback), pay no more than 2.3× EBITDA with a 50–80% vendor note and a 10-year bank loan, keep the manager, and take a modest owner draw (~£35–45k) while covering the rest of living costs with about two 13-week contracting blocks a year. Run a one-line B2B import probe (£5k) from 2027 alongside. Taper contracting only as the draw grows. This is the highest-probability route to £750k invested by end-2034 that also delivers day-to-day independence from 2028, and it is 20+ points ahead of every alternative examined (build, AWS partner, AI app, contracting plus investing).

## 3. Why this shape, in evidence

- **What is actually for sale in the envelope is servicing, not distribution.** SE scan (29 Sep 2026): 5 electrical-compliance/critical-power firms (e.g. London EICR firm, T/O £498k, EBITDA £167k, 90% recurring; two London/SE firms where shareholders will stay), 3 HVAC (Surrey £295k for £96k NP = 3.1×), ~5 HVAC elsewhere at 2.05–2.5× NP. Water hygiene: none in SE. Lifts: none on the open market (LDC-backed Pace and Deltron buy Kent/London independents off-market).
- **Servicing is the cheapest recurring revenue on the market.** Entry 2.05–3.3× vs 2.8× distribution, 3.4× hire. 85–100% maintenance-contract revenue in the good ones. Regulatory calendars (BS 7671 EICR intervals, F-gas, L8, RRO, LOLER) drive demand regardless of technology winners.
- **Exit demand exists.** PE-backed consolidators did 70% of 2025 fire/security deals (Grant Thornton); Nordic Climate Group rolling up SE HVAC; Marlowe/Ranger in compliance. Small independents are what they buy.
- **Fit.** PrinciplesYou: Shaper, Non-Conforming 96, Independent 94, Demanding 96, Empathetic 1, Detail-Oriented 15. Electrical compliance is a systematic, low-drama, data-on-every-asset business; customers buy on certificate and price, engineers are less scarce than F-gas. HVAC is people-heavy with summer emergencies. Distribution suits the profile but needs inventory detail and supplier relationships; better as acquisition #2 with an operator. Fit moves P(£750k) by ≈15 points across 0.8–1.2 (50%→69%).
- **"Become the supplier" is real but is an exit, not a dynasty.** Companies House: HTS Spares (non-genuine JCB parts) £13.9m T/O, £2.0m PBT, founded 2011 by buying an existing trade; Hydraquip ~25 yrs to £31m then sold for ~£65m; R&G Fluid Power 14 acquisitions in 5 yrs with an investor, sold ~£100m. No UK distribution/servicing group reached £25m+ from one small acquisition without outside money. "Largest independent" was a sale trigger in every case (Cromwell, Hayley, Hydraquip, Segen, Weldability, Data Select). Solar's top distributors pre-dated the FIT by 4–6 years; no year-3–5 entrant reached the top tier. Conclusion: enter as the buyer of the fragmented servicing/parts layer; target a £1–1.5m exit, not £30m.
- **Forward demand is a filter, not a business type.** Heat pumps, EV charging, data-centre power and grid work show up as more compliance tests, more critical-power maintenance and more installers needing parts. Pick the firm and the probe line for those customers. Drones/robotics/flight: no fleet, no ageing-owner base, no spares trade yet (UK drone imports ≈ £30m, DJI-dominated). Hold as a post-2034 second cycle funded by the first.

## 6. What "doing it right" means (the conditions the probability depends on)

1. Price ≤2.3× adjusted EBITDA and ≥50% vendor finance. Walk away otherwise; asking prices (2.6–3.1×) cost 10 points.
2. Buy the manager, not the owner. Shareholder/manager stays ≥12 months on a retention; the seller's relationships transfer in writing (contract novations).
3. ≥80% recurring contract revenue, verified from the contract register and 3 years of bank statements, not the teaser.
4. Personal guarantee never exceeds the risk pot; floor never below £60k, and only at completion.
5. Two contracting blocks a year stay in the plan until the draw alone clears £45k. This is the SC/energy backstop, not optional.
6. One block only in flight: probe → purchase → bolt-on. No app, no AWS partnership, no second venture until the firm's draw covers living costs.
7. Crypto sleeve sized once a year (10–25%), never topped up from the risk pot.
8. Health: 3 days/week is the cap; a Crohn's flare means the manager runs the firm for a month, which is why the manager condition is non-negotiable.

## 7. What was rejected, and why

- **Build instead of buy** (services, import, AWS, app): 15–33%. Too slow to £70k profit inside the window; worst fit scores.
- **Contracting plus investing alone**: 29%; also fails the "hate working" and health constraints (≈1,000 employed days).
- **Full-time business with no contracting**: 45–48%; loses the cash build and the SC backstop.
- **Lifts, water hygiene**: no SE supply. **Distribution first**: fewer listings, higher multiple; keep as acquisition #2.
- **Riding a wave (solar-type, drones)**: consolidation evidence says year-3–5 entrants do not become the large supplier; enter as the buyer later.
