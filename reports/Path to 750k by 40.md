# Cash Above the Floor Decides Everything

On the evidence assembled, no route as currently specified reaches £750k invested by end-2034 with better than about 10% probability: the SOT plan as written (one pre-sales block, then buy an importer-distributor) scores **0.9%**, its best labour-front-loaded variant (two blocks a year for two years, then search) **3.3%**, three blocks a year **5.4%**, and the one route that beats them on money, building an AWS/AI consultancy where Dale sells and delivers, **7.8%** (10.2% before the fit penalty) at 40 hours a week of the sell-and-deliver shape the SOT says he cannot sustain (model: results_summary.md). The reason is arithmetic, not sector: £150–170k of investable cash minus a £120k floor that is never touched leaves **£30–50k** to buy with, a £300k business needs **£110–130k** of cash outside the floor once the deposit, a working-capital reserve and £20k of deal costs are counted, one 13-week block nets roughly **£23k** against a **£20k** burn, and at 2.8–3× with 65–75% debt the owner's draw after a £55k manager is near zero for the first three to four years (model: params_evidence.md; SOT v8.2). The SOT's £70–80k draw needs a business with £125–135k of EBITDA before any debt service, i.e. an enterprise value of roughly £400–450k at 3×, and more once the vendor note and bank loan are being repaid. What moves the number is buying at 2.3× rather than 3× (10% versus 0.9% across the plausible range), starting with £200k rather than £160k, and time: at 44 rather than 40 the hybrid plan's odds roughly double and the labour route's reach 28% (model: results_summary.md). The route the evidence supports is still the SOT's own shape, an owner-as-seller with a manager underneath, because it is the only one where fit, health and the listing data point the same way; what the evidence does not support is the date, the deal size and the draw the SOT has attached to it.

The first thing the table shows is that **the deal itself is the bottleneck, not the outcome after it**. A pure buy route with no contracting closes a deal in only 5% of paths; the SOT plan with its one block closes in 19%; two blocks a year for two years lifts that to 40%. The market is not the constraint, since the listing scan found roughly 30 relevant distributor listings and about 50 services, hire and logistics listings priced £150–600k on one day ([Daltons wholesale category](https://www.daltonsbusiness.com/wholesale-and-distribution-businesses-for-sale/); [BusinessesForSale commercial cleaning](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/commercial-cleaning-businesses-for-sale)). The constraint is cash. In the model a deal only completes if the cash above the floor covers at least 60% of the equity the deal needs, and the deals that do complete are scaled down to what the cash allows: the median completed deal is **£151k of enterprise value on £60k of EBITDA for £71k of cash** in the pure buy route, and **£231k on £85k of EBITDA for £108k of cash** in the two-block variant (model: results.json, conditional.median_ev_closed). That is a £150–250k business, not the £250–400k the SOT sizes for.

The plain arithmetic runs as follows. Investable cash of £150–170k less the £120k floor is **£30–50k** (SOT v8.2, WEALTH LADDER). A 13-week block at the £625/day Solutions Architect and Presales median, 3.5 days a week, grosses about £28,500 ([ITJobsWatch Solutions Architect contracts](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/contracts/uk/solutions%20architect.do); [ITJobsWatch Presales contracts](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/contracts/uk/presales.do)); if it is the only income in the tax year it nets roughly 80%, about **£23k**, after 19% corporation tax on profits above the £12,570 salary and 10.75% basic-rate dividend tax ([Contractor UK dividends 2026/27](https://www.contractoruk.com/news/dividends-in-2026-27-an-explainer-for-contractors); [GOV.UK corporation tax rates](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax)). Against a £20k burn, one block adds about £3k a year to the risk pot; the index return on £160k adds about £9k. A £300k deal with a 35% vendor note leaves a £195k cash gap; practitioner norms say lenders fund 50–70% of price and want 30–50% from the buyer ([Buy a Business Ltd, May 2026](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026); [Capitalise, Aug 2026](https://capitalise.com/gb/insights/business-loans/loan-to-buy-a-business)), and the SOT's own rule caps guaranteed debt at the risk pot, so even at the model's generous reading (a personal guarantee of 35% of the facility must sit inside cash above the floor, following the 20–50% PG caps advisers describe under the Growth Guarantee Scheme, [Connection Technologies, Sep 2026](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/growth-guarantee-scheme-personal-guarantee)) the buyer must find about **£80k of deposit, £20k of deal costs and a £30k working-capital reserve: £110–130k** ([Zoppi legal costs](https://www.zoppi.co.uk/blog/understanding-legal-costs-for-buying-a-business-with-real-numbers); [Consult EFC FDD guide](https://consultefc.com/financial-due-diligence-smes-uk-guide/)). Forty thousand pounds does not buy that business, and one block a year does not close the gap before 2030.

The second piece of arithmetic is the draw. A £300k business bought at 2.8× has £107k of EBITDA. A £105k vendor note over three years at Bank Rate plus 3% (6.75%) costs about £40k a year, and a £120k bank loan at 11% over five years about £32.5k ([Valius, Aug 2026](https://www.valius.global/blog/what-is-deferred-consideration); [Allica business loans](https://www.allica.bank/business-loans); [Bank of England, Sep 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026)). Listed EBITDA is adjusted, i.e. the owner's pay is added back, so a replacement operations manager costs the business about £55k fully loaded on the £46,709 Indeed average ([Indeed UK operations manager salaries](https://uk.indeed.com/career/operations-manager/salaries); [HMRC employer rates 2026/27](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027)). EBITDA £107k minus £55k minus £72k of debt service is **−£20k** in years one to three, about **+£20k** once the vendor note is repaid, and about **£52k** only after the bank loan is gone in year six. The model's median draw for the SOT plan is **£9,129, £9,224, £8,589 and £8,038** in the first four years of ownership (model: results_summary.md). The SOT's "~30% earnings yield at 3×" is true before the manager and before the debt; after both it is roughly zero for the years that matter. Reaching a £70–80k draw after a £55k manager needs £125–135k of EBITDA before debt service, an enterprise value of about £400–450k at 3× and closer to £550k once years one to five carry £50–70k of debt service, which is outside the £200–450k envelope and outside the cash.

The two contracting comparators bracket the problem. One block a year plus index funds reaches a median of only £204k by 2034 and breaches the floor in 28% of paths, because £23k of net block income minus £20k of burn is close to a standstill (model: results_summary.md); three blocks a year, about 757 employed days over the eight years, reaches £500k in 32% of paths and £750k in 4%, but that is the five-day, sustained shape the SOT rules out on health grounds and the model does not penalise it (model: params.json, contract_heavy.fit_multiplier). Index arithmetic confirms that contributions dominate: £150k with no new money compounds to £239k at 6% over eight years, and hitting £750k unaided needs 22.3% a year, which no forecaster's range approaches (Vanguard 4.2–5.2% GBP for US equities, AQR 4.2% real for global developed, JP Morgan 7.0% USD for global equities) ([Vanguard UK outlook Dec 2025](https://www.vanguard.co.uk/content/dam/intl/europe/documents/en/vanguard-economic-and-market-outlook-for-2026-ai-exuberance-gbp-en-pro.pdf); [AQR 2026 CMA](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---2026-Capital-Market-Assumptions.pdf?sc_lang=en); [J.P. Morgan LTCMA 2026](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/institutional/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-releases-2026-long-term-capital-market-assumptions/)).

Third, **retirement is the dominant stated motive** where a reason is given (39% of distributors, 42% of logistics), consistent with 23.8% of UK companies having an average director age of 60 or over and age or succession driving 41% of UK&I sales in H2 2025 ([ExitRadar Companies House analysis](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-business-exit-statistics); [Dealsuite UK&I Feb 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-february-2026)).

On price, the transaction data and the listing data disagree in a way that favours the buyer who waits. Adviser-reported UK multiples for a £200k-EBITDA company are 3.6× (Dealsuite H1 2026) and 3.7× for sub-£2.5m deals (MarktoMarket), but half of UK&I sellers start 23% above the achievable price and a quarter of those deals collapse on it ([Dealsuite UK&I Aug 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-august-2026-copy); [MarktoMarket H1 2026](https://marktomarket.io/ma-valuation-indices-h1-2026/)), the two best-documented UK owner-buyer cases both bought at 3× ([Acquiring Minds, Sam Turner](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos); [Acquiring Minds, Paul Quirk](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)), and applying Dealsuite's size discount to its sector table gives about 2.3× for wholesale and retail against 4.2× for business services ([CLFI citing Dealsuite](https://clfi.co.uk/insights/ebitda-multiples-by-industry-uk-2025/)). The model uses 3.0× (p90 4.0×) for distributors and 2.8× for the hybrid; the listings say services and logistics sellers ask 2.3–2.6× on stated profit today, and that gap is worth more to the number than anything else in the plan.

The personality evidence supports the seat without oversupporting it. Single traits correlate with business success at r ≈ .10–.30 and the whole Big Five explains about 10% of performance variance; the strongest signals in the literature, need for achievement (r = .30), innovativeness (.27), proactivity (.27) and self-efficacy (.25), map onto the Demanding, Growth-Seeking and Non-Conforming scores, while the low-Agreeableness cluster (Empathetic 1%, Modest 10%) is unrelated to performance in the 2010 meta-analysis ([Rauch & Frese via Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007); [Zhao, Seibert & Lumpkin 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309335187)). The weakness the SOT names as decisive, Implementer-lowest and low Detail-Oriented, is on the evidence a cost to design around rather than a disqualifier: the dependability facet of conscientiousness does not distinguish entrepreneurs from managers at all (d = .01) while achievement striving does strongly (d = .59), and personality predicts early traction, not long-run survival, which depends on capital, execution and market ([Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007); [Kerr, Kerr & Xu 2018](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)). Task-related skill outperforms generic experience in the human-capital meta-analysis, which is the right reading of face-to-face selling, but the overall effect is small (rc ≈ .10) and same-sector experience is the replicated survival factor, lowering exit odds by about 20% ([Unger et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusvent.2009.09.004); [Xi et al. 2020](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)), which argues for the two lunches (crane hire, JCB parts) as more than social.

The health evidence is the sharpest constraint and it points at structure rather than route. Pooled IBD data show 16% absenteeism, 36% presenteeism and 39% overall work impairment; in Crohn's the impairment is 12% in remission and 51% in moderate-to-severe disease, about half of patients flare at least once a year, and high fatigue (47% of Crohn's patients) roughly quadruples the odds of presenteeism (aOR 4.20) without reducing the chance of being employed at all ([JCC 2024 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjae057); [Crohn's & Colitis 360, 2022](https://academic.oup.com/crohnscolitis360/article/4/3/otac023/6608899); [CorEvitas registry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11684069/)). The accommodations most wanted and least provided are flexible hours (65% needed, 41% provided) and remote work (41% vs 21%), the Cochrane review CCUK cites finds health gains only for worker-controlled flexibility, and the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing finds that after a chronic-disease diagnosis working conditions improve only for the self-employed ([PMC6390415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6390415/); [CCUK submission 2021](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/media/4ermv5lj/crohns-colitis-uk-making-flexible-working-the-default-nov-2021.pdf); [ELSA analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6161656/)). Applied to a 40-hour norm that gives a planning envelope of about 35 effective hours in remission, 27 in mild activity and 20 in a flare, and the counterweight is that self-employed people with chronic conditions report business-maintenance losses and describe a "temporal substitute", someone who can run things during a flare, as both the support and the worry ([Yagil & Cohen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3445); [Yagil & Cohen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/13591053241239462)). That is the manager the SOT already requires, and it is why the model's routes without one (build, buy SaaS, buy agency) carry the lowest fit, and why routes whose year-one hours are 35–45 sit above the envelope in a flare year.

Two more findings bear directly on how the plan is run. Partnered searches out-return solo searches (IRR 40.5% vs 30.3%), solo founders who succeed do so by hiring early or attaching to allies and benefactors, and neither dataset covers a sales-oriented owner with a hired operations manager, so the SOT's structure is supported by analogy, not measured ([Stanford 2024 study](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf); [Howell & Bingham](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Howell-Travis-Bingham-Chris_Solo-vs.-Co.-Under-What-Conditions-Can-Solo-Founded-Ventures-Perform-as-well-as-Co-Founded-Ventures.pdf)). And for a serial re-opener of decisions, implementation intentions ("if situation Y, then I do X") carry one of the largest replicated effects in behavioural science, d = .65 on goal attainment, d = .61 on getting started and d = .77 on shielding a course of action from derailment, while hard commitment devices work but are taken up by only 11–28% of those offered and work best when public ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1); [NCI chapter text](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf); [Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010](http://houdekpetr.cz/!data/public_html/papers/Bryan%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). The SOT's "same conclusion twice = decided" and kill criteria written before signing are that mechanism; the evidence says make them if-then and tell one believable human.

## Decisions this changes

These are the decisions the evidence bears on, in the SOT's own terms, each with its trade-off. Where a SOT assumption does not reconcile with the numbers it is said once, here, and not repeated.

1. **The floor stays, and its price is named.** Every buy route breaches the £120k floor at some point in 19–30% of paths even with the backstop block, and the pure-build routes in 51–55% (model: results_summary.md). The floor is also the reason the number is out of reach: £160k minus £120k is £40k of risk capital, and the tornado shows start cash at £200k roughly triples the odds (2.3% → 6.3%). The evidence does not say lower the floor; it says the floor and the £750k-by-40 date cannot both be held with £160k, and the SOT's own ordering, floor first, then the business, then the number, resolves it in favour of the floor (SOT v8.2, THE ONE GOAL).

2. **The one-block rule is a health rule, not a funding plan, and the two numbers that depend on it do not reconcile.** The risk pot "£50–70k now, £80–110k after the one block" cannot be derived from £150–170k investable less a £120k floor (£30–50k) plus one block that nets about £23k against a £20k burn (net +£3k) (SOT v8.2, WEALTH LADDER; [ITJobsWatch](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/contracts/uk/solutions%20architect.do); [TaxDCal 2026/27](https://www.taxdcal.co.uk/blog/inside-vs-outside-ir35-take-home-comparison)); on the same arithmetic the "£200k coast floor by end-2027" is reached in the model at the median around 2034 on one block a year (model: results_summary.md, contract_only median NW £204,027). The sweep's honest trade-off: two blocks a year for two years is about 37 more employed days over eight years, doubles the chance of owning a business (19% → 41%) and lifts the affordable deal from £175k to £231k, but each block is roughly 45 employed days that the SOT says cost the day and the day before, plus health, and no block count gets the £750k number above 7%. A block that funds the deal, yes; a block quoted as free money, no.

3. **The sizing session in November should be run on what the cash buys, not on the £250–400k envelope.** At £40k above the floor the model's completed deals are £150k of enterprise value on £60k of EBITDA; at £108k of cash they are £231k on £85k (model: results.json). The SOT's "buy smaller (EV £250–300k, pays £40–50k)" is still a size too large: at 3× with a £55k manager and a three-year vendor note, the draw on a £250k business is negative in years one to three (SOT v8.2, THE BUSINESS; model: params_evidence.md). The trade-off is that a £150–230k business is an owner-run micro-business in the listing data (manager stated in 4–12% of cases), so "manager in place" becomes something Dale hires and pays for, which the model already prices at £50–55k and which is the third-largest lever (£0 → 4.5%, £45k → 1.8%) ([Indeed](https://uk.indeed.com/career/operations-manager/salaries); model: results_summary.md). Promoting an existing number two, as one listing describes ("two employees can effectively absorb the managing director's responsibilities"), is the cheap version of this and should be a screening question, not a hope ([Daltons B1](https://www.daltonsbusiness.com/listing/distributor-of-bearing-and-power-transmission-for-sale-DB2181381/)).

4. **The £70–80k draw assumption is retired for the £200–450k band.** It requires £125–135k of EBITDA before debt service, an enterprise value of £400–450k at 3× and more with debt, and the two best-documented UK owner-buyer cases both hit a liquidity or margin crisis within 18 months despite competent, better-capitalised buyers ([Acquiring Minds, Turner](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos); [Harber, 12 months in](https://blakeharber.substack.com/p/12-months-into-our-1m-acquisition)). The model's median draw is £8–9k a year for four years on the SOT plan and £25–38k with every favourable assumption stacked (model: results_summary.md). The trade-off is a rewrite of Layer 2 and Layer 3 of the wealth ladder: business profit does not build the stack in years one to four; debt repayment builds equity in the business instead, which only becomes money at a sale (18% BADR on the first £1m from April 2026, [GOV.UK BADR](https://www.gov.uk/business-asset-disposal-relief)) and the SOT says a sale is never on the critical path.

5. **Buy on achieved multiples, and let the multiple choose the sector, not the label.** The multiple is the single largest lever (2.0× → 10.0%, 3.6× → 0.9%), listed services and logistics ask 2.3–2.6× on stated profit against 3.4× for hire and 3.5× for SaaS and agencies, and the only verifiable in-range distributor point is 1.4× on a retirement sale ([BSR MRO parts](https://www.business-sale.com/companies-for-sale/distributor-of-industrial-maintenance-parts-wholesale-uk-wide-669977); [Daltons cleaning](https://www.daltonsbusiness.com/listing/domestic-commercial-and-window-cleaning-for-sale-DB2490217/); model: results_summary.md). The five real sets of accounts and the one-page buy criteria should carry a hard ceiling on the entry multiple of adjusted EBITDA after a manager's salary, because that number, not the sector, is what the model says moves the outcome. The trade-off is fewer candidates: the SOT's lead candidate, the exclusive-line importer-distributor, is the best structural fit (fit 1.10, payroll 16% of revenue) but its exclusive agency can be terminated on 3–12 months' notice with no statutory compensation, so the cheap multiple has to be checked against the agreement's term and change-of-control clause before anything else ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-distribution/england-and-wales)).

6. **Seller finance is negotiated, not found; make it a term sheet item, not a filter.** Zero of 81 UK listings advertise it; practitioner norms put 20–40% deferred over two to three years at Bank Rate plus 2–4 points, subordinated to any bank; and pushing it from 35% to 50% with a ten-year GGS term adds only half a point to the number (model: results_summary.md; [Valius](https://www.valius.global/blog/what-is-deferred-consideration)). Its real value is the one the cases show: the lender's number can drop in the final week and the seller note is what bridged it ([Acquiring Minds, Quirk](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)). The SOT's "≥30% seller finance" filter would exclude every listing on its face; as a heads-of-terms requirement it is right.

7. **The five-buyer test stands, and its arithmetic is now explicit.** The app built from inside the bought business's customers adds 0.8 points (3.3% → 4.0%) and lifts P(£750k given a deal) from 8% to 10%, at £30k from the risk pot two years after completion and eight hours a week as architect and seller (model: results_summary.md; params.json, hybrid_plan_app.app_option). The same app built standalone scores 1.9% (9.0% before fit) and breaches the floor in 55% of paths (model: results_summary.md). That is the SOT's 10% argument in numbers: the acquisition buys the right to a real but small bet, and the standalone version is the 0/6 shot. One yes by 30 November boxes a budget and a quarter; none closes it for 2027 (SOT v8.2, OASIFY).

8. **The backstop is the likely reality, and the SOT's rule about it will be tested.** In 40–65% of buy-route paths the cash falls within £10k of the floor at least once and the model fires a contracting block, on average about one in the pure buy routes and 0.9 in the two-block hybrid (model: results.json, p_backstop_ever). The SOT says the odd contract is a top-up never used to cover the business's own numbers and never before the first 100 days; the model says that in most paths it is used exactly to protect the floor from the business's own numbers. The decision is whether to write that rule as an if-then in advance (if cash above the floor falls below £X in a quarter, then one block, pre-sales seat, three days) rather than discover it in a bad week.

9. **The horizon is the honest release valve.** Nothing in the evidence changes the goal; the horizon table changes its probability more than any route does (hybrid_3blocks 5.1% → 8.6% → 11.5%; hybrid_2blocks 3.2% → 5.7% → 7.9%). The SOT's pass/fail at 40 is a business that paid him last year while he was on holiday, and that test is met in 40–49% of hybrid paths; the £750k figure can be the by-product it is described as, on a date the evidence supports (SOT v8.2, THE ONE GOAL; model: results_summary.md).

10. **The AWS-partner result is recorded, not recommended.** It is the top money line (7.8%, median £285k, floor breached 8%) because it is the most days at the day rate for the longest, and it is listed here so that the loop "just build the consultancy" is answered with its own number and its own hours (40 a week for two to three years) instead of reopened (model: results_summary.md; params.json).

## Limitations: what the researchers could not get

The listing pool is a one-day sample of what could be fetched. Rightbiz returned HTTP 403 on every page (its own titles claim 793 wholesale and distribution, 228 haulage and 76 plant-hire listings), BusinessesForSale.com blocked every individual listing page (category pages worked, so reason for sale, owner role and finance terms are missing for those rows), Bizdaq returned HTTP 500, KBS Corporate and Knightsbridge sit behind JavaScript portals, Turner Butler rendered empty, Christie & Co, Ernest Wilson, RTA and Redwoods Dowling Kerr were not searched after the search budget ran out, and Daltons pagination beyond page one failed, so roughly 150 of its 178 wholesale listings were never reviewed ([Rightbiz wholesale](https://www.rightbiz.co.uk/wholesale-businesses-for-sale-in-uk.html); [BFS robots.txt](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/robots.txt)). Business Sale Report masks profit for non-members, so only two in-range distributor listings yield a computable multiple. Flippa and Acquire.com listing pages are JavaScript-rendered and Acquire.com's report PDFs sat behind a captcha, so live small-SaaS and ecommerce supply is represented by published sold comps rather than listings. Every one of the fourteen notes reports that its web-search budget was exhausted part-way through.

The base rates are borrowed from other populations. There is no UK study of the survival of acquired versus founded small businesses; the best proxy is a French INSEE cohort (takeovers 57% vs start-ups 55% alive at 5.5 years, advantage concentrated in years one to two) and US SBA 7(a) loan tapes, and the most relevant recent paper (van Balen et al. 2026 on survival after takeover entry) was paywalled ([Xi et al. 2020](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf); [Lendesca SBA analysis](https://www.lendesca.com/buying-an-existing-business-is-the-safest-sba-loan-the-numbers-prove-it-the-lowest-charge-off-rate-in-sba-lending-belongs-to-one-loan-type-heres-the-data/)). Search-fund and self-funded-search data (Stanford, IESE, Yale, SIG) describe elite-MBA, full-time, investor-vetted searchers buying businesses five to twenty times larger than £300k; the Stanford 2026 PDF could not be parsed and SIG explicitly declined to measure unconditional search success, so the "15–30 of 100 close within 18 months" figure is an inference and the model's 0.30 per search-year rests on it ([SIG 2023](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf); [Yale 2025](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf)). No survey anywhere reports post-acquisition owner hours by year or the share who install a general manager; the hours in the model are inferred, and the health note found no measurement of sustainable weekly hours in active IBD, only impairment percentages. No UK source gives the prevalence or median size of vendor finance in completed sub-£1m deals, heads-of-terms-to-completion fall-through rates, sold prices for small UK services businesses, or the true sell-through rate of UK listings (the "only 20% sell" figure is unsourced folklore). Filed accounts for the two UK AWS-partner exits (Ubertas, Rebura) are scanned image PDFs that could not be read. The IFS owner-manager income paper, the BDO PCPI, IBISWorld margin pages, British Business Bank scheme pages, Glassdoor, Quiet Light and Reddit all returned 403, 405 or gated content.

The model has its own simplifications. It runs in annual steps; it scales every deal down to the cash available rather than letting a buyer walk; it implements "guaranteed debt ≤ the risk pot" as a 35%-of-facility personal guarantee that must fit within cash above the floor, which is more generous than the SOT's literal rule and is why the deal probabilities are not lower still; it does not correlate index returns with business outcomes; it loads a health-recovery parameter but does not use it, so contracting's health cost appears only as hours; it counts the AWS consultancy's own billed days as hours rather than employed days, flattering that route's day count; and the services, hire and logistics parameters were generated before the listing note for those sectors existed, from sector and multiples notes instead, although the later listing medians (asks £285k, £400k, £325k; multiples 2.5×, 3.4×, 2.6×) sit close to the values used (£280k, £350k, £300k; 3.0×, 3.5×, 2.8×) (model: params_evidence.md; listings note). The fit multipliers are coordinator judgement applied to sourced evidence, not measured effects; the raw column shows every route without them, and the ranking does not change. Finally, the BADR rate and the dividend rates used are the enacted 2026/27 figures; the Autumn Budget 2026 had not been published at the time of research ([GOV.UK BADR](https://www.gov.uk/business-asset-disposal-relief); [ATT 2026/27 updates](https://www.att.org.uk/technical/news/202627-tax-year-updates-housekeeping-individuals)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The SOT treats sector choice as the open decision and the numbers (£120k floor, one block, £70–80k draw, £750k by 2034) as settled; the evidence says the reverse: the shape of the business is the well-supported part, and it is the money assumptions that do not reconcile with each other. A buyer with £40k above an untouchable floor is a £150–230k buyer, a £150–230k business run by a paid manager pays its owner almost nothing until its debt is gone, and no amount of front-loaded labour turns that into £750k in eight years without either a cheaper entry multiple, more starting cash or more time. The plan's own principle, floor first, then the business, then the number, already contains the answer to which of those gives.

What the evidence does support, and quantifies for the first time, is the value of the seat: the hybrid variants are the only routes where the health literature (worker-controlled schedule, a temporal substitute in place), the personality literature (achievement and selling traits, dependability irrelevant), the listing data (retiring owners of payroll-light businesses with repeat trade) and the model's fit all point the same way, and they put a business in Dale's name in two of every five paths. The decision that follows is not a new sector or a new goal but a sizing session run on the deal the cash can buy, a hard ceiling on the entry multiple after a manager's salary, and the £750k figure allowed to be what the SOT already calls it, the by-product and the insurance, on a date the arithmetic can carry.
