# Broker enquiry draft: Hornblower ref HMD242 (London electrical testing & maintenance)

Two parts. Part 1 is the first email: short, credible, asks for the information memorandum (IM) and the NDA, and puts three questions that decide whether it is worth your time. Part 2 is the full question list to send once the NDA is signed and the IM has arrived, grouped so the answers map onto the deal sheet. Brokers screen enquiries on whether the buyer is funded and specific, so the first email says both.

---

## Part 1: first email

**To:** Hornblower Business Sales (enquiry form / agent email for ref HMD242)
**Subject:** HMD242 Electrical testing & maintenance, London: funded individual buyer, request for IM

Hello,

I am interested in HMD242, the NICEIC electrical testing, maintenance and installation company in London, and would like to receive the information memorandum. Please send the NDA and I will return it the same day.

Briefly, so you can judge fit: I am an individual buyer based in the South East, with cash and a lender relationship in place for a purchase in the £300–500k range. My background is sales and solutions architecture for enterprise technology clients, and I have run my own trading business with an offshore operations team for several years. I am not an electrician and have no intention of being one. I would be buying to own and grow a compliance-led business, keeping the existing team and contractor network, with the current owner staying through a handover.

Three questions that would help me confirm this is a serious fit before we go further, if you can answer them at this stage:

1. What does the owner do day to day at present, roughly how many hours a week, and is there a second person (operations manager, scheduler, qualified supervisor) who runs the work when the owner is not there?
2. The adjusted EBITDA includes a director's replacement cost: what salary figure was used, and for what role?
3. The seller's position on structure: is a deferred element over two to four years something they would consider, and would they stay on for a 6–12 month handover?

I can move quickly on the IM, a call with the owner, and an indicative offer within two to three weeks of receiving the information.

Kind regards,
Dale Grant
[phone]

## Part 2: after the NDA, questions for the IM and the first owner call

Send as a numbered list with the IM request, or take them into the call. The headings are for you; the broker sees the questions.

### A. Your role and the day to day (what you most need to know)

1. Describe the owner's working week: what tasks, which days, on site or office, how many hours.
2. Which of those tasks could only the owner do today (relationships, pricing, NICEIC qualified supervisor duties, sign-off of certificates), and which are already done by someone else?
3. Who schedules the engineers and subcontractors each day, chases certificates and remedials, raises invoices and chases payment? Name the role, not the person.
4. Is there a qualified supervisor (QS) for the NICEIC registration who is not the owner? If the owner is the QS, who would hold it after the sale and does the registration transfer?
5. What software runs the business: scheduling, certification (e.g. an EICR/PAT certification platform), accounting, customer portal? Is it owned or subscribed?
6. Where is the business run from: office, unit, home? Lease terms if any.
7. If the owner took four weeks off tomorrow, what would break?

### B. People

8. PAYE headcount by role, length of service, salaries; who the key people are and whether they know of the sale.
9. The subcontractor network: how many electricians, how many active in the last 12 months, spend per subcontractor, how they are engaged (day rate, per test, retainer) and whether any single sub is critical.
10. Any staff or subs who would plausibly leave on a change of owner, and why.

### C. Customers and revenue quality

11. Revenue by customer for the top 10 and the share of the total they represent.
12. Split of revenue between recurring compliance testing (EICR, PAT, emergency lighting, fire alarm), remedial works that follow testing, and one-off installation.
13. How many sites or units are on an annual or multi-year testing calendar; renewal rate over the last three years.
14. Whether there are written contracts, framework agreements or just a renewal habit; any customers on 60- or 90-day terms.
15. How new work arrives: referrals, property managers, tenders, web enquiries, repeat.

### D. The numbers

16. Management accounts for YE April 2025 and year to date 2026, with the same adjustments shown as for 2020–24.
17. What happened between 2020 and 2022, when EBITDA fell from £181k to £115k, and what drove the 2024 recovery?
18. A full list of add-backs behind adjusted EBITDA, with the replacement-cost figure and role.
19. Debtor days, creditor days, and the working capital normally tied up; any bad debts in the last three years.
20. Vehicles, test equipment, stock, and anything else that transfers; anything the owner keeps.
21. Any claims, complaints, NICEIC audit findings, HMRC enquiries or disputes in the last five years.

### E. The deal

22. Reason for sale and the seller's preferred timetable.
23. Guide to price expectations, and whether a share sale or asset sale is intended.
24. Willingness to accept part of the price as deferred consideration over two to four years, and to stay on for a paid handover of 6–12 months.
25. Whether the seller would consider a short earn-out on the recurring testing book holding up through the first renewal cycle.

## What the answers tell you

- A2, A3, A4 and A7 together answer "what would I actually do". If a scheduler and a non-owner QS exist, your role is sales, pricing, subcontractor terms and the test calendar, at 2–3 days a week in year one. If the owner is the QS and the scheduler, scenario E on the deal sheet is the real case and the price has to reflect a £40–50k hire.
- C11 to C14 decide whether £147k or £115k is the right EBITDA base.
- E24 is the vendor note. Asked this early, in the open, it costs nothing and tells you whether to spend the next month on this firm.
