# Personality, health and work design as predictors of business success — evidence notes

Scope: quantitative evidence (meta-analyses, cohort studies, registry/industry datasets) on (1) personality traits and venture survival/profit, (2) sales ability and prior experience, (3) solo founders vs teams/early hires, (4) Crohn's/IBD and work, (5) self-employment and health for people with chronic conditions, (6) owner working hours and burnout, (7) acquisition entrepreneurs / search funds, (8) decision-making, pre-commitment and implementation intentions. All figures below trace to the linked source. Where a number came only from a search-engine excerpt and could not be verified on the live page, it is flagged as such or placed under Gaps.

Profile being calibrated: 32-year-old UK man, Crohn's disease with limited daily energy; PrinciplesYou "Shaper" (very high Non-Conforming, Agile, Demanding, Independent, Growth-Seeking, Critical, Receptive to Criticism; very low Empathetic, Status-Seeking, Impartial, Modest, Detail-Oriented, Practical; moderate Energetic); strong face-to-face selling; weak at implementation/linear grind and at content/marketing; 0/6 finish rate on solo software builds; two prior profitable businesses.

Note on tool budget: the web-search quota was exhausted part-way through; later gaps were filled with direct fetches of primary PDFs and the Europe PMC / Semantic Scholar APIs. Some intended follow-ups (e.g. Demos IBD workplace survey, Gompers et al. 2010 serial-entrepreneur persistence, Greenberg & Mollick "Sole Survivors") could not be reached at primary source and are listed as gaps.

## Key Question 1: Which personality traits predict venture creation, survival and profit, and how large are the effects? (Big Five, need for autonomy, risk propensity, self-efficacy, proactive personality, innovativeness)

### Takeaway
Personality effects on business outcomes are real but small-to-moderate: single-trait corrected correlations with business success cluster around r = .10–.30, and the full Big Five set explains roughly 10% of variance in performance (multiple R ≈ .31). Openness/innovativeness, achievement motivation, self-efficacy, proactivity and need for autonomy are the consistent positive predictors; low agreeableness is associated with *becoming* an entrepreneur but is unrelated to *performance*; the "dependability" facet of conscientiousness (the closest proxy for detail orientation) does not distinguish entrepreneurs from managers at all (d = .01), whereas the achievement facet does strongly (d = .59).

### Cited Findings

**Zhao & Seibert (2006), Journal of Applied Psychology 91(2): 259–271 — entrepreneurs vs managers (entrepreneurial status)**
- Meta-analysis of 23 studies (1970–2002); entrepreneurs scored higher on Conscientiousness and Openness, lower on Neuroticism and Agreeableness; no difference on Extraversion. Individual effect sizes small; multivariate R = 0.37 for the full set — [ERIC abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ936043); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.91.2.259); study count from [Kerr, Kerr & Xu 2018 HBS review PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)
- Reliability-corrected effect sizes (d) reported by Brandstätter's (2011) review of the same meta-analysis: Neuroticism −.37, Extraversion .22 (not significant), Openness .36, Agreeableness −.16, Conscientiousness .45. Within Conscientiousness, the achievement-motivation facet d = .59 and the dependability facet d = .01 — [Brandstätter 2011, Personality and Individual Differences 51: 222–230](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007) (full text: [PDF](https://members.bestbusinesscoach.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Personality-aspects-of-entrepreneurship-A-look-at-five-meta-analyses.pdf))
- Kerr, Kerr & Xu (2018) summarise the profile as O+, C+, E (no difference), A−, N− and note that the A− and N− differences "are quite small" — [HBS review PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)

**Zhao, Seibert & Lumpkin (2010), Journal of Management 36(2): 381–404 — intentions and performance**
- Multivariate effect of the Big Five: multiple R = .36 on entrepreneurial intentions and R = .31 on entrepreneurial performance (firm survival, growth, profitability). Conscientiousness, Openness, Emotional Stability and Extraversion positively related to performance; Agreeableness not related to either intentions or performance. Risk propensity positively related to intentions but *not* to performance — [SAGE abstract / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309335187)
- Brandstätter (2011) on the same paper: "Risk propensity is the only personality trait where the correlations with intentions and performance were significantly different. It is a good predictor of intentions, but irrelevant for performance." Performance was operationalised as profitability (sales revenue, profit, liquidity, ROI, ROA), operational effectiveness (firm size, productivity, firm survival, subjective ratings) and firm growth — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)

**Rauch & Frese (2007), European Journal of Work and Organizational Psychology 16(4): 353–385 — specific traits and business success**
- 116 independent samples from 104 articles. Traits judged by experts as "matched" to entrepreneurship correlated with business success at corrected r = .250 (K = 42, N = 5,607) versus r = .028 (K = 13, N = 2,777) for unmatched traits — [Taylor & Francis abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/13594320701595438)
- Average N-weighted corrected r across all personality scales: .19 for business creation, .20 for business success — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Trait-level corrected correlations (business creation, business success): need for achievement (.22, .30); innovativeness (.24, .27); proactive personality (—, .27); generalised self-efficacy (.38, .25); stress tolerance (.10, .20); need for autonomy (.31, .16); locus of control (.19, .13); risk-taking (.10, .10) — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Brandstätter notes success in Rauch & Frese was "measured partly by key informant ratings of success and satisfaction, partly by financial outcomes and firm growth. The effect sizes were commonly higher with ratings, possibly as a result of common method" — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)

**Other meta-analyses summarised by Brandstätter (2011)**
- Stewart & Roth (2001): 12 studies, ~3,000 participants; entrepreneurs vs managers on risk propensity, corrected d = .36 — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Stewart & Roth (2007): achievement motivation, entrepreneurs vs managers d = .35 overall (US studies d = .20; non-US d = .54) — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Brandstätter's overall conclusion: narrow traits (innovativeness, self-efficacy, proactivity) predict better than broad traits; "the effect sizes are mostly small" — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886910003454)

**Longitudinal single studies (from the HBS review)**
- Kessler et al. (2012): 227 Austrian founders interviewed three times 1998–2005; need for achievement, locus of control and risk-taking predicted *early* success (first sales revenues) but *not* longer-term business survival — [Kerr, Kerr & Xu 2018](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)
- Caliendo et al. (2014), German SOEP 2000–2009: Openness, Extraversion and risk tolerance predict entry into self-employment, whereas "entirely different ones, such as agreeableness or other levels of risk tolerance, govern exit choices" — [Kerr, Kerr & Xu 2018](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)

**Human capital (experience, knowledge, skills)**
- Unger, Rauch, Frese & Rosenbusch (2011), Journal of Business Venturing: 70 samples, N = 24,733; human capital–success correlation small, rc = .098; larger for *outcomes* of human capital (knowledge/skills) than for investments (education/years of experience), for task-related human capital, and for young businesses — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusvent.2009.09.004)

### Inferences
- Mapping the profile: Non-Conforming/Agile/Growth-Seeking map most naturally onto Openness and innovativeness (positive for both creation and performance, r ≈ .24–.36); Independent maps onto need for autonomy (creation r = .31, but success r only .16); Demanding/Growth-Seeking resemble achievement motivation (success r = .30; entrepreneur-vs-manager d = .59) — the strongest single trait signals in the literature.
- Very low Empathetic/Impartial/Modest and high Critical/Demanding correspond to low Agreeableness. Evidence says this is mildly associated with *being* an entrepreneur (d = −.16) but has no association with performance in Zhao et al. 2010. It is not a predictor of profit either way; the one caution is the Caliendo et al. finding that agreeableness relates to *exit* decisions (direction not extracted here).
- Low Detail-Oriented/Practical correspond to the dependability facet of Conscientiousness. The meta-analytic evidence (d = .01) suggests entrepreneurs are *not* more dependable/orderly than managers; the Conscientiousness effect is carried almost entirely by achievement striving. So low detail orientation is not, on this evidence, a disqualifier — but Zhao et al. 2010 still find overall Conscientiousness positively related to performance, so the deficit is a cost to be designed around (hire for it), not ignored.
- Risk propensity is irrelevant to performance, and personality predicts *early* traction better than long-run survival (Kessler et al.). Survival depends on execution, capital and market factors that personality does not capture.
- Because task-related *skills* (Unger et al.) outperform generic experience, this person's demonstrated selling/negotiation skill counts for more than years in any given industry — but the overall human-capital effect is small (rc ≈ .10).

### Gaps
- Trait-level corrected correlations with performance from Zhao et al. 2010 (e.g. exact rho for Openness or Conscientiousness on survival/profit) could not be retrieved: the SAGE and ResearchGate pages returned 403; only the multivariate R (.31) and directions are sourced.
- No meta-analysis was found that separates "big-picture/creative" from "detail-oriented" Conscientiousness facets *as predictors of profit* (Zhao & Seibert's facet split concerns status only).
- No study directly measures the PrinciplesYou instrument against business outcomes; all mapping to Big Five is inferential.

## Key Question 2: Do sales/persuasion ability and prior industry or sales experience predict small-business success and acquisition outcomes?

### Takeaway
There is no meta-analysis isolating sales ability as a predictor of survival or profit. Indirect evidence points the same way: Extraversion is positively related to performance (Zhao et al. 2010), task-related skills matter more than generic experience (Unger et al. 2011), and in search funds "sales" is a minority background (6% in 2022–23) with no published outcome breakdown by background.

### Cited Findings
- Extraversion is positively related to entrepreneurial performance (survival, growth, profitability) in the 2010 meta-analysis, though it does not distinguish entrepreneurs from managers in the 2006 one — [Zhao, Seibert & Lumpkin 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309335187); [Zhao & Seibert 2006](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ936043)
- Kerr, Kerr & Xu note extraversion "could be more important for entrepreneurs than managers since entrepreneurs act as salespeople for their ideas to investors, partners, employees, and customers" — [HBS review PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)
- Human capital–success rc = .098 (70 samples, N = 24,733); effect higher for knowledge/skills than for education/experience and for task-related human capital — [Unger et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusvent.2009.09.004)
- Kauffman Firm Survey (2004–2008 cohort): "Previous industry experience and startup experience had less impact on firm survival prospects than owner age did" — [Kauffman overview](https://www.kauffman.org/entrepreneurship/reports/kauffman-firm-survey-series/an-overview-of-the-kauffman-firm-survey-20042008/); by contrast Shah & Winston Smith (Kauffman Firm Survey data) find prior industry experience "confers direct survival benefits on young firms" — [Wharton Mack Institute PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/ShahWinston-Smith_Intellectual-Property-Prior-Knowledge-the-Survival-of-New-Firms.pdf)
- Personality traits (need for achievement, locus of control, risk-taking) predict *first sales revenues* but not survival in 227 Austrian founders — Kessler et al. 2012 via [HBS review](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)
- Search fund principals' backgrounds 2022–23: investment banking/finance 23%, management consulting 16%, line/general management 14%, private equity 12%, military 10%, entrepreneur 7%, sales 6%, operations 4%, marketing 1% — [Stanford GSB 2024 Search Fund Study, Exhibit 2](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Self-funded (non-traditional) searchers "lean toward operations (43%), sales and marketing (27%), and prior entrepreneurship (27%)" — [Startup Resources self-funded search guide](https://startupresources.io/self-funded-search-fund-investor-guide/) (secondary, industry source)
- Lazear's "balanced skills" (jack-of-all-trades) theory of entrepreneurship, American Economic Review 2004 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828041301425) (theory reference; no effect size extracted)

### Inferences
- Selling skill is a task-related skill in Unger et al.'s terms and therefore sits in the higher-yielding part of the (small) human-capital effect, but nothing in the evidence base supports treating it as a large or decisive predictor on its own.
- The absence of sales backgrounds among traditional searchers (6%) is a description of who searches, not evidence that sales backgrounds do worse; Stanford publishes no outcome split by background.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis or large cohort study found that measures sales/persuasion ability directly against survival or profit.
- Gompers, Kovner, Lerner & Scharfstein (2010) "Performance persistence in entrepreneurship" (prior successful founders' next-venture success rates) could not be fetched (Semantic Scholar returned no abstract; search budget exhausted) — its figures are not reported here.
- No published data linking searcher background (sales vs consulting vs operations) to acquisition returns.

## Key Question 3: Solo founders vs co-founders vs founder-plus-early-hire — completion, survival and exit

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed and heavily confounded by venture type: VC-portfolio data favour teams (First Round: multi-founder companies +163% revenue; solo seed valuations 25% lower), Crunchbase exit data slightly favour solo founders (52.3% of exited startups had one founder), and the only study that explicitly examines *when* solo founders do as well finds they substitute for co-founders through early hires, alliances and "benefactors". In search funds, partnered searches earn a higher IRR (40.5% vs 30.3%) but most recent 10x+ outcomes were solo.

### Cited Findings
- Howell & Bingham (UNC Kenan-Flagler; Wharton Mack Institute working paper): qualitative fsQCA study of 59 ventures (33 solo-founded) from an incubator, a co-working space and a grant foundation; finding that "solo founders can mobilize many of the same resources provided by co-founders, but through different means" — three configurations: early employees hired from the outset, alliances with other organisations, and "benefactors" who relax a resource constraint. Also cites Wasserman (2012) that "up to 65% of startup failures may be tied to conflicts among the co-founders" and that "most VCs are reluctant to fund a company with only one founder" — [PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Howell-Travis-Bingham-Chris_Solo-vs.-Co.-Under-What-Conditions-Can-Solo-Founded-Ventures-Perform-as-well-as-Co-Founded-Ventures.pdf)
- Wasserman (Founder's Dilemmas, data on >10,000 founders): only 16% of US tech start-ups are solo-founded; teams of two or three co-founders outperform solo founders and larger teams — [Business of Software talk summary](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/understanding-founders-dilemmas/); [Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas)
- First Round Capital 10-year portfolio review: companies with more than one founder outperformed solo-founder companies by 163% in revenue; solo founders' seed valuations 25% lower; authors acknowledge VC-portfolio bias — as reported by [Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/the-case-for-solo-founders/)
- Crunchbase analysis (Haje Kamps, TechCrunch 2016): 52.3% of successfully exited start-ups had a single founder; among start-ups raising $10M+, 45.9% were solo-founded; average 1.72 founders per start-up — as reported by [Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/the-case-for-solo-founders/) and [Theanna](https://theanna.io/building-theanna/do-you-need-a-cofounder-to-succeed)
- Greenberg & Mollick, "Sole Survivors: Solo Ventures versus Founding Teams" (Kickstarter data): "Ventures started by solo entrepreneurs generally outperform teams of co-founders, particularly two-person teams" — as reported by [Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/the-case-for-solo-founders/)
- Equidam's own 2025 data: solo founders are 20% of VC-backed start-ups but 42% of start-ups with $1M+ revenue (two-founder teams 37% and 33% respectively) — [Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/the-case-for-solo-founders/)
- CB Insights (2026 update, 431 VC-backed post-mortems since 2023, 385 with identifiable reasons): ran out of capital 70%; poor product–market fit 43%; bad timing/macro 29%; unsustainable unit economics 19%. "Not the right team" is not listed in this version — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/)
- Search funds: partnered searches IRR 40.5% vs solo 30.3% (n = 296: 178 solo, 118 partnerships); "five of the six searchers that achieved a 10x+ ROI in the last two years were solo searchers"; partnerships were 19% of searches launched in the last two years (41% in the prior two) — [Stanford GSB 2024 Search Fund Study](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- The team-vs-solo literature is dominated by VC-backed tech; almost none of it concerns owner-operated services or acquired SMEs. The Howell & Bingham finding that solo founders succeed by *hiring early* or attaching to an established partner is the most directly applicable result for someone who scores low on Implementer and has a 0/6 solo completion record.
- The 65% co-founder-conflict figure and the solo-founder exit share argue against assuming a co-founder is necessary; the First Round and Wasserman figures argue for having someone else in the operating seat. Both are compatible with "founder + early operations hire".

### Gaps
- Kauffman's "two co-founders reach scale 3.6x quicker" figure appeared only in a secondary blog ([Seedblink](https://seedblink.com/blog/2024-06-03-the-founder-factor-on-startup-success-solo-vs-co-founders)) and could not be traced to a Kauffman publication.
- The earlier CB Insights percentage for "not the right team" (widely quoted as ~23%) was not on the current page and is not reported here.
- No survival data specifically for "founder with an early operations manager" versus co-founder pairs in non-tech small business.

## Key Question 4: Chronic illness (Crohn's/IBD) and work — employment, absence, fatigue, and the effect of autonomy/flexibility

### Takeaway
Pooled data show IBD costs roughly 16% absenteeism, 36% presenteeism and 39% overall work impairment, with only about two-thirds of patients employed and one in three reporting job loss due to IBD; impairment scales steeply with disease activity (Crohn's overall work impairment 12% in remission vs 51% moderate-to-severe). Fatigue is the strongest driver of productivity loss (47% of Crohn's patients report high fatigue; adjusted OR ≈ 4 for presenteeism). Flexible hours and home working are the accommodations most wanted and least provided, and the only causal-style review (Cochrane 2010) finds worker-controlled flexibility improves health while employer-imposed flexibility does not.

### Cited Findings

**Pooled estimates**
- Systematic review and meta-analysis (Journal of Crohn's and Colitis, 2024; literature to Feb 2023): among IBD patients, absenteeism 16.4%, presenteeism 35.9%, overall work impairment 39.4%, non-work activity impairment 46.0%; indirect cost €5,131 per patient per year; "only two-thirds of IBD patients were employed, and one in three lost their jobs due to IBD"; among the employed, 39.5% report sick days, 21.3% work disability, 12.3% disability pensions; most studies show clinically meaningful productivity improvement with medical/surgical therapy — [DOI 10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjae057](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjae057)

**Disease activity and fatigue**
- US Crohn's cohort (n = 281 working patients, data 2014–2018): overall work impairment 12.4% in remission, ~33% mild, 51.0% moderate-to-severe; absenteeism 0.95% vs 14.6%; presenteeism 11.7% vs 44.9%; annual indirect cost $7,169 (remission) vs $29,524 (moderate-to-severe); no non-IBD control group — [Crohn's & Colitis 360, 2022](https://academic.oup.com/crohnscolitis360/article/4/3/otac023/6608899)
- CorEvitas IBD Registry 2017–2022 (640 Crohn's, 569 UC): 47% of Crohn's patients reported high fatigue (PROMIS ≥ 55); 88–89% of high-fatigue patients persisted or worsened at 6 months. In Crohn's, high vs low fatigue: absenteeism aOR 1.94 (p = .046), presenteeism aOR 4.31 (p < .001), work productivity loss aOR 4.20 (p < .001), activity impairment aOR 3.30 (p < .001). Employment at enrolment did not differ by fatigue (aOR 0.93, p = .76) — [PMC11684069](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11684069/)
- Dutch multicentre cohort (510 employed IBD patients, 18-month follow-up): fatigue and reduced HRQoL were the strongest determinants of work productivity loss — [Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol, DOI 10.1097/MEG.0000000000002178](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/eurojgh/abstract/10.1097/meg.0000000000002178~work-productivity-loss-is-determined-by-fatigue-and-reduced?redirectionsource=fulltextview)
- Dutch outpatient study (n = 202, mean age 41, 63% Crohn's, 19% active disease): 61% in paid employment, of whom 25% on sick leave; 23% receiving a disability pension; most prevalent work difficulties were low working pace (78%), concentration problems (72%) and delayed work production (50%) — [De Boer et al. 2016, DOI 10.1097/MEG.0000000000000685](https://doi.org/10.1097/MEG.0000000000000685)

**Accommodations and flexibility**
- Systematic review of workplace accommodations in IBD (6 studies, 7,700 participants): toilet access needed by 83%, provided for 71%; time for medical appointments needed 88%, available 69%; flexible hours needed 65%, provided 41%; remote work wanted 41%, available 21%; part-time/reduced days needed 35%, available 17%; only 34–40% of participants managed to make adjustments that avoided time off — [PMC6390415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6390415/)
- Crohn's & Colitis UK submission to BEIS "Making flexible working the default" (Nov 2021): around 50% of people with Crohn's or Colitis experience at least one flare per year; 78% of young people with IBD with work experience worried about managing symptoms at work (Bajorek et al. 2016); of those yet to enter the labour market, 82% worried about managing flares, 65% about employer inflexibility, 57% about finding a job that fitted the condition (Gay et al., CCUK 2011); patients reported that flexing hours, reducing travel and working from home "improved their ability [to] manage and cope better with symptoms such as frequent bowel movements/incontinence, pain and/or fatigue" (Digestive Diseases and Sciences, 2020); the 2010 Cochrane review is quoted: "flexible working interventions that increase worker control and choice (such as self-scheduling or gradual/partial retirement) are likely to have a positive effect on health outcomes", whereas employer-dictated flexibility (fixed-term contracts, involuntary part-time) had "equivocal or negative health effects"; nearly 8,000 people responded to CCUK's lockdown survey, over half said they had more time to rest — [CCUK PDF](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/media/4ermv5lj/crohns-colitis-uk-making-flexible-working-the-default-nov-2021.pdf)
- CCUK employer guide (2024 finding): "the biggest impact of living with Crohn's or Colitis was managing fatigue"; recommends later starts ("many people find their bowels are more active in the morning"), shorter hours or home working during flares, and phased return starting "with shorter hours and slowly increase" — [CCUK employer guide](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/info-support/information-about-crohns-and-colitis/all-information-about-crohns-and-colitis/employment-and-education/employment-a-guide-for-employers)
- CCUK 2018 Quality of Life survey: 44% of people with Crohn's or Colitis in the UK agree their condition has affected their careers; separate 1,000-adult poll: only 15% of the public thought Crohn's/Colitis an acceptable reason to call in sick — [CCUK news, 26 June 2018](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/news-stories/news-items/the-great-british-cover-up-workers-fear-stigma-over-invisible-health-conditions)
- CCUK employee guide: "eight out of 10 employees had told their employer or HR department about their IBD" — [CCUK employee guide PDF](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/media/1q3pz5io/crohns-colitis-uk-employee-guide.pdf)
- Unverified (search-engine excerpt attributed to CCUK material; not found on the live employer-guide page when scraped): a Demos survey found 57% of people with IBD had at some point reduced their working hours because of IBD and 68% thought IBD had negatively affected their career — [CCUK employer guide (attributed)](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/info-support/information-about-crohns-and-colitis/all-information-about-crohns-and-colitis/employment-and-education/employment-a-guide-for-employers)

### Inferences
- For the profile in question, the operative constraint is fatigue rather than employment status per se: fatigue does not reduce the chance of *being* employed (aOR 0.93) but roughly quadruples the odds of presenteeism and productivity loss. Work designed around low-fatigue windows (later starts, shorter blocks) addresses the actual mechanism.
- The Cochrane distinction (worker-controlled vs employer-imposed flexibility) is the clearest evidence that *ownership of the schedule*, not flexibility as such, is what improves health outcomes — an argument for owner/operator structures where the person controls timing.
- With ~50% of patients flaring at least once a year and impairment at 33–51% during mild-to-severe activity, any plan should assume several weeks per year at sharply reduced capacity and build in a person who can run operations during those periods.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies *sustainable weekly hours* for people with active IBD; the literature measures impairment percentages, not hours worked.
- NICE/NHS employment guidance for IBD was not retrieved (no search budget remaining); the CCUK parliamentary written evidence (DYE0019) returned 403 on both HTML and PDF.
- No IBD-specific trial of remote work or "burst" scheduling on symptoms was found; the flexibility evidence is cross-sectional or from the general-population Cochrane review as quoted by CCUK.

## Key Question 5: Does self-employment/ownership with autonomy improve or worsen health for people with chronic conditions, compared with employment?

### Takeaway
Autonomy is the most consistent protective factor in the entrepreneur well-being literature (positive in 18 of 20 studies), the only quasi-causal study finds opportunity-driven moves into self-employment improve both physical and mental health, and the one longitudinal comparison after chronic-disease diagnosis finds working conditions improve only for the self-employed. Against this, self-employed people with chronic conditions report substantial business-maintenance losses and well-being that falls with absences and slowed growth.

### Cited Findings
- Nikolova (2019), Journal of Business Venturing: German SOEP 2002–2014, difference-in-differences; opportunity entrepreneurship (regular employment → self-employment) "leads to both physical and mental health gains"; necessity entrepreneurship (unemployment → self-employment) improves mental but not physical health; gains "cannot be explained by changes in income or working conditions and are not driven by personality and risk preferences" — [DOI 10.1016/j.jbusvent.2018.09.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusvent.2018.09.001)
- Stephan & Roesler (2010), J Occup Organ Psychol 83(3): 717–738, German national representative sample: entrepreneurs showed "significantly lower overall somatic and mental morbidity, lower blood pressure, lower prevalence rates of hypertension, and somatoform disorders, as well as higher well-being and more favourable behavioural health indicators" (sick days, physician visits); interpreted via the "active job" hypothesis (high demands + high control) — [Aston open-access PDF](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/21179/1/Health_of_entrepreneurs_vs_employees_in_a_national_representative_sample.pdf); [Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1348/096317909X472067). Cross-sectional; selection into entrepreneurship not controlled.
- Stephan (2018), Academy of Management Perspectives 32(3): 290–322, systematic review of 144 empirical studies; presentation slides summarise: work resource "high autonomy" associated positively with mental well-being in 18 of 20 studies; long working hours associated negatively in 8 of 13 studies; stressors include uncertainty, financial problems, job insecurity, role overload — [AMP DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/amp.2017.0001); [Stephan 2018 slides PDF](https://imes.vse.cz/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/IMES18_KNS_Stephan-Ute_Entrepreneurshipo-well-being-and-mental-health.pdf)
- English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (1,389 participants, 4,824 observations, ~12 years, fixed-effects): after chronic-disease diagnosis, employees' job autonomy fell (−0.10, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.00) and physical demands rose (+0.13, 0.01 to 0.25); for the self-employed, autonomy did not change, physical demands fell (−0.36, −0.64 to −0.07) and hours fell non-significantly (−2.78 h/week, −6.03 to 0.48). "Improvements in working conditions after diagnosis of chronic disease were restricted to the self-employed." Limitations: self-reported diagnosis; cannot separate conditions — [PMC6161656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6161656/)
- Whitehall II (10,308 civil servants aged 35–55): low job control at both measurement occasions, OR 1.93 (95% CI 1.34–2.77) for any subsequent coronary event vs high control at both; not explained by grade, negative affectivity or classic risk factors; job demands and social support not related — [Bosma et al., BMJ 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9055714/)
- Yagil & Cohen (2024), Stress and Health: 294 self-employed workers with respiratory disease, heart conditions, IBD or cancer survivorship; all four conditions "had substantial negative effects on business maintenance" (declining income and activity, slowed growth); work absences and slowing growth were negatively associated with well-being; "the inability to maintain a business can negatively affect well-being" — [DOI 10.1002/smi.3445](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3445)
- Yagil & Cohen (2025), J Health Psychol, 23 interviews (incl. IBD): four themes — uncertainty in planning work and committing to customers; acceptance vs denial of reduced work ability; disclosure to workers and customers; "temporal substitutes as a source of both support and concern"; "a sense of sole responsibility" — [DOI 10.1177/13591053241239462](https://doi.org/10.1177/13591053241239462)
- French SME entrepreneurs (n = 273): entrepreneurial burnout correlated with emotional demands r = .56 and autonomy r = −.36; regression β autonomy = −.122 (p ≤ .01); autonomy buffered the emotional-demands → burnout link (interaction β = −.125, p ≤ .05); job satisfaction did not buffer — [PMC9589686](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9589686/)
- Contrary evidence: a systematic review comparing chronic physical disorders in self-employed vs employees exists ([PubMed 36710710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36710710/)) and a US pandemic-era analysis found self-employment associated with worse health via delayed care and financial difficulty ([PMC11691287](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11691287/)) — abstracts not fully retrieved; noted for balance.

### Inferences
- The health case for ownership rests on control, not on being one's own boss per se: the ELSA and Cochrane findings both show benefits only where the worker controls scheduling and demands. A business that puts the owner in a fixed-hours operating role (e.g. retail counter, on-site services) would forfeit the mechanism.
- Yagil & Cohen's finding that business decline and absences erode well-being is the counterweight: sole-responsibility structures convert flares into financial stress. A structure with a "temporal substitute" (an operations manager or partner) is exactly what their interviewees describe as both the support and the worry.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found comparing health trajectories of people with IBD specifically in self-employment vs employment.
- Nikolova's effect sizes (magnitude of health gains) were not extracted from the abstract.

## Key Question 6: Owner working hours by business type, and burnout

### Takeaway
Hours vary enormously by owner type: a 2024 US probability survey puts the *median* small-business owner at 30 h/week (35% under 20 h; 19% at 50+), while UK self-employed averaged 40 h vs 38 h for employees (ONS 2014). Long hours are associated with worse entrepreneur well-being in most studies, and autonomy is the main buffer against burnout. No robust dataset was found that breaks owner hours down by services vs distribution vs online.

### Cited Findings
- NerdWallet/Harris Poll, October 2024 (2,000+ US adults, 425 current small-business owners): typical owner works 30 h/week; 35% work under 20 h; 19% work 50+ h; 52% sell goods, 50% sell services, 25% earn via online sponsorship (multiple selection) — [NerdWallet 2024 Small Business Report](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/learn/2024-small-business-report)
- ONS (2014): self-employed averaged 40 h/week vs 38 h for employees — [ONS Self-employed workers in the UK 2014 (web archive)](https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukgwa/20160105210312tf_/http:/www.ons.gov.uk/ons/rel/lmac/self-employed-workers-in-the-uk/2014/rep-self-employed-workers-in-the-uk-2014.html)
- ONS Labour Force Survey via Statista: in Q2 2024 about 40.9% of UK self-employed worked 31–45 h/week and 21.2% worked more than 45 h (figures from search excerpt; the live page is paywalled) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/382420/uk-self-employed-weekly-hours-of-work-by-hours/); underlying ONS series e.g. [LFS usual weekly hours, self-employed, 31–45 h](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/timeseries/lzjh/lms)
- Alternative Board survey: over 30% of small-business owners work over 50 h/week and 25% more than 60 h — [The Alternative Board](https://www.thealternativeboard.com/blog/new-survey-shows-work-life-balance-is-possible-but-not-likely-for-entrepreneurs) (industry survey; sample not verified)
- Stephan & Roesler (2010) note that "the number of weekly working hours is higher for entrepreneurs compared with employees (Paoli & Merllié, 2001)" — [Aston PDF](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/21179/1/Health_of_entrepreneurs_vs_employees_in_a_national_representative_sample.pdf)
- Stephan (2018) review: long working hours negatively associated with well-being in 8 of 13 studies; autonomy positive in 18 of 20 — [slides PDF](https://imes.vse.cz/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/IMES18_KNS_Stephan-Ute_Entrepreneurshipo-well-being-and-mental-health.pdf)
- Burnout mechanism: emotional demands β = .455 on burnout, autonomy β = −.122, autonomy × demands β = −.125 (n = 273 French SME owners) — [PMC9589686](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9589686/)
- Widely quoted founder-burnout figures (e.g. "72% report burnout symptoms", "70% cite 60+ h weeks") come from vendor blogs ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/founder-burnout-statistics-2026)) with no primary methodology visible; not relied on here.

### Inferences
- The 30-h median in a probability sample shows that low-hour ownership is common, not exceptional — but that sample includes side businesses. The 40 h UK self-employed mean and the 21% above 45 h are the more relevant reference for a full-time owner.
- The burnout evidence identifies emotional demands (customer/employee conflict) as the main driver and autonomy as the buffer; for a very-low-Empathetic profile, businesses heavy in emotional labour would be doubly costly.

### Gaps
- No credible dataset found giving owner hours by business model (owner-operator services vs distribution vs e-commerce). The NerdWallet report does not cross-tabulate hours by type; the ONS HOUR03 dataset gives hours by *industry* for all workers, not owners.
- No study of "burst" or compressed work patterns for owners with chronic illness.

## Key Question 7: Acquisition entrepreneurs / search funds — outcomes and searcher profiles; do sales-oriented owners with a hired operations manager do better?

### Takeaway
Search-fund data give hard base rates for "buy": 63% of concluded searches acquire a company (57% over the last decade); 31% of acquisitions lose money (about a third of those a total loss); aggregate IRR 35.1%, ROI 4.5x, with 11% of companies returning >10x. Partnered searches out-return solo searches on IRR (40.5% vs 30.3%). Searchers are overwhelmingly finance/consulting-background MBAs; no published analysis links searcher personality or a hired operations manager to outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford GSB 2024 Search Fund Study (681 US/Canadian search funds since 1984; 94 launched in 2023): of concluded searches, 63% made acquisitions (37% none); acquisition rate "around 57%" since 2014. Of acquisitions: 69% gain, 31% loss; of losses, 66% partial and 34% total. ROI distribution among gains: 1–2x 27%, 2–5x 36%, 5–10x 25%, >10x 11%. Aggregate IRR 35.1%, ROI 4.5x (Dec 2023); exited-company IRR rose to 42.9% — [Stanford 2024 study PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf); [Stanford GSB search funds page](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/experience/about/centers-institutes/ghces/research/search-funds)
- Deal profile (medians): purchase price $14.4m (down from $16.5m), EBITDA $2.2m, 27% margin, 25% growth, 34 employees; median search length 19 months; first LOI signed on average 7.8 months in, 3.6 LOIs signed per search; median first-year CEO salary $190k; median equity earned per entrepreneur still operating $1.98m (mean $6.09m) — same PDF
- Searcher profile: 79% aged 35 or under; 24% without an MBA; 18% female; 2022–23 backgrounds: banking/finance 23%, consulting 16%, line/general management 14%, PE 12%, military 10%, entrepreneur 7%, sales 6%, operations 4% — same PDF and [Searchfunder summary](https://searchfunder.com/post/stanford-2024-search-fund-study-highlights)
- Partnership: partnered searches IRR 40.5% vs solo 30.3% (n = 296; 178 solo, 118 partnered); five of six recent 10x+ outcomes were solo; partnerships fell to 19% of recent launches — same PDF
- IESE International Search Fund Study 2024: 79% of concluded international search funds acquired a company (vs 63% US/Canada); of 146 acquisitions, 125 still operating, 15 exited with positive return, 6 exited at a loss; aggregate ROI 2.0x, IRR 18.1%, top performer 31.4x; partnerships 40% of all international funds; new principals' backgrounds: consulting 26%, banking/finance 20%, line/general management 14% — [IESE ST-0658-E PDF](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)
- Industry commentary claims "operational agility" rather than prestige background predicts search-fund success — [Long Angle](https://www.longangle.com/alts-education/search-funds) (opinion; no data)

### Inferences
- Base rates for an acquisition path are far better documented than for solo builds: roughly 6 in 10 searchers buy, roughly 7 in 10 of those make money, and the median searcher earns about $2m of equity — but with heavy selection (elite-MBA, investor-vetted) and a 19-month full-time search that itself demands linear diligence work.
- The partnered-vs-solo IRR gap (10 points) is the closest search-fund evidence on "don't run it alone"; it is not adjusted for selection and the report itself notes that recent top outcomes were solo.
- There is no evidence either way on "sales-oriented CEO plus hired operations manager". The searcher population contains too few sales backgrounds (6%) for the data to speak to it.

### Gaps
- No study of searcher *personality* (Big Five or otherwise) and outcomes was found; Stanford and IESE report demographics and professional background only.
- No outcome data by searcher background, and no data on post-acquisition management structure (owner-CEO with ops manager vs hands-on owner).
- Self-funded/"buy then build" (Deibel) case data and Harvard ETA research were not reachable after the search quota was exhausted; the self-funded background split (operations 43%, sales/marketing 27%) is from an industry guide, not an academic source.
- UK-specific ETA data absent.

## Key Question 8: Decision-making patterns — indecision, over-deliberation, pre-commitment devices and implementation intentions

### Takeaway
Implementation intentions ("if situation Y, then I do X") have a robust medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = .65 across 94 tests), with the largest effects on *getting started* (d = .61) and *shielding a course of action from derailment* (d = .77) — precisely the two failure modes of a serial re-opener. Hard commitment devices show real but small effects with low take-up (11–28%), and reviews find they work best when public.

### Cited Findings
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38: 94 independent tests; implementation intentions had "a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d = .65) on goal attainment"; effective in promoting initiation, shielding ongoing pursuit, disengagement from failing courses of action and conserving capability — [DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1). The NCI-hosted chapter text gives d = .61 for alleviating failures to get started and d = .77 for preventing derailment — [NCI PDF](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf). A secondary source reports the 95% CI as 0.6–0.7 and >8,000 participants — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)
- Bryan, Karlan & Nelson (2010), Annual Review of Economics, "Commitment Devices": CARES smoking-cessation savings contract (Philippines) taken up by 11% of smokers offered; those offered were 3 percentage points more likely to pass a 6-month cessation test, holding at 12 months. SEED commitment savings account: 28% take-up; those offered increased bank savings by ~80% vs control after one year. Save More Tomorrow: 78% of those offered enrolled; 80% still enrolled after four pay rises. Documented failure rates without commitment: only 12% of New Year's resolution makers succeed; 4–7% of smoking quit attempts succeed — [Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010 PDF](http://houdekpetr.cz/!data/public_html/papers/Bryan%20et%20al%202010.pdf)
- Systematic review of commitment-making for weight loss: 3 trials, 409 participants, mean difference 1.5 kg short-term; 12-month effect 1.7 kg (95% CI 0.0–3.4) in 2 RCTs (302 patients); commitment devices "appeared most successful when made publicly" — [PMC6591991](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6591991/)
- Behavioural-economics interventions for physical activity (meta-analysis): small significant effect during the intervention that did not persist at follow-up — [Social Science & Medicine 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953623006986)
- Entrepreneurship theory: "hesitancy, indecisiveness, and procrastination are thought to lead to missed opportunities"; action-state orientation has decision-, failure- and performance-related dimensions that influence goal engagement in entrepreneurship — [Unpacking the uncertainty construct (effectuation.org PDF)](https://effectuation.org/hubfs/Journal%20Articles/2017/06/Unpacking-the-uncertainty-construct-Implications-for-entrepreneurial-action.pdf?hsLang=en); [Psychological determinants of entrepreneurial success, PMC5486516](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5486516/)

### Inferences
- The implementation-intention effect is among the largest and best-replicated in behavioural science and costs nothing; for a person who re-opens decisions, the "shielding" result (d = .77) is the most relevant.
- Hard financial commitment devices are effective for those who take them up, but take-up is low and the person here is low on Status-Seeking (public accountability may bite less) — the weight-loss review's finding that public commitments work best suggests external accountability to a specific person (investor, partner, ops manager) rather than a self-imposed penalty.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found linking *indecisiveness or decision re-opening* to entrepreneurial performance; the entrepreneurship literature treats it theoretically.
- No meta-analysis found on external accountability/coaching for business owners specifically.
- Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions meta-analysis ([PMC8149892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)) was identified but effect sizes not extracted.

## Synthesis against the three headline questions

### Which of this person's measured traits are associated with higher or lower small-business survival/profit, and how large are the effects?

### Takeaway
Positives (Openness/innovativeness, achievement striving, autonomy, proactivity, self-efficacy) have corrected correlations with success of roughly .16–.30 each; the low-Agreeableness cluster is neutral for performance; low detail orientation is a cost to design around but not, on the meta-analytic evidence, a predictor in its own right. All effects are correlational and small next to market, capital and execution factors.

### Cited Findings
- Success correlations: need for achievement .30, innovativeness .27, proactive personality .27, generalised self-efficacy .25, stress tolerance .20, need for autonomy .16, locus of control .13, risk-taking .10 — [Rauch & Frese 2007 via Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Agreeableness unrelated to intentions or performance; Big Five multiple R = .31 for performance — [Zhao et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309335187)
- Dependability facet d = .01 vs achievement facet d = .59 (entrepreneurs vs managers) — [Brandstätter 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.07.007)
- Personality predicts first sales but not long-run survival (227 founders) — [Kessler et al. 2012 via HBS review](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/KKX-Personality-Review_RIS_5ea5da25-c8ab-41d2-90ee-e30b3d5b071c.pdf)

### Inferences
- The profile is close to the meta-analytic "entrepreneur" pattern on everything except dependability/detail, where the literature says entrepreneurs are no different from managers — i.e. the gap is normal, not disqualifying, but it is the facet that Zhao et al. still find matters for performance via overall Conscientiousness.

### Gaps
- See Key Question 1 gaps (Zhao 2010 trait-level rhos not retrieved).

### Does the evidence support "buy with a manager in place" over "build solo" for someone with low Implementer/Detail scores and a chronic energy constraint?

### Takeaway
No study tests this comparison directly. The indirect evidence is consistent with it: solo founders succeed when they secure early hires/allies (Howell & Bingham); partnered searchers out-return solo searchers (40.5% vs 30.3% IRR); self-employed people with chronic conditions describe "temporal substitutes" as essential; and acquisition base rates (63% acquire, 69% of those gain) are well documented whereas the person's own solo-build base rate is 0/6.

### Cited Findings
- Solo founders mobilise co-founder-equivalent resources through early employees, alliances and benefactors (59 ventures) — [Howell & Bingham PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Howell-Travis-Bingham-Chris_Solo-vs.-Co.-Under-What-Conditions-Can-Solo-Founded-Ventures-Perform-as-well-as-Co-Founded-Ventures.pdf)
- Partnered IRR 40.5% vs solo 30.3%; 31% of acquisitions lose money — [Stanford 2024](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Self-employed with chronic conditions: "temporal substitutes as a source of both support and concern"; business decline erodes well-being — [Yagil & Cohen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/13591053241239462); [Yagil & Cohen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3445)
- Improvements in working conditions after chronic-disease diagnosis were restricted to the self-employed (ELSA) — [PMC6161656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6161656/)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports the *structure* (owner who controls schedule + someone else running operations) more than it supports "buy" over "build" as such. The search-fund data show that buying still requires a 19-month, diligence-heavy search — a linear-grind task — unless the search itself is outsourced or shortened.

### Gaps
- No data on acquired-SME survival under owner-CEO-plus-operations-manager structures; no UK small-acquisition outcome data.

### What weekly hours and work pattern do people with active IBD sustain, and what design features help?

### Takeaway
The literature does not report sustainable hours; it reports impairment: roughly 12% overall work impairment in Crohn's remission rising to ~33% in mild and ~51% in moderate-to-severe disease, with high fatigue (47% of Crohn's patients) quadrupling the odds of presenteeism. The design features with evidence are worker-controlled scheduling (Cochrane 2010 as quoted by CCUK), later starts and shorter blocks during flares, home working, and toilet/appointment access — the accommodations most wanted (flexible hours 65%, remote 41%) and least provided (41%, 21%).

### Cited Findings
- Impairment by activity, absenteeism/presenteeism figures — [Crohn's & Colitis 360, 2022](https://academic.oup.com/crohnscolitis360/article/4/3/otac023/6608899); pooled figures — [JCC 2024 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjae057); fatigue aORs — [PMC11684069](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11684069/)
- Accommodation need vs availability — [PMC6390415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6390415/); Cochrane quote and patient-reported benefit of flexing hours/home working — [CCUK 2021 PDF](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/media/4ermv5lj/crohns-colitis-uk-making-flexible-working-the-default-nov-2021.pdf); morning symptoms and later starts — [CCUK employer guide](https://www.crohnsandcolitis.org.uk/info-support/information-about-crohns-and-colitis/all-information-about-crohns-and-colitis/employment-and-education/employment-a-guide-for-employers)
- Self-employed hours after chronic-disease diagnosis fell by 2.78 h/week (not significant) — [PMC6161656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6161656/)

### Inferences
- Applying the impairment percentages to a 40-h UK self-employed norm gives a rough planning envelope of ~35 effective hours in remission, ~27 in mild activity and ~20 in moderate-to-severe activity, with ~50% of patients flaring at least once a year. This is an arithmetic inference from the cited percentages, not a measured figure.

### Gaps
- No direct measurement of sustainable weekly hours in active IBD; no trials of burst-work patterns.
