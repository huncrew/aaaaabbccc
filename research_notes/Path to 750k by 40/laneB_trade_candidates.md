Method (budget ~120 fetches/searches; actual: 143 raw page/API downloads incl. 38 Made-in-China searches, 43 TLC Direct pages, 24 Trade Tariff API calls, 3 Reed job pages, 2 Hydraquip store-API calls, 2 gov.uk pages, plus 3 WebFetch attempts and 5 web searches; ~25 of the downloads were access probes that returned blocks or 404s):

### 1e. Compliance facts pulled this session
- Waste Batteries and Accumulators Regulations 2009: anyone placing batteries (including in products) on the UK market is a producer; portable-battery producers above 1 tonne/yr must join a Battery Compliance Scheme; distributors/retailers supplying more than 32 kg of portable batteries a year must offer free take-back; industrial-battery producers must take back waste industrial batteries free of charge when supplying new ones — [gov.uk Regulations: batteries and waste batteries](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-batteries-and-waste-batteries).
- Dangerous goods by road: ADR applies; limited-quantity exemptions per column 7a of ADR Table A; a DGSA is required for international movements — [gov.uk Moving dangerous goods](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/moving-dangerous-goods). Lithium cells/packs are UN3480/UN3481 Class 9 with UN 38.3 test summaries (on file, `datacentre_grid_supply_gaps.md` KQ2); VRLA non-spillable batteries are UN2800 and exempt from ADR when SP238 is met (on file).
- Emergency lighting: secondary sources describe an annual full-duration (3-hour) test under BS 5266-1 and a post-battery-replacement retest after 24 h on charge (clause 13.2 cited on an IET forum); the "replace batteries every 4 years" interval is manufacturer guidance, not confirmed in the standard — [Oxford safety office](https://safety.web.ox.ac.uk/node/1212901), [LBBD annual testing PDF](https://www.lbbd.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Annual%20Emergency%20Lighting%20Testing.pdf), [IET EngX thread](https://engx.theiet.org/f/wiring-and-regulations/30927/emergency-lighting---do-you-carry-out-the-drop-test-after-replacing-a-light-or-replacing-batteries).
- Fire alarms: a widely circulated BS 5839-1 guide lists sealed lead-acid standby batteries for replacement every 4 years (sooner above 25 °C); 24 h standby + 30 min alarm sizing — [pdfcoffee guide](https://pdfcoffee.com/a-guide-to-bs5839pdf-pdf-free.html), [ADI fire-panel batteries](https://adiglobaldistribution.co.uk/articles-and-resources/fire-control-panel-batteries).
- On file: CE marking recognised indefinitely in GB (SI 2024/696); PPE Regulation and BSIF scrutiny (82% of non-RSSS PPE failed); CPR for construction products; LVD/EMC/RoHS for electrical goods (`import_line_candidates.md` KQ5, `import_logistics_playbook.md` §3).

## 2. Scoring method (0–100)

| Component | Weight | How computed | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | 25 | 25 × (0.35 × growth + 0.25 × buyer population + 0.25 × consumption velocity + 0.15 × job-ad proxy). Growth = HMRC Jan–Jul 2026 y/y of the parent line mapped −20%→0, 0→0.5, +20%→1. Buyer population from Companies House SIC counts (electrical 61k = 1.0; HVAC 54k = 0.9; plant/machinery/machining 15k = 0.5; fire/generator specialists = 0.3). Job-ad proxy = Reed count ÷ 218 (electrical 0.55, generator 0.84, HVAC 1.0; none 0.3). Velocity = 0–1 judgement of how often a buyer re-buys and how searchable the SKU is (**eBay sold counts were blocked**, so this component is explicitly a judgement; the value used is in the CSV column `velocity`). | growth/buyers/jobs sourced; velocity judgement |
| Competition | 15 | 15 × (1 − (c−1)/4) where c = 1 (specialist, few online sellers) to 5 (TLC/Toolstation/Amazon all carry it at a commodity price). Amazon seller counts and eBay active-listing counts were blocked; c is a judgement informed by whether a branded item is already at or under £3 at TLC, and by the on-file importers-per-£10m density. | judgement, flagged |
| Landed gross margin | 20 | GM at the line's primary channel (trade: (0.9 × TLC benchmark − landed) ÷ sell; marketplace: (benchmark × 0.85 − landed) ÷ benchmark). <15% → 0; 15–30% → 0–8; 30–60% → 8–20; ≥60% → 20. Lines with no supplier quote or no UK benchmark get 6/20 and are flagged. | computed from sourced prices |
| Reorder cycle | 15 | typical weeks between re-orders by the trade buyer: ≤4 wk 15; ≤8 wk 13; ≤13 wk 11; ≤26 wk 7; ≤52 wk 4; >52 wk 2 | judgement from product nature |
| MOQ / capital fit | 10 | MOQ × landed cost: ≤£500 10; ≤£1k 8; ≤£2k 6; ≤£5k 3; >£5k 1; unknown 4 | computed from MIC MOQ |
| Compliance burden | 10 | tier 0 none → 10; tier 1 marking/standard but no third-party certificate → 8; tier 2 Batteries Regs / DG shipping / LVD-UKCA with type-test expectation → 5; tier 3 third-party certification expected (CPR EN 54, BS EN 60898/61008 ASTA, PPE, DG Class 9) → 2 | sourced regimes |
| Captive-customer fit | 5 | would a London electrical-compliance firm's subcontract electricians consume it on EICR/PAT/EL/fire-alarm work: 5 daily, 3 occasional, 0 never | judgement |

Survivor filter (as specified): primary-channel GM ≥30% after marketplace fees where sold on marketplace, reorder ≤13 weeks, compliance tier ≤2, and both a supplier quote and a UK benchmark on record. 27 of 66 lines pass; the top 15 by score are marked **S** / **F15**.

Unit-economics assumptions (all adjustable in `build.py`): FX £1 = US$1.30 (playbook assumption); landed = FOB × (1.20 + duty) — the playbook's 1.11–1.23 band for a 20ft at 0% duty, pushed to 1.20 to cover LCL (US$45–150/cbm) and pallet-level first orders; trade sell price = 90% of the TLC Direct ex-VAT benchmark (an assumed 10% undercut to win accounts); marketplace sell = the benchmark; Amazon referral 15%, eBay 12.5% + £0.40 ≈ 13%; per-order fulfilment £12 trade (carton/pallet pick + carrier), £8 captive (van drop), £6 marketplace (3PL pick £2–6 + Tracked 24/48 £3–4); units per order per line as listed; stock at 6 turns/yr = 2 months of landed COGS at the volume needed for £2,500/month gross profit.

## 8. Compliance and shipping warnings
1. **Batteries (NiCd, NiMH, VRLA):** you become the "producer" under the Waste Batteries and Accumulators Regulations 2009 — register with the environment agency (portable: via a Battery Compliance Scheme above 1 t/yr; below that, register directly), and offer take-back once you supply more than 32 kg/yr of portable batteries; industrial batteries (UPS/fire-panel VRLA are typically classed industrial) must be taken back free when you supply replacements ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-batteries-and-waste-batteries)). NiCd is permitted only because emergency-lighting/alarm systems are exempt from the cadmium restriction — label packs for that use only. VRLA are UN2800 Class 8 but ADR-exempt when non-spillable (SP238) and packed to prevent short-circuit (on file); dry NiCd/NiMH are not dangerous goods but packs must be short-circuit protected. Weight: 12V 7Ah = 2.2 kg, 17Ah ≈ 6 kg — parcel carrier surcharges and pallet freight apply.
2. **Lithium (C1-13 emergency packs, any Li starter packs):** UN3480/UN3481 Class 9, UN 38.3 test summary required from the cell maker, ADR/IMDG limited-quantity packing, and most UK parcel carriers restrict or ban loose lithium cells from account customers without DG approval (Royal Mail/Parcelforce/DPD pages were blocked this session — confirm with the carrier before listing). Do not ship by air without a DG-trained consignor. Treat as a later line, not a launch line.
3. **Aerosols / chemicals:** none of the survivors is an aerosol; resin joint kits contain 2-part epoxy (CLP labelling, SDS on file, not DG in retail packs); welding wire needs an SDS for fume (on file). Cable-pulling lubricants, contact cleaners and WD40-type aerosols were excluded from the list because UN1950 aerosols are limited-quantity DG by road and barred from many parcel services.
4. **Third-party certification expectations (kills generic import):** MCB/RCBO (BS EN 60898/61009 with ASTA/Kitemark and enclosure compatibility), RCD (BS EN 61008 Type A), BS88 and BS1362 fuses (ASTA), fire-alarm sounders/detectors (CPR, EN 54 LPCB/VdS certificate + Declaration of Performance), PPE (PPE Regulation, Module B certificate, BSIF RSSS scrutiny — 82% of non-registered PPE failed), photoluminescent signs (PSPA/DIN 67510 class). These lines score ≤2/10 on compliance and are excluded from the survivors.
5. **UKCA/CE and importer duties:** CE remains recognised in GB; for SPDs, test leads, luminaires and batteries you are the importer of record — your name and address go on the product or packaging, you hold the Declaration of Conformity and technical file access for 10 years, and you carry product liability (playbook §3; £2–5m product-liability cover from ~£100–500/yr). For SPDs insist on the BS EN 61643-11 type-test report and for test leads on EN 61010-031 CAT III/IV test evidence; GS38-style fused leads are what electrical-compliance firms will accept.
6. **Abrasives:** bonded cutting/grinding discs are safety-critical (EN 12413, oSa/MPA burst test, 3-year expiry marking); buy only from makers that show the certificate (MIC listings cite "MPa certificates"); flap discs (EN 13743) are lower risk.
7. **Copper/earth rods:** price tracks LME copper; quote with a 30-day validity; 1.2 m lengths exceed many parcel carriers' standard length and go by pallet.
8. **Hydraulics:** hose assemblies carry pressure-failure liability; sell adaptors/QRCs as components and leave crimped assemblies to the hose shops unless you buy EN 853/856-marked hose and keep crimp records.
