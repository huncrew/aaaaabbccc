## Listings dataset (Categories A, B, C)

### Takeaway
Sixty-one listings/sold comps were captured (A: 27 ecommerce, B: 17 SaaS/software, C: 17 agencies/MSPs/IT). Within a £200–450k budget the UK ecommerce supply is thin and mostly single- or dual-marketplace; UK SaaS at £50–300k ARR is rarer still on public brokers (most UK "SaaS" listings are either sub-£50k micro-projects or £1m+); UK agencies/MSPs at £60–150k profit are plentiful and priced at roughly 3–5x EBITDA, but the detail pages rarely state whether the owner does delivery.

## Key question 4: What share of ecommerce listings are single-platform (Amazon FBA only) vs multi-channel?

### Takeaway
On Empire Flippers, Amazon FBA alone was 36.1% of all 2025 sales versus 9.6% DTC ecommerce and 5.4% dropshipping (so ~71% of ecommerce-type deals were FBA), and 5 of 13 live marketplace listings were Amazon-only; on UK brokers the picture inverts — most UK listings are own-site or eBay-led, with about a fifth explicitly selling across own site plus two or more marketplaces, and none naming Etsy.

### Cited Findings
- Empire Flippers top business models sold in 2025: Amazon FBA 60 deals (36.1%), display advertising 22 (13.3%), DTC ecommerce 16 (9.6%), YouTube 14 (8.4%), dropshipping 9 (5.4%); "Amazon FBA continues to dominate our marketplace" — [Empire Flippers 2026 report](https://info.empireflippers.com/hubfs/2026%20Lead%20Magnets/2026%20State%20of%20the%20Industry%20Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Among ecommerce-type deals sold on Empire Flippers in 2025 (FBA 60 + DTC 16 + dropshipping 9 = 85), Amazon FBA was ~71%, DTC ~19% and dropshipping ~11% (my arithmetic from the report's counts).
- On the live Empire Flippers page, "true" multi-channel (no single channel above ~90%) is rarer than the tags suggest: at most 4 of 13, once the two lopsided sub-$300k listings are reclassified as effectively single-channel.

- Etsy never appeared as a named channel in any listing fetched.
