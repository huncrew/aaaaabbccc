# Base rates for buying (vs starting) a small business (£100k–£2m): survival, returns, hours, deal-completion odds

Scope notes for the writer: UK-specific data on the survival/returns of *acquired* small businesses essentially does not exist in public form (no ONS/Companies House study of acquired-vs-founded survival was found). The best quantitative evidence is (a) a large French INSEE cohort study (EU), (b) US SBA 7(a) loan-tape analyses (US), (c) the Stanford/IESE search-fund studies (US/Canada + international incl. UK, but deals are far larger than £300k), and (d) the Search Investment Group 2023 self-funded search survey (US, deal sizes closest to the brief but self-selected sample). Each number below is flagged US / UK / EU / FR / Global. Web-search budget ran out mid-task, so a few sources (Stanford 2026 PDF, BizBuySell reports, British Business Bank pages) could only be accessed via secondary summaries; these are flagged.

## Q1. Survival of acquired small businesses vs newly-started ones at 1, 3, 5 years

### Takeaway
The only rigorous cohort study found (France, n=34,872) shows takeovers survive better than start-ups, but the gap is modest at 5 years (57% vs 55% unmatched; not significant after matching) and is concentrated in the first 1–2 years (odds of exit for takeovers 0.46× start-ups in year 1, converging to 0.93× by year 5). US SBA loan data tell the same story from the lender side: change-of-ownership loans charge off ~30–50% less often than start-up loans. UK: only generic ONS survival rates exist (38.4% five-year survival for the 2019 birth cohort); no UK acquired-vs-founded survival data was found.

### Cited Findings
- (FR/EU) Xi, Block, Lasch, Robert & Thurik (2020), "The survival of business takeovers and new venture start-ups", *Industrial and Corporate Change* 29(3):797, uses the French INSEE SINE 2002 cohort (population 100,731 firms founded/taken over Jan–Jun 2002, surveyed 2002/2005/2007). Final sample 34,872 entrepreneurs, of which 3,758 (10.8%) were business takeovers and 31,114 (89.2%) new ventures — [Source (author PDF)](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf); [journal page](https://academic.oup.com/icc/article/29/3/797/5714934)
- (FR/EU) Same study: 55% of the whole 2002 cohort was still active in Sept 2007 (~5.5 years). Takeovers 57% vs start-ups 55% survival (unmatched, statistically significant); mean survival time 56.06 vs 51.51 months. After propensity-score matching, start-up survival rises to 56% and "is no longer significantly different" from takeovers, though survival months still differ (4.08 months, P<0.001) — [Source](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)
- (FR/EU) Same study, life-cycle result: odds ratio of exit for takeovers vs start-ups = 0.46 (2002–03), 0.64 (2002–04), 0.71 (2002–05), 0.82 (2002–06), 0.93 (2002–07, P<0.05 only) — i.e. the survival advantage of buying is large in year 1 and almost gone by year 5 — [Source](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)
- (FR/EU) Same study cites INSEE benchmarks for French firms founded in 2010: 91.1% active at 1 year, 71.8% at 3 years, 60.4% at 5 years — [Source](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)
- (FR/EU) Same study, replication on SINE 2006 cohort (33,294 firms, 2,835 takeovers = 8.5%): "survival probabilities are higher for business takeovers than for new venture start-ups" again — [Source](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)
- (Canada, secondary) Repreneuriat Québec (formerly CTEQ) data as quoted by a finance-broker blog: transferred businesses 80% five-year survival vs 57% for start-ups; 87.5% when supported by experts. No year, sample size or link to the original study given — treat as low-confidence — [Source](https://www.ec2finance.com/en/business-succession-a-strategic-lever-for-growth/)
- (US) SBA 7(a) loan tape FY2020–FY2025 (373,981 loans, data as of 31 Mar 2026), analysed by lender Lendesca: 38,824 change-of-ownership (acquisition) loans (10.4% of volume, avg loan $1,121,005). Charge-off rate 0.71% for acquisition loans vs 1.5% for start-up loans vs 2.3% for businesses ≤2 years old. By acquisition loan size: <$150k 1.21%; $150–350k 1.09%; $350–500k 0.83%; $500k–1M 0.59%; $1–2M 0.44%; $2–5M 0.46%. Caveat stated by source: FY2024–25 cohorts are unseasoned so rates are understated — [Source](https://www.lendesca.com/buying-an-existing-business-is-the-safest-sba-loan-the-numbers-prove-it-the-lowest-charge-off-rate-in-sba-lending-belongs-to-one-loan-type-heres-the-data/)
- (US) Fully-seasoned cohorts FY2018–FY2019 (SBA FOIA loan tape, analysed by CT Acquisitions): lifetime charge-off 6.88% for change-of-ownership loans vs 9.83% for all other 7(a) loans (~30% lower) — [Source](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/sba-financing-statistics-2026/)
- (UK) ONS Business Demography 2024 (published 20 Nov 2025): five-year survival of UK businesses born in 2019 = 38.4% (South West highest 43.5%, West Midlands lowest 30.6%). 2024 birth rate 11.1% (317,000 births of 2.86m active), death rate 9.8% (280,000). This is all businesses, not acquisitions — [Source](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/businessdemography/2024)
- (US, general M&A context, not SMB-specific) BYU study of start-up acquisitions by larger firms: 33% of acquired workers left within the first year vs 12% of regular hires with similar skills — [Source](https://marriott.byu.edu/upload/event/event_755/_doc/Danny%20Kim%20Start-up%20Acquisition.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a UK buyer, the French result is the best proxy: expect a roughly 5–10 percentage-point survival advantage over starting in the first 1–2 years, fading to near parity by year 5. Note the French "takeover" category is small firms (France is a micro-firm economy), which fits the £100k–£2m brief better than search-fund data.
- SBA data suggest the *lender-loss* rate on acquisitions of this size is low single digits over a full loan life (seasoned cohorts ~7% charge-off vs ~10% for other purposes), but a charge-off is a far worse outcome than "business fails to pay the owner"; the latter is not captured by loan data.
- UK ONS survival rates (38% at 5 years) are much lower than French INSEE (60%) largely because of definitional differences (VAT/PAYE-based births incl. many micro-entities), so do not compare UK ONS numbers directly with the French takeover numbers.

### Gaps
- No UK or Companies-House-based study of survival of acquired vs founded small businesses was found. No ONS breakdown by ownership change.
- No 1-year/3-year survival rates specifically for takeovers were printed in the French paper's text (only the odds ratios and the 5.5-year 57% vs 55%); the Kaplan–Meier curves are in Figures 1–2 of the PDF.
- The European Commission report "Improving the evidence base on transfer of business in Europe" (2020) is referenced on the EC page but its statistics could not be retrieved — [EC page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/smes/supporting-entrepreneurship/transfer-business_en).
- A 2026 paper "Survival after takeover entry: Effects of entrepreneurs' domain experience and knowledge acquisition behaviour" (van Balen, de Regt, de Jong, *International Small Business Journal*, DOI 10.1177/02662426261470403) exists but was paywalled (403) — likely the most directly relevant recent study.
- Kauffman Foundation and US Census ABS acquired-vs-founded survival tables: not retrieved (Census ABS page returned 404; search budget exhausted).

## Q2. Search fund / ETA outcomes (Stanford 2024/2026, IESE 2024, Yale 2025, self-funded data)

### Takeaway
Traditional search funds (US/Canada): ~58–63% of concluded searches acquire; median 19–21 months from start of search to close; 26–31% of acquisitions lose investor money (about a third of those are total losses); aggregate IRR ~34–35% / 4.5–4.75x ROI, but ~2.1x ROI / 20% IRR excluding the top 10%. Yale's investor-side sample (n=1,192) finds the median entrepreneur outcome including broken searches is 1.0x MOIC and only 51% of entrants make any money. Self-funded searchers (SIG 2023, n=279, US) close faster (53% within 12 months, 74% within 18, 83% within 24 — conditional on acquiring), buy at <5x EBITDA with 80–90% leverage, and report only 5% investor capital destruction and 9% EBITDA declines, but the sample is self-selected and 81% had owned <3 years.

### Cited Findings
**Stanford GSB 2024 Search Fund Study (US/Canada; 681 funds since 1984; 328 acquisitions; returns to 31 Dec 2023; published 28 Jun 2024)**
- 63% of concluded search funds made an acquisition (down from 66% in 2022 study); "since 2014, the acquisition rate has been consistently around 57%" — [Source PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Time from start of search to deal close (n=324): median 19.0 months, min 0, max 72; <11 months 16%, 11–20 months 36%, 20–30 months 31%, 30+ months 18% (Exhibit 6) — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Unsuccessful searchers signed their first LOI on average 7.8 months into the search — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Outcomes of acquisitions (Figure I, n=296 with ≥1 year of operation): 69% gain, 31% loss; of the losses, 66% partial loss and 34% total loss (i.e. ~10.5% of all acquisitions are total losses, ~20.5% partial). Of gains: 27% 1–2x, 36% 2–5x, 25% 5–10x, 11% >10x (text: "11% of companies achieving greater than 10x return"). "There has also been an increase in the percentage of acquisitions that reported a loss in the last ten years" — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Aggregate IRR 35.1%, ROI 4.5x (2022 study: 35.3%, 5.2x); exited funds IRR 42.9%. 2021–22 acquisition cohort so far: IRR 23%, ROI 1.5x. Partnered searches IRR 40.5% vs solo lower. Median purchase price $14.4m at 7.0x EBITDA, 27% EBITDA margin, 34 employees — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Searcher wealth: mean equity earned per still-operating entrepreneur $6.09m (median $1.98m); exited: mean $5.7m (median $2.25m). Median first-year CEO base salary $190k; total comp by tenure (median): ≤1yr $200k, 1–2yr $262k, 2–3yr $265k, 3–4yr $292k — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Sellers' post-close engagement lengthened to a median 6 months (from 4); over 90% of searchers report a positive post-close relationship with the seller — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- Stanford explicitly states self-funded searchers "rely on raising more debt … often target materially smaller [companies] … with higher percentage equity ownership (50 to 70%)" and that it has "not collected sufficient data" to report comparable outcomes — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)

**Stanford GSB 2026 Search Fund Study (US/Canada; data to 31 Dec 2025; 862 funds; 337 acquired companies) — accessed only via secondary summaries; primary page: [Stanford case E967](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations)**
- 58% of concluded searches acquired overall; only 48% for funds launched 2021–2024 that had concluded. Outcome distribution (337 companies): total/partial loss 26.4%, 1–2x 20.2%, 2–5x 27.6%, 5–10x 17.5%, 10x+ 8.3%. Aggregate IRR 33.9%, ROI 4.75x; excluding 10x+ funds: 27% IRR / 2.8x; excluding top 10% by ROI: 20% IRR / 2.1x. Median search duration 21 months (2024–25 deals); median price $16m, EBITDA $2.5m, 6.2x — [Source (CapitalPad summary)](https://capitalpad.com/search-fund-statistics/)
- Early LOI predicts success: searchers who signed a first LOI within 6 months acquired 74% of the time, within 12 months 65%, vs 48% cohort average. Median 20 months start-to-close; first LOI typically at month 7; ~2.5 LOIs per close; ~6 months diligence-to-close. Reasons LOIs failed: 79% something found in diligence, 45% valuation gap, 40% lack of investor support (multi-response) — [Source (SMEVentures summary of Stanford 2026)](https://www.smeventures.com/insights/why-business-acquisition-deals-fall-apart)
- Stanford Search Fund Primer (as quoted): "1 in 3 traditional search funds don't close an acquisition within 24 months" — [Source](https://buysmallsellhigh.substack.com/p/what-are-your-odds-of-finding-a-company)

**IESE International Search Funds 2024 (outside US/Canada incl. UK; 320 funds; data to end-2023; published Sep 2024)**
- Of 199 concluded funds: 146 acquired, 38 ended without acquisition, 15 deviated from the model → 79% acquisition rate (vs 63% US/Canada). Of the 146: 125 operating, 15 exited with positive return, 6 exited at a loss — [Source PDF](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)
- Returns (130 funds with ≥1 yr operation incl. 37 failed searches): ROI 2.0x, IRR 18.1%; median fund 1.4x; top fund 31.4x; operating companies 2.1x/19.6%; terminal funds 1.9x/17.3%. 62% of international acquisitions made since 2020 (immature) — [Source](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)
- UK: 35 first-time search funds over the decade (4th globally after Spain 67, Mexico 50, Brazil 34); first search fund ever outside US was UK 1992; 3 of the 15 positive international exits were in the UK — [Source](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E); [IESE Insight](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-global-growth/)
- Searchers who terminated without acquiring signed their first LOI in month 9 on average; median European searcher salary $90k (range $35k–$127k); median international deal $11.7m price, $7.8m revenue, 24% EBITDA margin, 50 employees — [Source](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)

**Yale SOM case "How are Search Fund Investors Really Faring?" (Lazier, Thomas, Wasserstein; 27 Oct 2025; US; 1,192 entrepreneur-backing observations from ~12 serial investors, $700m deployed)**
- 64% of backed entrepreneurs found an actionable deal (768 of 1,192); broken searches cost only 2% of capital. Only 51% of entrepreneurs (612/1,192) produced ≥1.0x MOIC — "there is only a 51% chance they will make any money at all". 31% of completed deals returned >3.0x, = 20% of all entrants — [Source PDF](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf)
- Mean MOIC incl. broken searches 2.0x (excl. 2.78x); median incl. 1.0x (excl. 1.6x); investor-weighted 2.50x (vs Stanford 4.5x). 58% of deal MOICs fall in 0–1.99x; only 2% exceed 10x (vs 11% in Stanford). Loss bucket 32% of deals (Stanford 31%); of losses 23% total / 77% partial (Stanford 34% / 66%). "None [of the investors] match the Stanford benchmark." Authors flag possible sampling bias — [Source](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf)

**Search Investment Group "2023 Self-Funded Search Study" (US; survey Aug–Oct 2022; 1,027 invited via Searchfunder/Twitter/LinkedIn; 279 respondents; 109 had acquired = 39%; published Jan 2023)**
- Search duration for those who acquired: ≤6 months 21%, 7–12 months 32%, 13–18 months 21%, 19–24 months 9%, 25–30 months 9%, >30 months 8% → 53% within 12 months, 74% within 18, 83% within 24; median 7–12 months (vs 20 months traditional). Full-time searchers: 58% closed within 12 months; part-time: 46%. Intermediary-sourced deals: 55% closed within 12 months vs 44% for proprietary outreach — [Source PDF](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf); [Acquiring Minds summary](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/robert-graham-jordan-carter-sig-search-investment-group)
- Target found → close: median 4–6 months. Successful searchers submitted 6.9 LOIs on average, 2.4 executed; <44% of executed LOIs closed. 54% sourced via broker/intermediary, 25% proprietary outreach, 15% personal network — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- Pricing/leverage: 83% paid <5.0x EBITDA; 74% of sub-$1m-EBITDA deals paid <4.0x; 58% used SBA 7(a), 45% seller note, 12% no debt; most common total LTV 80–90%; 70% of sub-3.0x deals achieved ≥80% LTV; >60% of seller notes were 10–19.9% of price; 62% raised outside equity; 68% put in <$200k personal capital, 24% <$50k, 11% none — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- Post-close (all who acquired; 81% had owned <3 years): EBITDA declined 9%, maintained 54%, increased 37% (16% by ≥$500k); revenue printed as declined "--%", maintained 67%, increased 33% (14% by ≥$5m). Seller relationship: 78% positive (53% very), 13% negative. First-year salary: 95% took one; 30% <$100k, 56% $100–199k, 14% ≥$200k — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- Returns (self-reported, operating deals marked at "a reasonable multiple"): investor IRR ≥40% for 39%; <10% for 16%; median 25–29.9%; MOIC 1.0–3.0x for 62%, ≥3.0x >33%, ≥5.0x ~15%, ≥10x >11%; "only 5% of searchers destroyed capital for investors". Searcher net equity proceeds >$1m for 53%, >$3m 29%, >$10m ~10% — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- The study's own Appendix: "This study didn't attempt to collect data or draw conclusions on the rate of success of the search stage"; acknowledges self-selection bias — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- Acquisition Lab (Chelsea Wood, May 2022, US): ~40 of 250+ members had closed (~16%); average search 8–10 months for broad criteria, ~12 months for specific — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/chelsea-wood-acquisition-lab)

### Inferences
- Combining Stanford 2024 (31% of acquisitions lose money; 34% of those are total losses) gives ~10% of traditional-search acquisitions ending in total loss and ~20% in partial loss; Yale's investor sample implies ~7% total / ~25% partial. The self-funded 5% "capital destroyed" figure is likely a lower bound (young holds, self-selection, preferred-equity structure protects investors not the searcher).
- Self-funded search data is the closest analogue to a £300k UK owner-operator buy, but even SIG's deals are mostly $500k–$2m EBITDA, i.e. 5–20x bigger than the brief. Base rates for £300k deals must be extrapolated downward; SBA size-band charge-off data (1.1–1.2% for <$350k loans vs 0.4–0.6% for $1m+) suggests smaller deals fail more.
- Unconditional deal-completion probability for self-funded searchers is unknown; the 39% of SIG respondents who had acquired and Acquisition Lab's 16% are snapshot figures, not cohort rates.

### Gaps
- Stanford 2026 PDF could not be parsed (page fetched but content not exposed); total-vs-partial loss split for 2026 not available.
- No UK-only search fund returns (IESE does not break out UK IRR/ROI).
- No SIG/Searchfunder data on year-1 vs year-3 owner hours, GM hiring, or unconditional search success.

## Q3. Loan default / charge-off rates for acquisition finance (US SBA 7(a); UK CBILS/RLS/GGS)

### Takeaway
US: change-of-ownership 7(a) loans are the best-performing purpose category — 0.71% charged off so far in FY2020–25 cohorts (unseasoned) and 6.88% lifetime in fully-seasoned FY2018–19 cohorts, vs 9.83% for other purposes. UK: no acquisition-specific default data exists; the government guarantee schemes show 4.85% of Growth Guarantee Scheme facilities (1.69% by value) claimed by Dec 2025, CBILS 0.99% defaulted / 7.58% settled by Sept 2024 — but these are pandemic/general-purpose loans, not acquisition loans.

### Cited Findings
- (US) FY2020–FY2025 7(a) loan tape (373,981 loans): acquisition charge-off 0.71% vs start-ups 1.5% vs ≤2-yr-old businesses 2.3%; by size and industry as listed in Q1; hotels 0.06%, full-service restaurants 0.92%, limited-service restaurants 1.30% — [Source](https://www.lendesca.com/buying-an-existing-business-is-the-safest-sba-loan-the-numbers-prove-it-the-lowest-charge-off-rate-in-sba-lending-belongs-to-one-loan-type-heres-the-data/)
- (US) Seasoned FY2018–19 cohorts: 6.88% vs 9.83% lifetime charge-off; FY2025 change-of-ownership volume $8.29bn, ~7,003 loans, avg ~$1.18m, +34.6% YoY, ~22% of 7(a) approvals (iBusiness Funding analysis) — [Source](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/sba-financing-statistics-2026/)
- (US) 2026 SBA 7(a) acquisition loan terms: max Prime+3.0% on >$350k (9.75% at 6.75% Prime, March 2026); average all-in 8.86% (Q4 2025 FOIA); 10-year fully amortising; 10% minimum equity injection; guarantee fee 3–3.75% of guaranteed portion; DSCR floor 1.1x for small loans — [Source](https://ebitcommunity.com/p/sba-7-a-acquisition-loan-rates-and-fees-in-2026)
- (US) SBA 7(a) requires 100% change of ownership (cannot buy 60% with seller keeping 40%); 10% down on change-of-ownership loans >$500k — [Source (Nav)](https://www.nav.com/blog/sba-loan-requirements-89236/)
- (UK) Growth Guarantee Scheme incl. RLS iteration 3, as at 31 Dec 2025: 19,307 facilities, £3.25bn; 937 lender claims of £55.03m settled = 1.69% of drawn value / 4.85% of facilities (70% government guarantee). Earlier: 0.26% of value at 30 Sep 2024; 1.14% at 30 Jun 2025 — [Source (BBB, page blocked to bots; figures from search summary)](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/about/research-and-publications/ggs-including-rls-iteration-3-performance-data-31-december-2025)
- (UK) COVID schemes as at 30 Sep 2024 (gov.uk): CBILS £25.83bn drawn — 42.81% fully repaid, 46.59% on schedule, 1.34% in arrears, 0.99% defaulted, 7.58% settled (paid out to lenders). BBLS £46.52bn — 14.05% repaid, 56.33% on schedule, 5.04% arrears, 0.93% defaulted, 22.39% settled. All schemes: £76.89bn drawn, £10.96bn settled, £1.91bn flagged suspected fraud — [Source](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/covid-19-loan-guarantee-schemes-repayment-data-september-2024/covid-19-loan-guarantee-schemes-performance-data-as-at-30-september-2024)
- (UK) Earlier BBB statement: fewer than 0.2% of CBILS facilities in arrears and ~0.6% defaulted (undated) — [Source](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/finance-options/legacy-programmes/coronavirus-business-interruption-loan-scheme-cbils/cbils-faqs-for-smes)

### Inferences
- The UK has no equivalent of the SBA 7(a) acquisition loan tape. GGS is the closest instrument a £300k UK buyer would use (70% guarantee, up to £2m), and its cumulative claim rate (4.85% of facilities in ~18 months) is already above the US acquisition-loan charge-off rate — but GGS includes working-capital and growth loans to weaker credits, so it is not an acquisition base rate.
- The seasoned US figure (~7% lifetime charge-off) is the most defensible "lender-loss" base rate for a leveraged owner-operator acquisition.

### Gaps
- No UK data on default rates for business-acquisition loans specifically (BBB does not publish by purpose; broker/lender data not found). RLS iterations 1&2 Dec 2025 page could not be read (bot-blocked).
- CRS report R41146 on SBA 7(a) (403) — program-wide purchase rates by year not retrieved.

## Q4. What happens to revenue/profit in the first two years after a founder sells to an individual buyer (attrition, staff, seller dependency)

### Takeaway
Quantitative evidence is thin. Self-funded searchers (US) self-report that 9% saw EBITDA decline post-close and 91% maintained or grew it; traditional-search first cohorts show 1.5x ROI / 23% IRR after 1–2 years. The only structured failure taxonomy found (24 failed acquirer interviews) attributes ~65% of failures to seller misalignment/dishonesty, plus cash-conversion-cycle misjudgement, ~6/24 to market/supplier shocks, and over-leverage, owner dependency and key-employee departure as recurring factors.

### Cited Findings
- (US) SIG 2023 self-funded study: post-close EBITDA declined for 9%, maintained 54%, increased 37%; revenue maintained 67% / increased 33% (as printed); 81% had owned <3 years — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- (US) Stanford 2024: companies acquired 2021–22 report IRR 23% and ROI 1.5x as of end-2023 ("off to a slightly slower start than prior cohorts"); the 2017–2020 cohort >50% IRR with 44% exited — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- (US) Stanford Primer guidance (as summarised in the 2024 study): "the first six to 18 months after the acquisition, searchers typically make few significant changes" — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- (US) Jed Morris, *Buyer Beware* research, 24 interviews with failed acquirers (Acquiring Minds, 20 Mar 2025): ~65% of failures traced to misalignment or outright dishonesty with the seller; cash-conversion-cycle misjudgement "a major recurring killer"; ~6 of 24 involved black-swan market/supplier shocks; other recurring factors: over-leveraging on first deal, inadequate quality-of-earnings, owner dependency and key-employee departure, post-close insurance cost jumps (30% in his own first deal), weak pricing/cost tracking, integration failures in multi-unit buys — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/jed-morris-buyer-beware)
- (US) Seller involvement post-close: Stanford 2024 median seller engagement 6 months; >90% positive seller relationship. SIG: 78% positive, 13% negative — [Stanford](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf); [SIG](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- (US) Why deals die pre-close (Stanford 2026 via SMEVentures): 79% diligence findings, 45% valuation, 40% investor support — [Source](https://www.smeventures.com/insights/why-business-acquisition-deals-fall-apart)
- (US, aggregator, unsourced) "Most failed acquisitions fail in the first 12 months post-close, with a weak transition plan killing more deals after the wire than any other factor" — [Source (CT Acquisitions buyer guide)](https://ctacquisitions.com/things-to-consider-when-buying-a-business-2026/); "average employee turnover after a merger hits 47% within the first year and 75% within three years" — [Source (M&A Community, no primary cited)](https://mnacommunity.com/insights/employee-retention-after-acquisition/) — both low-confidence.

### Inferences
- Top 5 documented causes of post-acquisition failure for owner-operator buys, ranked by how often they appear across the Morris interviews, Stanford LOI-failure data and Acquisition Lab: (1) seller misrepresentation / misaligned incentives (incl. undisclosed customer or key-employee dependency), (2) cash-conversion-cycle / working-capital misjudgement, (3) over-leverage relative to true (post-QoE) earnings, (4) owner dependency and key-employee departure after handover, (5) external market/supplier shocks (≈25% of Morris's cases). This ranking is an inference; only Morris gives frequencies.
- A first-time buyer should expect roughly a 1-in-10 chance of EBITDA decline in the first years even in the optimistic SIG sample; traditional-search data implies a higher (≈1 in 4) chance of ending in a loss position over the hold.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying customer attrition % or key-staff departure % after a founder sells a sub-£2m business to an individual. The BYU 33%-in-year-1 figure is for start-ups acquired by corporates, not applicable.
- No UK data at all on post-transfer performance.

### Takeaway
No survey reporting post-acquisition owner hours by year, or the share installing a GM, was found. The only hours data is for the *search* phase (69% of full-time self-funded searchers spend ≥40 h/week). ## Q6. Probability a buyer who starts searching closes within 12 / 24 months

### Takeaway
Conditional on eventually acquiring, self-funded searchers close within 12 months 53% of the time and within 24 months 83%; traditional searchers ~35–40% within 12 months and ~65–70% within 24 (median 19–21 months). Unconditionally, 58–63% of traditional searchers ever acquire (48% for 2021–24 launches), and the Yale investor sample gives 64%; self-funded unconditional rates are unmeasured (snapshot: 39% of SIG respondents, ~16% of Acquisition Lab members had closed). Broker-side: only ~20–30% of listed small businesses ever sell, and Stanford's 1-in-3 never close within 24 months.

### Cited Findings
- (US) SIG 2023 (conditional on acquiring): ≤12 months 53%, ≤18 months 74%, ≤24 months 83%; full-time 58% ≤12 months; part-time 46% — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf); [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/robert-graham-jordan-carter-sig-search-investment-group)
- (US) Stanford 2024 (conditional): median 19 months; 16% <11 months; 52% <20 months; 82% <30 months; unconditional acquisition rate 63% (57% since 2014) — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- (US) Stanford 2026: 58% overall, 48% for 2021–24 launches; first LOI within 6 months → 74% acquire; within 12 months → 65% — [Source](https://www.smeventures.com/insights/why-business-acquisition-deals-fall-apart); [CapitalPad](https://capitalpad.com/search-fund-statistics/)
- (US) Yale 2025: 64% of 1,192 backed entrepreneurs found an actionable deal — [Source](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf)
- (International incl. UK) IESE 2024: 79% of concluded international funds acquired — [Source](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)
- (US) Acquisition Lab: ~40 of 250+ members closed (~16%) as of May 2022; typical search 8–12 months — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/chelsea-wood-acquisition-lab)
- (US) Stanford Primer: 1 in 3 traditional search funds do not close within 24 months — [Source](https://buysmallsellhigh.substack.com/p/what-are-your-odds-of-finding-a-company)
- (US) Seller-side conversion: "only 30% of small businesses successfully sell" of 200,000+ listed annually (Exit Planning Institute, via Teamshares); Teamshares reports 80% close rate on its signed LOIs — [Source](https://www.teamshares.com/resources/succession-planning-statistics/). ProCloser (aggregator citing BizBuySell/IBBA, no URLs): 20–25% of publicly listed main-street businesses complete a sale; post-LOI close 70–85% in lower-middle-market; main-street time to sell 7–10 months; SDE multiples 2.0–3.5x under $2m EV — [Source](https://procloser.ai/blog/business-sale-statistics/)
- (US) SIG: <44% of executed LOIs closed; 6.9 LOIs submitted / 2.4 executed per successful searcher; Stanford 2026: ~2.5 LOIs per close — [SIG](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf); [SMEVentures](https://www.smeventures.com/insights/why-business-acquisition-deals-fall-apart)
- (US) Buyer optimism: 2013 BizBuySell survey — >78% of prospective buyers believed they could buy within a year — [Source](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/Survey-Prospective-Business-Buyers-Sellers-Confident-They-Can-Close-Deals-In-Current-Business-for-Sale-Market/)

### Inferences
- **Answer to key question, part 1 (100 UK first-time buyers targeting £300k):** Multiplying the traditional-search unconditional rate (48–63% ever acquire) by the share of acquirers who close within ~18–20 months (52% Stanford; 74% SIG self-funded) gives roughly 25–45 closes within 18 months for full-time, funded, MBA-type searchers. Part-time or unfunded UK first-timers (Acquisition Lab snapshot 16%; SIG part-time 46% within 12 months of those who closed) plausibly land lower: **~15–30 of 100 within 18 months**. This is an inference from US data; no UK buyer-conversion data exists.
- Broker-side data (only 20–30% of listings sell; <44% of executed LOIs close) implies a buyer should expect to sign 2–3 LOIs to close one deal.

### Gaps
- No UK broker data on buyer conversion (BizBuySell/IBBA pages returned 403; UK equivalents e.g. Daltons/BusinessesForSale not found without search).
- No cohort study following self-funded searchers from search start (SIG explicitly declined to measure).

## Q7. Returns on capital: cash-on-cash for a business bought at ~3× SDE with 50–70% leverage

### Takeaway
No published survey reports cash-on-cash returns at this deal size. The only worked figure found is an Acquiring Minds illustration ($1m price / $300k SDE / 80% SBA debt → ~$130k net to owner after interest, amortisation and tax, "not much better than a salaried job"). Using cited SBA rate/term inputs, a £300k / £100k-SDE / 60%-leverage deal implies ~£72k pre-tax cash to the owner after debt service on £120k equity (~60% cash-on-cash before paying yourself, ~20–25% after a £45k owner salary) — my arithmetic, assumptions stated.

### Cited Findings
- (US) Illustration from SIG partners (Nov 2021): $1m purchase, $300k SDE, $800k SBA loan, $200k down (100% ownership); annual interest ~$40k, amortisation ~$80k, taxes ~$50k → ~$130k net cash; authors' conclusion: "not much better than a salaried job" given the personal guarantee — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/jordan-carter-robert-graham-search-investment-group)
- (US) SIG 2023: at 80% LTV and low multiples "it is not uncommon for an SBA 7(a)-financed acquisition … to generate twice as much cash flow as needed to service the debt (DSCR of 2.0x or greater)"; investor IRR median 25–29.9%; 62% of investors at 1.0–3.0x MOIC — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- (US) Debt cost inputs 2026: average 7(a) all-in 8.86%, 10-year amortisation, 10% minimum equity, DSCR floor 1.1x — [Source](https://ebitcommunity.com/p/sba-7-a-acquisition-loan-rates-and-fees-in-2026)
- (US) Main-street pricing: 2.0–3.5x SDE for sub-$2m deals (aggregator) — [Source](https://procloser.ai/blog/business-sale-statistics/); SIG: 74% of sub-$1m-EBITDA deals bought <4.0x EBITDA — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- (US) Traditional-search equity outcomes over full holds: median searcher equity $1.98m operating / $2.25m exited; aggregate investor ROI 4.5x but 2.1x excluding top 10% (2026) — [Stanford 2024](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf); [CapitalPad/Stanford 2026](https://capitalpad.com/search-fund-statistics/)
- (UK) GGS terms: 70% government guarantee to lender; borrower remains 100% liable — [Source](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/finance-options/debt-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme)

### Inferences
- Worked example (my calculation, not a sourced statistic): price £300k = 3.0× SDE £100k; 60% debt £180k at 9% over 10 years → annual debt service ≈ £28k (annuity factor 0.156). Pre-tax cash after debt service ≈ £72k on £120k equity + ~£15–20k fees ≈ 50–60% cash-on-cash *if the owner draws no separate salary*; after a £45k market salary for the owner's own labour (assumption), ≈ £27k ≈ 20% cash-on-cash. At 70% leverage (£210k debt, £33k service) the pre-salary figure rises to ~£67k on £90k equity (~70%) but DSCR falls to ~3.0x pre-salary / ~1.7x post-salary. UK borrowing costs were not sourced (GGS pricing not published centrally), so the 9% assumption is borrowed from US data.
- The distribution matters more than the mean: applying Stanford/Yale loss rates (26–32% of acquisitions lose money; ~7–10% total loss) to the above means the *expected* cash-on-cash is materially below the headline.

### Gaps
- No empirical survey of realised cash-on-cash returns for sub-£500k/sub-$1m owner-operator acquisitions (US or UK).
- No UK acquisition-loan pricing data found.

## Key question synthesis: "If 100 people like a first-time UK buyer each start looking for a £300k business…"

### Takeaway
All-inference, built from the cited US/EU numbers above (no UK cohort exists): ~15–30 close within 18 months; of those, ~70–80% are still trading and paying the owner at least the pre-purchase profit at 3 years; ~25–30% end in a loss position over the hold and ~5–10% lose most or all of their equity.

### Cited Findings
- Inputs: unconditional acquisition rate 48–63% (traditional) / snapshot 16–39% (self-funded, Acquisition Lab & SIG); conditional close ≤18 months 52–74%; EBITDA decline post-close 9% (SIG); loss share of acquisitions 26–32% (Stanford 2024/2026, Yale); total-loss share of acquisitions ~7–10% (Yale 23% × 32%; Stanford 34% × 31%); self-funded investor capital destroyed 5% (SIG); seasoned SBA acquisition charge-off 6.88%; takeover survival advantage over start-ups largest in year 1 (odds ratio 0.46) and ~gone by year 5 (0.93) — sources as cited in Q1, Q2, Q3, Q6.

### Inferences
- Of 100 starters: **~15–30 buy within 18 months** (lower end for part-time/unfunded; upper end for full-time, funded, broker-sourced — SIG shows broker-sourced deals close faster than proprietary outreach).
- Of ~25 buyers, at 3 years: **~18–20 still trading with EBITDA ≥ pre-purchase level** (SIG: 91% maintained/grew EBITDA in a young, optimistic sample; French takeover 3-year survival is above the 71.8% start-up benchmark; SBA charge-offs ≤7%), **~5–7 in a partial-loss position**, and **~2–3 having lost most of their equity** (7–10% total-loss rate). These are extrapolations from larger US deals; smaller deals (SBA <$350k loans charge off ~1.1–1.2% vs 0.4–0.6% for $1m+) likely sit at the worse end.
- "Paying the owner ≥ pre-purchase profit" is stricter than "EBITDA maintained" because debt service must be covered first: at 60–70% leverage roughly 25–35% of SDE goes to debt service in the worked example, so a buyer needs EBITDA growth of ~30% just to match the seller's pre-sale take-home. Applying that filter, the "still paying the owner ≥ pre-purchase profit" count is plausibly closer to **10–14 of 25** than 18–20.

### Gaps
- No UK data at any step; no data for sub-£500k deals specifically; no cohort study starting at "began searching".

## Do buyers with sales backgrounds do better than technical buyers?

### Takeaway
No study directly tests sales vs technical backgrounds. The only quantitative hints: in the SIG self-funded survey, sales & marketing was the one background over-represented among those who acquired (33% of acquirers vs 27% of all respondents, +6 pts — the largest positive gap), while operations/management was under-represented (37% vs 43%); Stanford's traditional searchers are predominantly consulting/PE/finance (sales only 2–8% of principals). The French cohort finds same-sector experience (OR 0.80) and management experience (OR 0.88) reduce exit odds, with no sales/technical split.

### Cited Findings
- (US) SIG 2023 professional backgrounds (multi-response), acquired vs all respondents: Operations/management 37% vs 43% (−6); Sales & Marketing 33% vs 27% (+6); Entrepreneurship 26% vs 27%; Consulting 22% vs 25%; Private Equity 21% vs 25%; Engineering 19% vs 20%; Investment Banking 18% vs 16%; Military 15% vs 15%; Corporate finance 13% vs 12%. The authors state "there was not a material proportional difference in the backgrounds" — [Source](https://hadleyfamilycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/Search-Investment-Group-2023-Self-Funded-Search-Study.pdf)
- (US) Stanford 2024 Exhibit 2, backgrounds of search fund principals over 12 two-year cohorts: Consulting 26%→16% (latest), Sales 12%→6% (range 1–12%), Operations 4%→4% (range 1–23%); category definitions changed in 2018/2020 — [Source](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)
- (US) Stanford 2026: management consulting is the largest background at 19% of the 2024–25 cohort; 80% MBAs — [Source](https://capitalpad.com/search-fund-statistics/)
- (US) SIG partners' view: prior private-equity experience is "sometimes a detriment" in micro-cap acquisitions because of mismatched expectations — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/jordan-carter-robert-graham-search-investment-group)
- (FR/EU) Xi et al. 2020: same-sector experience odds ratio for exit 0.80 (P<0.001) across models (0.70–0.82); management experience 0.88 (P<0.01); for takeovers specifically, same-sector experience OR 0.69–0.83; small-firm work experience and entrepreneurial motivation matter for start-ups but "for takeovers, these factors do not have an effect" — [Source](https://personal.eur.nl/thurik/Research/Articles/The%20survival%20of%20business%20takeover%20and%20new%20startups%20in%20ICC.pdf)
- (US) Acquisition Lab: "nearly every searcher questions whether they have the right background"; the coach's view is that team assembly beats individual expertise — [Source](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/chelsea-wood-acquisition-lab)
- (General) NBER survey: CEOs explain only 3.9–7.0% of firm performance variance — [Source](https://www.nber.org/reporter/2023number1/ceos-and-firm-performance)

### Inferences
- The SIG +6-point tilt toward sales backgrounds among acquirers is suggestive (sales skills help in sourcing and negotiating with owners) but it is a cross-sectional survey difference in a self-selected sample, not an outcome measure; nothing shows sales-background owners run the business better post-close.
- The stronger, replicated evidence is that *same-sector experience* — not sales vs technical — lowers exit risk by ~20%.

### Gaps
- No study of post-acquisition performance by buyer functional background (sales vs technical vs finance) at any deal size; the paywalled van Balen et al. 2026 ISBJ paper on domain experience after takeover entry is the closest candidate.
