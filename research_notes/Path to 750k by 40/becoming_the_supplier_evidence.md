# "I'll become the supplier myself" — what the filed accounts and ONS data actually show

*Research note, 29 Sep 2026. All company figures below come from accounts filed at Companies House (CH), read by OCR from the filed PDFs, unless another URL is given. CH links are to the company's filing-history page; the accounts year is stated so the exact PDF can be opened. "Op profit" = operating profit as stated in the P&L; "NA" = net assets. Where a company filed small/abridged accounts, turnover is not disclosed and the cell says "n/d".*

---

## 1. Short answer

1. **Becoming a large supplier from a standing start is rare but real, and it took the winners 10–25 years, external capital, and (usually) a sale to a bigger consolidator at the £30m–£100m stage.** Of the 20 UK firms examined, only four founder-started businesses reached >£25m turnover while still founder-controlled (Cleveland Group, Portable Space, Midsummer, Hydraquip pre-2022), and two of those have since taken PE/corporate money.
2. **Base rate:** 11.8% of all UK wholesale enterprises (SIC 46) have turnover over £5m, and only 41.7% of wholesale start-ups survive five years. Roughly 4% of established wholesalers (10+ staff) qualify as ONS "high-growth" in any year. So "small wholesaler → £5m+" is a minority outcome, and "→ £25m+ founder-owned" is a low-single-digit-percent outcome.
3. **The phone and solar analogies cut both ways.** In phones, people who entered in year 2–5 of the wave (Caudwell 1987, Dunstone 1989, Peter Jones 1998) *did* become the giants — but each did so on wholesale/retail volume with 1–3% margins and later either sold out (Caudwell £1.46bn, 2006; Data Select → Westcoast, 2019) or was destroyed when the upstream network operators pulled supply (Phones 4u, 2014). In solar, the three largest UK distributors (Segen 2004, Midsummer 2005, Alternergy 2006) were all founded **4–6 years before** the 2010 Feed-in Tariff wave; nobody who entered in year 3–5 of that wave appears among the leaders, and even the leaders have just posted operating losses (Segen −£22.6m FY24, −£7.8m FY25; Midsummer −£0.65m FY25).
4. **The consolidators in distribution are overwhelmingly PE- or corporate-funded, and the successful independents were their targets, not their peers:** Cromwell → Grainger (£310m), Hayley → Descours & Cabaud, Hydraquip → Finning (£65m), R&G Fluid Power → Diploma (~£100m), Segen → CEF/Labora, Weldability → Indutrade, Ainscough → Bank-of-Scotland-backed MBO (£255m), 20:20 Mobile → Brightstar, Data Select → Westcoast.

## 3. What the trajectories say about "years to £1m / £5m"

- **The winners were at £1m within 1–2 years and £5m within 3–6 years.** Data Select: £2.1m in its first 17 months, £36.9m in year 3. Caudwell: £1m in year 2, ~£5m in year 3. Cleveland: £1.1m year 1 (press), £15.5m by year 7. Segen: £2.3m in year 4–5, £7.8m in year 5–6, £83.6m in year 8. In every case the jump coincided with a demand wave (analogue/GSM handsets 1987–91; pre-pay/3G 1998–2003; FIT solar 2010–12; Covid/Brexit container shortage 2020–22 for Cleveland, whose revenue rose 73% to £35.6m in FY21 and 65% to £58.6m in FY22, then fell back 23% over the following two years to £51.4m in FY25).
- **Firms that grew "the normal way" took 15–40 years to get to £8m–£26m:** Portable Space 23 years to £26.5m; HTS Spares 14 years to £13.9m (having bought an existing trade on day 1); Weldability 38 years to ~£8m; S Jones ~50 years of container work to £14.7m; Hydraquip 32 years to £26m.
- **Many never leave the micro band:** Billie Box (13 years, 3 staff), Chris Beard APS (13 years, 1 employee), Jakespare (19 years, 2 employees), Bearing Traders (40 years, 32 staff, NA £364k). These are the modal outcome, not the exceptions — see the base rates.

## 6. Counter-evidence: what happens to independents when the sector consolidates

- **Builders'/plumbers' merchants:** the top five (Travis Perkins, Saint-Gobain, Grafton, Wolseley, Screwfix) held "over 75%" of the market by 2015 after "larger companies acquired established smaller merchants" ([AMA/PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/uk-builders-and-plumbers-merchants-market-report-2015-2019---travis-perkins-saint-gobain-grafton-wolseley-and-screwfix-account-for-75-of-market-share-565884501.html)). BMBI data show merchant volumes down 8.1% and almost a quarter of general merchants loss-making in 2024 ([Professional Builders Merchant](https://professionalbuildersmerchant.co.uk/news/assessing-the-biggest-challenges-and-opportunities-for-independent-merchants-in-2026/)).
- **MRO/industrial supplies:** Cromwell was explicitly "the largest independent British MRO distributor" when Grainger bought it; Rubix has since absorbed Brammer, Buck & Hickman and ERIKS UK&I into a >€850m UK business. Hayley survived as an independent only until 2016. The surviving independents in the sample are small (Bearing Traders NA £364k after 40 years).
- **Mobile phones — the upstream can switch you off:** Phones 4u (720 stores) went into administration in Sept 2014 when EE (>50% of its sales) and Vodafone (20%) withdrew; its CEO: "if the mobile network operators decline to supply us, we do not have a business" ([Financier Worldwide](https://www.financierworldwide.com/phones-4u-enters-administration)). Data Select — the largest UK handset distributor — ran at 0.9–2.3% operating margin (CH FY19, FY21) and was bought by Westcoast in 2019 chiefly for its Samsung relationship ([Mobile News](https://mobilenewscwp.co.uk/News/article/westcoast-grabs-data-select-get-samsung/)); 20:20 Mobile went to Brightstar in 2014 ([Telecompaper](https://www.telecompaper.com/news/brightstar-acquires-20-20-mobile-to-expand-european-presence--984673)).
- **Solar — policy-driven demand whipsaws even the leaders:** Segen's turnover fell 36% in FY2013 (after the Dec 2011 tariff cut from 43.3p to 21p/kWh) and 54% in FY2016 (after the 64% cut announced Dec 2015), then fell from £349.9m to £213.0m over FY23–FY25 with cumulative operating losses of £30.4m and headcount cut from 198 to 118 (CH). Midsummer went from £17.3m operating profit (FY23) to a £0.65m loss (FY25). FIT timeline: [Wikipedia FIT UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feed-in_tariffs_in_the_United_Kingdom). Installer side: STA/PwC estimated 12,500 solar jobs lost in the year after the 2016 cuts; fewer than 1,400 MCS-accredited installers remained by early 2018 ([Solar Power Portal](https://solarpowerportal.co.uk/blogs/long_read_a_better_fit), [Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/cuts-solar-tariffs-could-destroy-sector/)).
- **What protected the survivors (from their own accounts):** a service layer (Hydraquip 49% gross margin, 60% of revenue from on-site vans; Pirtek franchisor 70% GM; Cleveland's hire and modification divisions), an own-brand/"like-for-like" range (HTS 35% GM; Weldability's own-brand consumables from the 1990s), and a hire fleet plus manufacturing (Portable Space 22% GM, 37 of 72 staff in manufacturing). Pure box-shifting shows 1–5% gross margins (Data Select 4.5% GP/sales 2021; Segen 1.4% FY24, 5.9% FY25; Midsummer 9.5% FY25) and is where the losses appeared.

## 7. The phone-shop and solar analogies, tested on timing

| Wave | Wave "year 0" | Who became the large supplier | Year they entered | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK cellular | Jan 1985 launch | Caudwell (Midland Mobile Phones, wholesale) | 1987 = **year 3** | £1bn by 2000; sold 2006 £1.46bn; retail arm bust 2014 |
| UK cellular | 1985 | Dunstone (Carphone Warehouse, retail) | 1989 = **year 5** | IPO 2000; merged Dixons 2014; stores closed 2020 |
| Pre-pay/3G handsets | ~1997–99 | Peter Jones (Data Select, distribution) | 1998 | £194m by 2011 at 2% margin; sold 2019; entity dissolved 2023 |
| UK solar FIT | Apr 2010 | Segen | Dec 2004 = **year −5** | 40% UK share; sold 2021; loss-making FY24–25 |
| UK solar FIT | 2010 | Midsummer | 2005 = **year −5** | £108m; founder-owned; loss FY25 |
| UK solar FIT | 2010 | Alternergy | 2006 = year −4 | Top-3 distributor ([Alternergy](https://www.alternergy.co.uk/blog/news-3/20-years-of-alternergy-from-one-solar-module-to-a-trusted-uk-solar-distributor-159)) |
| Container boom | 2020–22 | Cleveland Containers | 2011 = year −9 | £66m peak; LDC in |

So: in phones the "year 3–5 entrant becomes the supplier" story is literally true — but those entrants were building national distribution on borrowed stock at the very start of a 15-year demand explosion, and the *distribution* layer was later absorbed by global distributors (Brightstar, Westcoast) or killed by the operators. In solar, no year-3–5 entrant reached the top tier; the three leaders all pre-dated the subsidy by 4–6 years and had already passed £5m (Segen £7.8m FY May-2010) when the wave began. Someone starting a solar wholesaler in 2013–15 would have faced Segen at £54–84m turnover with an online portal and 30–40% share.

## 8. Conditions under which "becoming the supplier" worked

1. **Entered before or at the start of the demand wave, not in year 3–5** (Segen, Midsummer, Alternergy, Caudwell, Cleveland); the exception (Dunstone 1989) entered a retail gap, not distribution.
2. **Had a supplier agency or product access others lacked** (Data Select/Samsung; Hydraquip/Finning-Cat; Segen's manufacturer lines) — and paid for it later by being absorbed by the party that valued the agency.
3. **Added a service/manufacturing/hire layer** that lifted gross margin to 20–50% (Hydraquip, Pirtek, Portable Space, Cleveland Hire & Modular, HTS own-range) rather than trading at 1–5%.
4. **Took external capital at the £20–80m stage** (Cleveland/LDC; Ainscough/BoS; R&G/Scowcroft then Diploma; Marlowe/AIM) — organic-only firms topped out at £8–26m over 15–40 years.
5. **Bought from retiring owners in fragmented, service-heavy niches** (R&G: 14 deals in 5 years; Ainscough: 13 deals in 17 years).

## 9. Conditions under which it did not work

1. **Thin-margin box-shifting exposed to upstream power** — network operators (Phones 4u), manufacturers choosing their distributor (Data Select's fate hinged on Samsung), policy (FIT cuts).
2. **Late entry into a wave already owned by an online-portal incumbent** — no 2013–15 solar distributor entrant found in the top tier.
3. **Sub-scale one-yard/one-person operations** — Billie Box, Chris Beard APS, Jakespare, Bearing Traders: 13–40 years in, still micro. The ONS stock data say 68% of wholesalers are below £1m turnover.
4. **Being "the largest independent"** is a sale trigger, not an end-state: Cromwell, Hayley, Hydraquip, Segen, Weldability, R&G and Data Select were all bought by a corporate or PE consolidator within a few years of reaching that description.
5. **Cyclical over-expansion:** Cleveland's operating profit fell from £10.2m (FY22) to £2.0m (FY23) as container prices normalised; Segen and Midsummer swung to losses on stock devaluation after the 2023 boom.
