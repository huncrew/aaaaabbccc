# UK Small-Business Sale Market, £200k–£2m EV (as of September 2026)

Scope note: British spelling, GBP. Every figure is dated. "Dealsuite UK&I" = Dealsuite's semi-annual survey of UK & Ireland M&A advisers covering the "mid-market" (targets with £1m–£200m revenue), so its sub-£2.5m band is the closest published proxy for £200k–£2m EV deals. The BDO PCPI is gated (clients only from the 2025 edition) so no 2025–26 PCPI multiples could be verified. BusinessesForSale.com publishes no UK sold/listed ratio or time-to-sell index that I could find. Where a figure is unsourced or US-only it is flagged.

## Key Question 1: What multiple of adjusted profit does a £300k-EV UK business transact at in 2025–26, by sector?

### Takeaway
Advisor-reported data put a UK business with ~£200k EBITDA at about 3.1x–3.6x EBITDA in 2025–26 (Dealsuite), and independent transaction data put sub-£2.5m-EV deals at a median 3.6x–3.7x (MarktoMarket), versus a headline UK mid-market average of 5.4x and ~6.0x all-cap. Sector matters enormously: wholesale and retail sit at ~3.4x–3.5x, logistics ~4.0x, business services ~5.9x–6.3x, e-commerce ~6.4x, and software/IT services ~7.6x–8.2x — but those sector figures are mid-market averages, so a £300k-EV business should be expected to trade at a discount to them.

### Cited Findings

**Headline UK multiples, 2025–26**
- Dealsuite UK&I M&A Monitor H1 2026 (published 17 Aug 2026; 102 responses from 437 advisory firms, 23% response rate): average EBITDA multiple 5.4x, unchanged on H2 2025. Business Services 6.3x (+0.3). Software Development remained the highest-valued sector and IT Services second (both eased 0.2). Media & Communication and Construction & Engineering each +0.2; E-commerce −0.2; Agri & Food and Hospitality −0.1. — [Dealsuite UK&I M&A Monitor August 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-august-2026-copy)
- Same H1 2026 edition as summarised in Dealsuite's companion post: Software Development 8.0x, Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals 7.7x, IT Services 7.6x, Business Services 6.3x. — [Dealsuite, "Sellers' valuation expectations too high…" (17 Aug 2026)](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/sellers-valuation-expectations-too-high-in-nearly-half-of-uk-i-deal-processes)
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2025 (published Feb 2026; 106 responses from 435 firms): average 5.4x (up on H1 2025); Healthcare & Pharma 7.5x (from 7.6x); Agri & Food 5.2x (from 5.3x). — [Dealsuite UK&I M&A Monitor February 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-february-2026)
- Dealsuite UK&I H1 2025 (published Aug 2025; 104 responses from 433 firms): average 5.3x (from 5.35x in H2 2024); Business Services and Industrial & Manufacturing each +0.2. — [Dealsuite UK&I M&A Monitor August 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-august-2025)
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2024 (published Feb 2025; 107 responses from 431 firms): average 5.35x (from 5.2x in H1 2024); E-commerce & Webshops 6.6x; Agri & Food 5.5x. — [Dealsuite UK&I M&A Monitor February 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-february-2025)

**SDE / adjusted-profit framing for very small businesses**
- A UK valuation guide (last reviewed July 2026) states "Most UK owner-managed businesses transact between 3x and 8x adjusted EBITDA, with the sector setting the band". Its 12-sector, size-banded multiples table is not on the public page. — [businessvaluation.co.uk 2026 guide](https://www.businessvaluation.co.uk/2026-business-valuation-guide)

These pages were not individually fetched, so treat as indicative broker commentary rather than transaction data. — [Lansley Commercial](https://www.lansleycommercial.co.uk/insights/news/understanding-small-business-valuation-uk/); [YourCompanyFormations](https://www.yourcompanyformations.co.uk/blog/scale/how-to-value-a-business/)

**Search-fund / ETA buyers in the UK (relevant comparators for £1m–£10m deals, not £300k)**

### Inferences
- For a £300k-EV UK business (implying roughly £80k–£120k EBITDA/SDE at 2.5x–3.7x), the two independent datasets converge on ~3.5x EBITDA as the centre of gravity in H1 2026 (Dealsuite £200k-EBITDA band 3.6x; MarktoMarket nano-cap 3.7x), with the UK200Group median (4.5x) reflecting a somewhat larger, adviser-led SME sample.
- Applying Dealsuite's size discount (£200k EBITDA ≈ 0.67x the overall 5.4x average in H1 2026) to the H1 2025 sector table gives an indicative sub-£500k-EBITDA range of roughly 2.3x (wholesale/retail) to 4.2x (business services) to ~5x+ (software/IT). This is my derivation, not a published figure.
- Sector figures for "hire" and "agencies" are not separately reported by any UK index; hire sits inside Industrial/Business Services (5.3x–6.3x mid-market) and agencies inside Media & Communication (4.1x–4.3x mid-market) or Business Services.

### Gaps
- BDO PCPI/PEPI 2025–26 multiples: gated; not verifiable.
- Dealsuite H1 2026 and H2 2025 full sector tables (wholesale, retail, logistics, construction, industrial, e-commerce, media) are in the PDF charts, not the public text; only directional changes are published.
- No UK source publishes SDE multiples by sector from actual completions; UK data is almost all EBITDA-based and skewed to £1m+ revenue targets.
- MarktoMarket's micro-cap/small-cap bands and EV/revenue multiples are in the paid report only.

## Key Question 2: What percentage of small businesses listed in the UK sell within 12 months? Within 24? Median time to sell?

### Takeaway
No UK marketplace publishes a sold/listed ratio. The widely repeated "only ~20% of listed businesses sell" is unsourced broker folklore. The only hard UK numbers are: 71% of advisor-led sell-side mandates close (Dealsuite, H2 2024); 80% of advisor-guided UK&I sale processes finish in under 12 months (Dealsuite, H2 2025); and one UK broker's own book showing 53% of its sales completing within 12 months of listing (Intelligent, 2021). Broker consensus is 6–12 months from instruction to completion for a well-prepared business.

### Cited Findings
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2024: "71% of sell side assignments result in a closed deal"; 54% of deal terminations attributed to "unrealistic valuation expectations from the seller"; 15% of advisers cited unavailable financing. (Adviser-led, £1m+ revenue targets.) — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-february-2025)
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2025: "80% of sales processes guided by an M&A advisor had a duration of less than 12 months"; 46% of advisers reported an increase in the number of transactions. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-february-2026)
- Dealsuite European H2 2025: 66% of European processes under 12 months; UK&I, Southern Europe and the Nordics report the shortest sales processes. — [Dealsuite European Monitor March 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/european-monitor-march-2026)
- Dealsuite UK&I H1 2026: average of 8.5 "serious buyers" per company for sale (up from 7.9 in H1 2025); 47% of advisers reported more assignments; deals below £2.5m fell from 42% to 32% of the sample while deals above £10m rose from 15% to 19%. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor August 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-august-2026-copy)
- Intelligent (UK business brokerage), data for the six months to 24 June 2021: 53% of its businesses sold within 12 months of listing, 47% took longer; 26% of 2021 sales completed in 3–6 months; fastest 40 days. — [Intelligent, How long does it take to sell a business](https://www.intelligent.co.uk/guides/how-long-does-it-take-to-sell-a-business)
- Eddisons Business Sales (20 Oct 2025): "Most UK businesses take between six and twelve months to sell from initial instruction to completion"; phases: preparation 4–6 weeks, marketing 2–4 months, negotiation/DD 2–3 months, legals 4–8 weeks. No completion-rate data. — [Eddisons](https://www.eddisonsbusinesssales.com/insights/how-long-does-it-take-to-sell-a-business-in-the-uk)
- KBS Corporate (25 Sep 2026): "Most UK businesses typically take around 6–12 months to sell"; small businesses "within 3 to 9 months"; 12–18+ months for complex deals. No sources cited. — [KBS Corporate](https://www.kbscorporate.com/resources/insights/how-long-does-it-take-to-sell-a-business/)
- Exits.co.uk (updated 29 Jul 2026): "Six to twelve months from launch to completion, after three to twelve months of preparation". — [Exits.co.uk](https://www.exits.co.uk/2026-business-sale-exit-strategies)
- ExitRadar (2026): "Only around 20% of businesses listed with a broker complete a sale", attributed only to "industry research"; also claims "over 90% of small businesses fail to complete sales" (unsourced). — [ExitRadar UK business exit statistics](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-business-exit-statistics); NewOwner repeats "Only 20% of UK businesses that go to market actually sell" with no source. — [NewOwner](https://newowner.co.uk/blog/common-seller-mistakes-uk)
- Listing stock (site counts as of late Sept 2026): BusinessesForSale.com shows 14,856 UK businesses for sale, 1,257 in its "mid-market" category, 1,420 in London. — [BusinessesForSale.com UK search](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/businesses-for-sale); [mid-market](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/mid-market-businesses-for-sale); [London](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/businesses-for-sale-in-london)
- Broader UK deal volumes: ONS Q2 2026 (published 1 Sep 2026; covers only deals ≥£1m with change of control): 130 domestic UK-on-UK deals worth £4.2bn, 111 fewer transactions than Q2 2025. — [ONS M&A Q2 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/changestobusiness/mergersandacquisitions/bulletins/mergersandacquisitionsinvolvingukcompanies/latest). PitchBook (via Business Sale Report, 7 Jul 2026): 4,759 UK deals in 2025 (£257.1bn); 2,137 deals to 7 July 2026 (£178.9bn). — [Business Sale Report](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/industry-insights/uk-ma-value-on-track-to-reach-record-high-in-2026-229538). KPMG: 1,751 UK PE deals in 2025 (down ~10%), Business Services 45% of them (Feb 2026, via CT Acquisitions). — [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/uk-sme-succession-exit-2026/)

### Inferences
- Combining Dealsuite's 71% close rate with 80% of processes under 12 months implies roughly 55–60% of adviser-led UK&I mandates close within a year; the 24-month figure is not published but is likely to approach the 71% ceiling since few processes run beyond two years.
- For self-listed sub-£500k businesses on marketplaces (BusinessesForSale, Daltons, Bizdaq) the sell-through rate is almost certainly far lower than 71%, but there is no verifiable UK number — the "20%" claim should be labelled as unsourced.
- ONS domestic counts (130/quarter) capture only £1m+ deals and are irrelevant to the £200k–£2m market's true volume, which is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No UK source for percentage of listed businesses sold within 12 or 24 months (BusinessesForSale.com, Daltons, Bizdaq and Business Sale Report publish none).
- No median time-to-sell statistic from a current (2025–26) UK broker dataset; the only measured figure is Intelligent's 2021 book.
- Annual number of sub-£2m business transfers in the UK is not published by ONS, Companies House or BDO.

## Key Question 3: The "retiring owner" wave — share of UK SME owners over 55/60 planning to exit

### Takeaway
Age/succession is the single biggest UK sale motive (41% of UK&I sales in H2 2025; 46% in Europe), and Companies House data show 52.8% of UK companies have an average director age of 50+ and 23.8% of 60+. Survey evidence that "nearly a third" of owners plan to sell within five years / to fund retirement comes from 2023–24 studies (ThinCats/Ownership at Work; Mazars). I could not find current FSB, Rathbones, Lloyds or ONS/LSBS figures specifically on owner age and exit intent.

### Cited Findings
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2025: age/succession was the primary reason for selling in 41% of transactions. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-february-2026). Europe-wide H2 2025: 46%. — [Dealsuite European Monitor March 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/european-monitor-march-2026)
- ExitRadar analysis of the Companies House register (2026): 1,889,051 companies (52.8% of 3.6m) have an average director age of 50+; 852,756 (23.8%) 60+; 212,848 (5.9%) 70+; 2,154,332 (60.2%) have a single director; 173,807 sole directors aged 60+ with >£50k assets and no successor hold £198.9bn aggregate assets; 120,515 sole directors in their 70s hold £57.6bn. Also cites Companies House: 726,735 UK companies dissolved in 2024/25, a record (+9.6%). — [ExitRadar UK business exit statistics](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-business-exit-statistics)
- Ownership at Work / ThinCats "Generation EO" (Dec 2023): nearly a third of SME owners aged 43+ say they are likely to sell at least part of their stake within five years; ~58,000 businesses likely to see owner sales within 5 years and ~94,000 within 10 years across all exit routes. — [CT Acquisitions UK SME Succession Exit 2026 (citing the study)](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/uk-sme-succession-exit-2026/)
- Mazars survey (reported 14 Feb 2024): nearly one-third (30%) of UK business owners intend to sell their business to fund retirement; about two-fifths intend to put proceeds into pensions. No sample size or age split published. — [Business Matters](https://bmmagazine.co.uk/in-business/almost-a-third-of-uk-business-owners-to-sell-up-to-fund-retirement/)
- FSB Small Business Index Q1 2026: 35% of small firms expect "to close, shrink or sell up within a year"; index at −53; 87% cited rising costs. (Reported second-hand by Whito; the FSB page itself was not retrievable.) — [Whito UK business statistics 2026](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-business-statistics/)
- Evelyn Partners / Censuswide (Oct 2024): one in four UK owners of £5m+ turnover businesses fast-tracked exit plans in the prior 12 months; 23% of them cited CGT concerns. — [CT Acquisitions (citing Evelyn Partners)](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/uk-sme-succession-exit-2026/)
- Tax push factor: Business Asset Disposal Relief CGT rate rose from 10% (disposals to 5 Apr 2025) to 14% (6 Apr 2025–5 Apr 2026) to 18% (from 6 Apr 2026). — [GOV.UK BADR](https://www.gov.uk/business-asset-disposal-relief)
- Business population context: 5.7m UK private-sector businesses at start of 2025 (DBT BPE, Oct 2025), 4.3m with no employees; ONS recorded 280,375 business deaths in 2024 (9.8% death rate). — [Whito citing DBT/ONS](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-business-statistics/)
- British Business Bank Small Business Finance Markets 2025/26 (17 Mar 2026): gross SME bank lending +9% to £68bn in 2025; 314,000 start-ups in 2025; ~50% of smaller businesses used external finance in Q3 2025; challenger banks 60% of gross SME lending. No owner-age/succession data in the press release. — [British Business Bank press release](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/news-and-events/news/smaller-business-lending-markets-showing-signs-improvement-finds-latest-british-business-bank)
- An unsourced 2026 claim that "around a third of UK SME owners are now over 55" appears in CT Acquisitions commentary; I could not trace it to a primary survey. — [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/uk-sme-succession-exit-2026/)

### Inferences
- The director-age data (23.8% of companies with average director age 60+, 60% single-director) is the strongest hard evidence for a large supply of succession-driven sub-£2m sellers; the BADR increase to 18% from April 2026 likely pulled some 2025/26 exits forward and may temper 2026–27 supply.

### Gaps
- No Rathbones or Lloyds Bank owner-exit survey found via search.
- FSB: no dedicated succession report found; only the Q1 2026 SBI "35% close/shrink/sell" figure, reported second-hand.
- ONS/LSBS 2024 SME employers report (published 25 Sep 2025) does not surface owner-age or exit-intention statistics in its public summary.
- The BBB SBFM 2026 full PDF exceeded the fetch size limit; only press-release figures are cited.

## Key Question 4: How common is vendor/seller finance or deferred consideration in sub-£1m UK deals, and at what %?

### Takeaway
No UK source publishes a prevalence percentage for vendor finance in sub-£1m deals. The best evidence is directional: in H1 2025, 39% of UK&I advisers reported earn-outs becoming more frequent and 26% reported more vendor loans, and Dealsuite's H2 2025 report again notes an increase in deferred payments. Practitioner guidance for 2026 puts typical seller finance at ~20% of price (small deals, 2–3 years, with interest), with earn-outs of 10–25% and up to ~50% deferred in MBO/MBI structures.

### Cited Findings
- Dealsuite UK&I H1 2025: 39% of advisers reported a higher frequency of earn-outs; 26% reported increased use of vendor loans. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor August 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-august-2025)
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2025 mentions an "increase in the use of deferred payments" but gives no percentage. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/m-a-monitor-february-2026)
- WHN Solicitors (15 Jun 2026): deferred consideration "has become more prevalent in recent years and is now relevant, to some extent, to almost every company sale"; the deferred proportion "is typically higher in a management buyout". No numbers. — [East Lancashire Chamber / WHN](https://www.chamberelancs.co.uk/2026/06/deferred-consideration-a-key-ingredient-to-a-successful-business-sale-member-news/)
- Buy a Business Ltd guide (4 May 2026): seller finance "commonly structured over 2–3 years with interest"; worked example £120,000 deferred on a £300,000 deal over 3 years (40%); model £400,000 structure = £140,000 personal (35%) + £180,000 commercial loan (45%) + £80,000 seller finance (20%) over 3 years, plus £40–80k working capital and £5–15k professional fees. — [Buy a Business Ltd](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)
- Harper James (updated Jan 2020): in MBI/MBO deals "as much as 50% of the purchase price may be deferred and paid out gradually over 3–4 years"; vendor notes typically repayable over three to five years. — [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/vendor-deferred-loans/)
- Sprintlaw (22 Dec 2025): sellers offering finance "often look for at least 20–40% upfront"; no prevalence data. — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/seller-financing-a-business-in-the-uk-how-to-structure-and-protect/)
- Consult EFC (2026): "Public data does not give a clean UK percentage for earn-out use in 2024 to 2026"; earn-outs of 10–25% of total consideration are common where there is client concentration or owner dependence; most run one to three years. — [Consult EFC, Earn-outs in UK M&A 2026](https://consultefc.com/uk-earn-outs-deals/)
- Jonathan Lea Network (21 Jul 2026): "vendor finance is common in management buyouts and succession deals"; no statistics. — [Jonathan Lea](https://www.jonathanlea.net/blog/vendor-finance-vs-deferred-consideration/)
- UK search-fund commentary: seller notes of 10–25% of EV are usual (search-result summary, CT Acquisitions; not verified by full fetch). — [CT Acquisitions, search fund buyer](https://ctacquisitions.com/search-fund-buyer-vs-pe-buyer/)

### Inferences
- With ~40% of advisers reporting rising earn-out use and a quarter reporting rising vendor loans in the £1m+ revenue market, and legal practitioners calling deferred consideration near-universal, it is reasonable to assume a majority of sub-£1m UK deals now carry some deferred/vendor element; a 20–30% deferred slice over 2–3 years is the mode described in 2025–26 practitioner guidance.

### Gaps
- No Business Sale Report, Dealsuite or broker survey gives "% of sub-£1m deals with vendor finance" or the median % deferred; every figure above is a practitioner range, not measured transaction data.

## Key Question 5: What is the typical deposit / equity contribution a buyer puts in?

### Takeaway
UK practitioner guidance for 2026 says lenders expect the buyer to contribute 30–50% of price personally, with debt covering 50–70%, sized to at least 1.5x–2x debt service cover; seller finance of ~20% commonly fills the gap. The Growth Guarantee Scheme (70% government guarantee, £25,001–£2m, up to 6 years) is the main state-backed route for acquisition term loans.

### Cited Findings
- "Most lenders expect buyers to contribute 30–50% of the purchase price personally"; loans typically 50–70% of price; lenders require EBITDA to cover annual debt service 1.5x–2x; SME acquisition rates 6%–12%; GGS loans £25,001–£2m, up to 6 years, 70% guarantee to the lender. Example £400k deal: 35% personal / 45% loan / 20% seller finance. — [Buy a Business Ltd (4 May 2026)](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)
- Henderson Loggie (26 Jun 2026) illustrative £5m acquisition: 20% buyer cash, 50% bank loan, 20% mezzanine, 10% deferred consideration; no prescriptive percentages. — [Henderson Loggie](https://hlca.co.uk/resources/business-acquisition-financing/)
- Growth Guarantee Scheme: launched 1 Jul 2024; facilities £25,000–£2m; 70% guarantee; term loans 3 months–6 years; borrowers with turnover ≤£45m. — [British Business Bank GGS](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/finance-options/debt-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme)
- 100%-financed acquisitions are described as possible only for "high quality" targets using seller finance plus asset refinance (search-result summary of 2026 finance guides; not verified). — [UKTMS acquisition finance 2026](https://www.uktms.com/business-acquisition-finance-a-2026-guide-to-funding-uk-company-purchases/)
- UK search funds: 43 of 57 completed UK acquisitions (2021–26) had a named lender; predominantly specialist/challenger banks and private credit, not high-street banks. — [ExitRadar](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-eta-ecosystem)

### Inferences
- For a £300k deal the practitioner model implies roughly £90k–£150k buyer cash, £135k–£210k debt (GGS-backed unsecured/cash-flow loan) and £0–£60k vendor loan, before working capital and £5k–£15k fees.

### Gaps
- No lender or BBB data on the actual distribution of equity contributions or average GGS acquisition-loan size; the BBB SBFM 2026 PDF was too large to fetch.

## Key Question 6: How do asking prices compare with achieved prices?

### Takeaway
Dealsuite's H1 2026 survey found seller price expectations too high in 49% of UK&I processes, with an average 23% gap between expectation and achievable price; in 24% of those cases the gap killed the deal. No UK marketplace publishes asking-vs-sold data.

### Cited Findings
- Dealsuite UK&I H1 2026 (17 Aug 2026): 49% of transaction processes involve sellers whose valuation expectations are too high; average gap 23%; 24% of those cases result in deal collapse. — [Dealsuite](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/sellers-valuation-expectations-too-high-in-nearly-half-of-uk-i-deal-processes); also [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor August 2026](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-august-2026-copy)
- Dealsuite UK&I H2 2024: 54% of terminated deals attributed to unrealistic seller valuation expectations. — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-february-2025)
- NewOwner claims intermediary-sold businesses achieve 10–20% more than private sales and that broker fees run 2–5% of price — unsourced. — [NewOwner](https://newowner.co.uk/blog/common-seller-mistakes-uk)
- Zoppi (Oct 2023) cites broker fees of 5–10% of price, or "2.5%–5%" as more reasonable. — [Zoppi](https://www.zoppi.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-buy-a-business-uk)

### Inferences
- Half of UK sellers start ~23% above market; combined with the size discount, a buyer of a £300k-EV business should expect listed asking multiples (often SDE-based) to sit materially above the ~3.5x EBITDA that transactions clear at.

### Gaps
- No BusinessesForSale.com, Daltons or Bizdaq asking-vs-achieved index exists in the UK (unlike BizBuySell's US Insight Report).

## Key Question 7: Heads-of-terms-to-completion fall-through rates and typical DD/legal costs for a £250k–£450k deal

### Takeaway
No UK statistic measures fall-through after heads of terms specifically; the closest is Dealsuite's 71% close rate for adviser-led sell-side mandates (H2 2024) and its finding that valuation gaps kill 24% of affected deals (H1 2026). For a £250k–£450k acquisition, buyer legal fees are quoted at £8k–£20k + VAT (fixed-fee firms; £5k–£10k for a pragmatic scope) and financial due diligence at £8k–£20k, so £15k–£35k all-in is the documented range, with lender-imposed costs on top if debt-funded.

### Cited Findings
**Fall-through**
- 71% of UK&I sell-side assignments closed; 54% of terminations due to seller valuation expectations; 15% of advisers cited financing unavailability (H2 2024). — [Dealsuite UK&I Monitor February 2025](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/uk-i-m-a-monitor-february-2025)
- 24% of cases with a valuation gap collapse (H1 2026). — [Dealsuite](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/sellers-valuation-expectations-too-high-in-nearly-half-of-uk-i-deal-processes)
- US-only benchmark (not UK): roughly 65–75% of signed LOIs close; re-trades hit 30–40% of LOIs at 5–15% reductions. — [CT Acquisitions, why business sales fall through](https://ctacquisitions.com/why-business-sales-fall-through/)
- The ValuQ "sale fell through" page turned out to be about UK property (24% of agreed house sales collapsed in early 2026), not business sales — not applicable. — [ValuQ](https://valuq.co.uk/insights/my-sale-fell-through-what-do-i-do-now-uk-2026)

**Legal costs**
- Zoppi (6 Dec 2024): unlikely to find firms charging under £8,000 + VAT; deals under £2m "£12,000 + VAT to £20,000 + VAT"; £2m+ ~1% of price; packages for £1–2m deals: Basic £8k, Silver £15k, Gold £17k, Platinum £40k (+VAT). — [Zoppi, legal costs with real numbers](https://www.zoppi.co.uk/blog/understanding-legal-costs-for-buying-a-business-with-real-numbers)
- Zoppi (15 Oct 2023): legal fees 1–2% of sale value; due diligence 2–5% of transaction value; broker fees 5–10%. — [Zoppi, cost to buy a business](https://www.zoppi.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-buy-a-business-uk)
- UK Business Forums thread on a £350k purchase (Oct 2009, dated but illustrative): solicitor quotes £3–5k (minimal), £5–10k (pragmatic), £25k+ (comprehensive); one buyer paid ~£2,500 own legals but ~£12,000 for the bank's solicitor and £8,000 for the bank's accountant's report. — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/legal-fees-how-much-on-350k-business.129609/)

**Financial due diligence**
- Consult EFC (28 Jun 2026): UK SME FDD engagements "between £8,000 and £35,000"; deals under £10m EV typically £10,000–£20,000; 4–8 weeks (3 weeks expedited). No sub-£1m band given. — [Consult EFC](https://consultefc.com/financial-due-diligence-smes-uk-guide/)
- Buy a Business Ltd (May 2026): £5,000–£15,000 professional fees budgeted on a £400k deal. — [Buy a Business Ltd](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)

### Inferences
- A realistic all-in transaction budget for a £300k debt-funded UK acquisition is ~£20k–£35k (legals £8k–£15k, FDD £8k–£15k, lender fees/legals £2k–£5k), i.e. 7–12% of EV — materially higher as a percentage than for £1m+ deals.

### Gaps
- No UK dataset on heads-of-terms-to-completion rates for sub-£1m deals (Business Sale Report and BusinessesForSale.com publish none).
- No published FDD price band specifically for sub-£500k deals; the £8k floor is the lowest documented.
- Lender due-diligence/arrangement fees for GGS acquisition loans are not published.
