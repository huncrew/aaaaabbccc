# UK small-business sector economics and 2026 outlook (£200–450k EV targets)

Scope: (1) technical/industrial importer-distributor-wholesalers (SIC 46); (2) contracted B2B services — cleaning, fire safety/compliance, pest control, grounds maintenance, waste, facilities (SIC 81, 80, 38); (3) plant/tool/equipment hire (SIC 77); (4) small logistics — road freight, couriers, warehousing/3PL (SIC 49.41, 52, 53.20); (5) small SaaS / IT services / agencies (SIC 62, 73) as control. All figures GBP, UK, dated as at 28 September 2026 unless stated. Research-method note: web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so several trade-body pages (BPCA, IBISWorld UK detail pages, FIA, HAE, Pall-Ex/Palletways, Logistics UK) could not be opened; gaps are listed honestly under each question. Insolvency-by-SIC figures below were computed by me directly from the Insolvency Service's August 2026 Excel Table 1c (England & Wales, not seasonally adjusted, provisional) — 12-month windows are Sep–Aug sums of the monthly columns.

## Key Question 1: Which sector most reliably produces £60–150k EBITDA on £0.5–2m turnover with a manager in place and the owner in a selling seat?

### Takeaway
On the available benchmark data, professional/IT services and agencies have the highest median net margins (14–18%) but the thinnest evidence of owner-independence; contract cleaning shows the sharpest margin collapse as firms scale from owner-led (21% net under £1m) to managed (8.5% net at £1–5m); wholesale/distribution sits at a 5% median net margin with the lowest payroll intensity (16% of revenue), which is the structural reason it tolerates a salaried manager better than labour-heavy services. No source directly measures "EBITDA with a manager in place"; the inferences below are mine.

### Cited Findings
**Margin benchmarks (all sectors, £250k–£5m turnover, interquartile ranges, updated May 2026; sources stated as Companies House filed accounts, ONS Annual Business Survey, BCC SME data and 3DMAI platform data)**
- Wholesale/Distribution: gross margin 18–32% (median 24%); net margin 2–9% (median 5%); debtor days 35–65 (target <45); payroll 10–22% of revenue (median 16%); revenue per employee £180k–£420k (median £280k); stock cover 4–8 weeks. Import & Wholesale: gross margin 22–40%; stock cover 8–14 weeks — [3DMAI UK SME Benchmarks 2025/26](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)
- Professional Services: gross margin 52–76% (median 64%); net margin 12–26% (median 18%); debtor days 30–55; payroll 42–62% (median 52%) — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)
- SaaS/Software: gross margin 62–82% (median 73%); net margin 8–24% (median 16%); payroll 35–60% (median 48%) — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)
- Marketing/Digital Agency: gross margin 48–68% (median 58%); net margin 8–20% (median 14%); payroll 38–58% (median 48%) — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)
- Construction & Trade (proxy for grounds/fire-installation work): gross 22–40% (median 30%); net 3–12% (median 7%); debtor days 45–75 — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)

**Agencies / IT (control)**
- UK agencies over £1m revenue saw median gross profit margin fall below 40% for the first time on record, against a 50% target only ~24% hit (BenchPress 2025, The Wow Company); M&A advisers treat 20% EBITDA as the health benchmark, sub-15% as margin risk (FE International 2026); only 23% of UK agencies hold six-plus months' overhead in reserve (down from 29% in 2022); 33% made redundancies; staff turnover 24.1% in 2024 (IPA census) — [AgencyKit Agency Benchmarks 2026](https://agencykit.tech/agency-benchmarks-2026/)
- Most UK creative/digital agencies sell at 3.5–5x adjusted EBITDA in 2026 — [Alto Accounting](https://www.alto-accounting.com/insights/uk-marketing-agency-profit-margin-benchmarks)
- Small-firm valuation context: average multiple for firms with EBITDA c.£200k was 3.1x in H1 2025; retail/wholesale cluster at 3–4x — [Consult EFC / smebusinessvaluation.com](https://smebusinessvaluation.com/ebitda-multiples-uk-sme/)

**Manager salary (see KQ4) and payroll intensity feed the arithmetic below.**

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on the medians: a £1.5m wholesaler at the 5% median net margin yields ~£75k net (upper quartile 9% ≈ £135k); a £1.5m cleaning contractor in the £1–5m band at 8.5% ≈ £128k but the Growth Lab data shows the margin halves precisely when the owner stops doing the work; a £1m agency at 14–18% ≈ £140–180k. The Growth Lab and 3DMAI margins are stated as net/pre-tax, so add-backs (directors' pay, depreciation) would lift EBITDA — but whether those margins are already after an owner salary is not stated, so the "with a manager in place" adjustment cannot be quantified from published data.
- Payroll intensity is the discriminator for owner-independence: wholesale at ~16% of revenue can absorb a £47–60k manager (roughly 3–4 points of margin on £1.5m) without wiping out profit; cleaning and agencies at ~48–52% payroll are already labour-margin businesses where the manager's salary is a larger share of a thinner residual (cleaning) or where the owner is usually the rainmaker (agencies).
- Plant hire's 7.8% "profit" figure is an industry EBIT-type margin after heavy depreciation; EBITDA for a small hirer is structurally much higher but so is replacement capex, so EBITDA overstates free cash flow — a buyer should underwrite on EBITDA less maintenance capex.
- Haulage's ~2% average margin makes it the least reliable route to £60–150k on sub-£2m turnover; courier/last-mile subcontracting to networks is similarly thin.
- Combining margin reliability and manager-tolerance, technical wholesale/distribution is the most plausible "manager runs it, owner sells" candidate at this turnover, with contracted compliance services (fire, pest) next — but the latter's small-firm margins are not published (see gaps).

### Gaps
- No filed-accounts study found for pest control, fire safety servicing, grounds maintenance or small 3PL margins at £0.5–2m turnover; IBISWorld margin figures for cleaning, couriers and pest control were behind HTTP 405/403 on direct fetch.
- Companies House worked examples: I could not identify specific £0.5–2m firms filing full P&L accounts. A test company in SIC 81210 (Angel Cleaning & Maintenance Ltd, 09911311) had only micro-entity accounts and nothing filed since Dec 2021 — [Companies House filing history](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09911311/filing-history). Structural reason: small companies may currently omit the P&L; under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act reforms, abridged accounts are abolished and small and micro companies must file a profit and loss account from 1 April 2028, with software-only iXBRL filing from the same date — [Companies House: changes to accounts](https://changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk/changes-to-accounts/). Until then, gross-margin evidence for this size band comes from aggregators (Plimsoll, 3DMAI) or the buyer's own due-diligence access to management accounts.
- Plimsoll UK sector pages were not accessible without subscription; the only Plimsoll-derived figure is the 1,871-firm FM sample cited by Growth Lab.

## Key Question 2: Which sector has the lowest 3-year insolvency/closure rate for small firms in 2023–2026?

### Takeaway
Measured by formal insolvencies, the in-scope SIC groups are all low in absolute terms and most fell in the 12 months to August 2026 (economy-wide rate 50.1 per 10,000 companies, i.e. ~0.5% a year); road freight is the standout deteriorating-then-recovering group (501 → 407 insolvencies 2023→2025) and transport & storage has the worst business-death and five-year-survival record on ONS demography, while cleaning insolvencies have been flat at ~170 a year and machinery hire, though small in number, rose ~20% in the latest year.

### Inferences
- Rough insolvency incidence (E&W insolvencies 12m to Aug 2026 ÷ UK active enterprises 2024 — imperfect geography and denominators): wholesale div 46 ≈ 1,031/112,000 ≈ 0.9%/yr; transport & storage ≈ 693/138,000 ≈ 0.5%/yr; construction ≈ 0.9%/yr; professional/scientific/technical ≈ 0.4%/yr. At these rates a three-year cumulative formal-insolvency risk is roughly 1.2–2.7% — low everywhere; the far bigger closure risk in transport is non-insolvency deregistration (16.6% death rate), i.e. owner-drivers and micro hauliers simply stopping.
- Ranking by formal insolvency trend 2023–26: cleaning (flat), warehousing/3PL and couriers (falling), wholesale (falling in 2026 after a 2025 rise), computer consultancy (falling), road freight (falling fast from a 2023 peak), advertising (rising), machinery hire (rising off a small base, consistent with the CPA construction downturn), security-systems servicing (rising off a small base).
- The "lowest closure rate" answer differs by measure: on survival, waste collection (48.6%) and the wider cleaning/hygiene/waste industry (44.0%) beat the economy; on insolvencies, all in-scope service groups except 77.3 and 80.2 improved in the latest year; transport & storage is the worst on survival despite improving insolvencies.

### Gaps
- No official insolvency *rate* by SIC (Insolvency Service publishes counts only); the ratios above are my approximations with mismatched E&W/UK bases.
- ONS five-year survival by industry percentages (other than transport & storage being lowest) are in the dataset download, not the bulletin; not retrieved.
- Red Flag Alert did not publish Industrial Transportation & Logistics or Wholesale figures in the Q2 2026 RNS.

## Key Question 3: What are the wage-cost and regulatory headwinds from April 2026 for labour-heavy services vs distribution?

### Takeaway
From April 2026 the National Living Wage is £12.71 (+4.1%) with the 18–20 rate up 8.5% to £10.85, on top of employer NIC at 15% above a £5,000 threshold (Employment Allowance £10,500) and SSP payable from day one at £123.25 or 80% of earnings; these bite hardest where payroll is ~50% of revenue (cleaning, security, agencies) and least in wholesale where payroll is ~16% — but wholesalers instead face a −24% CBI wholesale volume balance, energy-price concern (64% of businesses) and Middle East-driven oil/product inflation.

### Cited Findings
**Wage floor**
- NLW £12.71/hr from April 2026 (from £12.21, +4.1%); 18–20 rate £10.85 (from £10.00, +8.5%); 16–17 and apprentice £8.00 (+6.0%); a 37.5-hour NLW worker gains £977 gross a year; LPC plans to extend NLW to 20-year-olds in 2027 and 18–19s in 2028–29 "contingent on economic conditions"; LPC chair: recent NLW increases "have not had a significant negative impact on jobs" — [Personnel Today, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/national-living-wage-2026-2027-nlw-nmw-lpc/)
- 40 hours at NLW (Apr 2026) = £508.40/week gross — [Xero NLW guide, updated 21 Jul 2026](https://www.xero.com/uk/guides/national-living-wage/)
- Accommodation offset £11.10/day from 1 Apr 2026 — [DavidsonMorris, 23 Jan 2026](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/statutory-minimum-wage-rates-april-2026/)

**Employer NIC and statutory pay 2026–27 (gov.uk, updated 1 Sep 2026)**
- Employer (secondary) Class 1 NIC 15% on earnings above the secondary threshold of £96/week, £417/month, £5,000/year; Employment Allowance £10,500; SSP "£123.25 or 80% of the employee's average weekly earnings, whichever is lower"; apprenticeship levy 0.5% only on pay bills over £3m — [HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027)
- SSP weekly rate £123.25 from 6 Apr 2026 (from £118.75), with removal of the lower earnings limit so those earning under £129/week receive the lower of the SSP rate or 80% of average earnings; statutory family pay £194.32/week — [DavidsonMorris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/statutory-minimum-wage-rates-april-2026/)
- 3DMAI summary of the cost squeeze: NLW £12.21 (Apr 2025), energy ~70% above 2021/22, employer NI 15% with £5,000 threshold, rising debtor days — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)

**How the headwinds land by sector**
- Cleaning: employment cost per cleaner £31,000 p.a.; industry pre-tax margin 4.0% — [Growth Lab FM](https://thegrowthlabfm.substack.com/p/only-4-of-uk-cleaning-companies-achieve). Average hourly pay in SIC 81.2 cleaning activities rose from £9.81 to £12.00 between the report's base years (+7.9% latest year) — [BCC Research Report 2024](https://www.bics.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/BCC-Research-Document-2024-final.pdf)
- Pest control: "profit is coming under pressure from the April 2025 rise in the National Living Wage [and] employers' National Insurance Contributions. Higher spending on compliance is also pushing profit down" — [IBISWorld UK pest control](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/market-size/pest-control/4285/)
- Payroll intensity: wholesale 10–22% of revenue (median 16%) vs professional services 42–62% (52%), agencies 38–58% (48%), SaaS 35–60% (48%) — [3DMAI](https://www.3dmai.co.uk/uk-sme-benchmarks-2026)
- Haulage: driver shortage — average driver age ~51, about half the workforce over 50, ~40,000 new drivers a year needed for five years; 24% of haulage firms had driver vacancies in Q4 2024 (28% in Q3); fuel about a third of opex — [Motive, 25 Mar 2026](https://gomotive.com/blog/uk-haulage-industry-crisis-operators-going-bust-uk/); HGV fleet running costs +~10% in 2024 — [trans.info, 15 Oct 2025](https://trans.info/en/uk-haulage-crisis-428175)
- Economy-wide cost pressure (ONS BICS wave 164, 7–20 Sep 2026, published 24 Sep 2026): cost of labour was the most reported challenge for businesses with 10+ employees (37%); 64% of businesses reported some concern about energy prices, citing Middle East conflict; 29% reported higher input prices in August 2026; 11% raised selling prices; 17% expect to raise prices in October — [ONS BICS latest](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/bulletins/businessinsightsandimpactontheukeconomy/latest)
- Distribution demand: CBI Distributive Trades, 25 Sep 2026 — wholesale sales volumes −24% year-on-year balance in September (from −13% in August), expected −23% in October; retail sales fell steeply, online retail dropping at its fastest pace in nearly three years, retailers cutting orders at record levels since 1983 — [CBI DTS September 2026 via WiredGov](https://wired-gov.net/wg/news.nsf/articles/Retail+conditions+deteriorate+further+in+September+2026+CBI+Distributive+Trades+Survey+25092026121500?open=); headline reading −55 vs −48 prior — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/cbi-distributive-trades)
- Construction input inflation: CPA cites "double-digit construction product price inflation, particularly for oil-based and energy-intensive products" and mortgage-rate rises (Spring 2026 forecast) — [Tile Association / CPA, 18 May 2026](https://www.tiles.org.uk/construction-activity-to-fall-2-5-in-2026-and-rise-1-2-in-2027-says-cpa/)

**Contract and supplier-termination risk (distribution)**
- Initial terms in UK distribution agreements are "often 2–3 years"; termination for convenience typically 3–12 months' notice; 30 days' written notice also common — [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/exclusive-distribution-agreement-key-clauses-and-legal-tips/), [Sprintlaw distribution norms](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/distribution-agreements-types-clauses-uk-norms/)
- Where no fixed term/notice, either party may terminate on "reasonable notice" (factors: relationship length, distributor investment, share of distributor's turnover); distributors have no statutory compensation on termination (unlike commercial agents under the 1993 Regulations); under the UK Vertical Agreements Block Exemption Order exclusivity is protected where both parties' market shares are below 30%; non-compete/80%-sourcing obligations capped at five years; suppliers may only set maximum/recommended resale prices — [CMS Expert Guide to Distribution: England & Wales](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-distribution/england-and-wales)

### Inferences
- A 4.1% NLW rise on a £31k-per-cleaner cost base adds roughly £1,000–1,300 per FTE including on-costs; on a £1.5m cleaning contractor with ~35–45 FTE that is £40–55k a year to recover through price — more than the whole net profit at the 4% industry average unless passed through. The same rise on a wholesaler with 5 staff mostly above NLW is immaterial.
- The 8.5% jump in the 18–20 rate and planned NLW extension to 20-year-olds in 2027 disproportionately hit grounds maintenance, cleaning and warehouse roles that employ younger staff.
- The distribution headwind is demand and FX/energy-driven rather than payroll-driven: the CBI wholesale balance of −24% is the weakest cited in this research and coincides with the retail-order collapse, so 2026–27 volume risk sits with the distributor, not its wage bill.
- The legal asymmetry (no statutory compensation for distributors; 3–12 months' convenience termination) means the exclusive agency that underpins a distributor's valuation can be removed within a year — buyers should treat agreement term and change-of-control clauses as the primary diligence item.

## Key Question 4: Typical 2026 salary for a UK operations/general manager of a £1m-turnover business?

### Takeaway
Job-ad data for September 2026 puts a UK operations manager at £46,709 (Indeed, range £31k–£70k) or £60,528 (Reed, range £56.6k–£66.4k), against an all-employee full-time median of £39,039 (ASHE April 2025); for a £1m-turnover firm the Indeed figure is the more relevant anchor because Reed's sample skews to larger employers and city-centre roles.

### Cited Findings
- Indeed UK: average operations manager salary £46,709 p.a. (8,800 salaries from job postings in past 36 months; last updated 7 Sep 2026); typical range £31,070–£70,220; senior operations manager £60,446; assistant/junior operations manager £34,152 / £33,040; London £49,047 — [Indeed UK operations manager salaries](https://uk.indeed.com/career/operations-manager/salaries), [Indeed London](https://uk.indeed.com/career/operations-manager/salaries/London)
- Reed.co.uk: average operations manager salary £60,528 (range £56,569–£66,365; 2,148 jobs; date not stated on page; regional outliers such as Manchester £121,175 reflect senior/corporate listings) — [Reed average operations manager salary](https://www.reed.co.uk/average-salary/average-operations-manager-salary)
- ONS ASHE April 2025 (released 23 Oct 2025): median full-time gross annual pay £39,039 (+4.3%); median weekly £766.60 (+5.3%); "managers, directors and senior officials" median full-time weekly earnings rose 6.7% April 2024–April 2025 — [ONS ASHE 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/bulletins/annualsurveyofhoursandearnings/2025)
- Xero Small Business Insights UK, June quarter 2026 (published 30 Jul 2026): small-business wages growth 2.8% y/y; jobs growth 1.7%; sales growth 3.6% — [Xero SBI UK latest](https://www.xero.com/uk/resources/small-business-insights/latest-united-kingdom/)

### Inferences
- Fully loaded cost of a £47k operations manager in 2026–27: employer NIC 15% × (£47,000 − £5,000) ≈ £6,300, plus 3% auto-enrolment ≈ £1,400, i.e. roughly £55k before car/bonus — about 3.7 points of margin on £1.5m turnover or 5.5 points on £1m. This is the hurdle any "owner in a selling seat" model must clear.
- Regional and sector variation is wide: a depot or contracts manager in cleaning/logistics outside London will sit toward the Indeed lower-middle range; an agency or IT delivery lead toward the Reed figure.

### Gaps
- ONS ASHE occupation-level annual median for SOC "production/operations managers" was not retrieved (bulletin gives only the growth rate); Glassdoor and Indeed general-manager pages returned HTTP 403.
