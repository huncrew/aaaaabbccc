# Build-from-zero base rates, UK (as of September 2026)

Scope: empirical base rates for starting (A) a multi-channel sourced-product ecommerce business (own site + Amazon + eBay + Etsy), (B) a B2B services start-up with hired staff (cleaning, grounds, compliance), and (C) a small B2B distribution/wholesale start-up. All figures in GBP unless the source is USD; US/global data flagged. Every figure traces to a URL; sample and date given where the source states them. Web-search quota was exhausted part-way through, so several sources (Amazon DSA transparency report, BizBuySell, Etsy fee page, FSB surveys, Jungle Scout 2025/2026 report PDFs) could not be reached and are listed as gaps.

Source-quality legend used below: [ONS/HMRC/CH] = UK official statistics; [Broker] = marketplace/broker transaction data (self-selected sample); [Survey] = seller survey; [Aggregator] = secondary compilation, lower confidence; [Author estimate] = guide-article numbers with no stated sample.

## KQ1. Of 100 UK people who start a multi-channel ecommerce business in 2027 with £30–60k, how many are still trading in 2030, and how many pay themselves ≥ £70k?

### Takeaway
ONS cohort data for SIC 479 (retail not in stores/stalls/markets, which contains 47.91 online retail) show only 34–37% of VAT/PAYE-registered online-retail start-ups survive three years, the second-worst survival of any 3-digit SIC group examined, versus 53–56% for all businesses. No UK dataset links survival to owner pay, but HMRC data show just 5.2% of all self-employed individuals report profit ≥ £50k and 1.9% ≥ £100k (3.8% and 0.7% respectively in wholesale/retail), so the ≥ £70k outcome after three years is a low-single-digit share of starters.

**Business population context** [DBT]
- 5.69 million UK private-sector businesses at start of 2025; 4.27m (75%) with no employees; 1.42m (25%) employers; 3.2m sole proprietorships (57%), 2.1m companies (37%); wholesale & retail 547,000 businesses — [Business Population Estimates 2025 (published 2 Oct 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release)

### Inferences
- Still trading in 2030 (three years after a 2027 start): the ONS SIC-479 3-year survival of 34–37% (three cohorts 2019–2021) is the best UK anchor, giving roughly **35 of 100** for VAT/PAYE-registered starters. Because the IDBR only captures businesses that register for VAT (turnover ≥ £90k) or PAYE, hobby-scale sellers that never register are excluded; among all 100 starters including those, the true 3-year figure is likely lower than 35. Companies House cohorts (all Ltd companies, including dormant) show 39–44% of 2022–2023 incorporations still on the effective register after 2.25–4.25 years, broadly consistent.
- Paying themselves ≥ £70k: no direct UK measurement exists. Combining (i) 3-year survival ≈ 35%, (ii) HMRC: 5.2% of all self-employed and 3.8% of wholesale/retail self-employed have profit ≥ £50k, 1.9%/0.7% ≥ £100k (so ≥ £70k is roughly 2–3% for all self-employed, ~1.5–2.5% for retail, by interpolation), and (iii) Jungle Scout's finding that only ~16% of Amazon sellers exceed $10k monthly sales (and margins median 20%), the ≥ £70k-profit outcome at three years is plausibly **2–5 of 100 starters**, and could be below 2 if the retail-specific HMRC distribution is used. The £30–60k capital assumption is above the Jungle Scout median starting capital (64% start with < $5,000), which may shift the odds up somewhat, but Jungle Scout does not publish profit by starting capital.
- Jungle Scout's "58% profitable within a year" and "87% profitable" are survivor-biased (only current sellers are surveyed) and treat any positive margin as "profitable", not owner pay.

### Gaps
- No UK source links survival to owner earnings for new businesses; the HMRC distribution covers all self-employed (any age of business) and excludes owner-directors paid via salary/dividends.
- Jungle Scout State of the Amazon Seller 2025 and 2026 PDFs could not be retrieved (report landing pages contain no data); figures above are from the survey fielded Dec 2023–Jan 2024. Neither Shopify nor eBay publishes merchant income distributions; no UK-specific seller-income survey was found.
- ONS does not publish 4-digit SIC survival, so 47.91 cannot be separated from the rest of group 479 (which also includes mail-order and market-stall-free home shopping).

## KQ2. Same question for a B2B services start-up (cleaning, grounds, compliance) with hired staff

### Takeaway
Registered cleaning start-ups (SIC 812) survive three years at 58–68% and five years at 47.5%, and landscape/grounds start-ups (SIC 813) at 68–74% (3-yr) and 59.4% (5-yr) — roughly double the online-retail rate and well above the UK average. There is no UK data on the share reaching £70k owner profit; the HMRC self-employment distribution (5.2% ≥ £50k across all sectors) and the fact that only 25% of UK businesses ever employ anyone are the only quantitative anchors.

- Staff cost anchor (author estimate, updated Mar 2026): a cleaner at £12.50/h for 25 h/week costs ≈ £18,500–19,000/yr including 13.8% employer NI, 3% pension and 5.6 weeks' holiday; a single office contract is worth £400–1,200/month — [Startup Costs UK](https://startupcosts.co.uk/articles/how-much-to-start-a-cleaning-business-uk) [Author estimate]
- Franchise resale/earnings claims on the market: Maid2Clean franchise "breakeven 12 months"; Bright & Beautiful "earning potential up to £80,000" — [BusinessesForSale UK cleaning listings](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/commercial-cleaning-businesses-for-sale) [Vendor claims]

### Inferences
- Still trading in 2030: ONS cleaning 3-year survival 58–68% (three cohorts) gives roughly **60 of 100** registered cleaning starters; grounds/landscape ≈ 68–74 of 100. Because a staffed cleaning business must run PAYE from day one, IDBR coverage is closer to complete than for ecommerce, so this estimate is less upward-biased than KQ1's.
- ≥ £70k owner profit: applying the HMRC ceiling (≈ 2–3% of all self-employed at ≥ £70k; a services business with staff is more likely to be a limited company, which HMRC's SA tables exclude) against 60% survival does not produce a defensible point estimate; the evidence supports only "low single digits to perhaps 5–8 of 100", the upper end resting on the fact that employer businesses (25% of the population) skew larger. Reaching £70k profit at a plausible 15–20% net margin implies £350–470k turnover, i.e. 25–100 office contracts at the £400–1,200/month benchmark — a scale most registered cleaning firms never reach (the BusinessesForSale listings show established firms with £18k–£381k turnover).

### Gaps
- No UK survey of cleaning/grounds/compliance start-ups reports owner profit distribution, time to profit, or starting capital; all "£2k–£8k to start, break-even in 3–6 months" claims are guide-article estimates without samples.
- FSB, Enterprise Nation and StartUp Britain new-business income surveys were not reachable after the search quota ended.
- Companies House micro-entity accounts do not expose profit, so no profit distribution for small Ltds exists in official statistics.

## KQ4. Capital required to reach ~£30k and ~£70k owner profit (stock, fit-out, marketing, working capital)

### Takeaway
No survey ties starting capital to owner-profit outcomes. The only distributional data is Jungle Scout's (US-weighted) starting-capital buckets, where 64% of Amazon sellers start with under $5,000; UK services guides quote £2k–£8k for a commercial cleaning launch but these are author estimates, not survey data.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon seller starting investment (Jungle Scout survey, Dec 2023–Jan 2024): < $500 16%; $500–1,000 9%; $1,001–2,500 16%; $2,501–5,000 23%; $5,001–10,000 18%; > $10,000 17%; "most sellers (64%) spent less than $5,000 to get started" and 25% < $1,000 — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/how-much-do-amazon-sellers-make/) [Survey; global/US-weighted]

### Inferences
- The £30–60k budget in the brief exceeds what 83% of Amazon sellers start with, so the relevant comparator is the top ~17% capital bucket, whose owners also report the heaviest hours.
- For cleaning, launch capital is trivial relative to the £30–70k profit target; the binding constraint is working capital to carry payroll ahead of 30–60-day B2B invoices, for which no survey figure was found.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies capital required to reach £30k or £70k owner profit for any of the three build types; this must be modelled rather than cited.

## KQ5. Median time to first profit and to £50k+ profit, and the distribution

### Takeaway
Only Amazon-seller survey data exist: 58% of surviving sellers report profitability within 12 months, but 47% report under $25k lifetime profit and only 30% have ever cleared $50k lifetime profit. No UK source measures time to a £50k profit run-rate.

### Cited Findings
- Time to profitability (Jungle Scout, ~2,000 sellers, Dec 2023–Jan 2024): within 3 months 22%; 3–6 months 16%; 6–12 months 20%; 1–2 years 11%; > 2 years 2%; don't know 7%; 13% not yet profitable — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/how-much-do-amazon-sellers-make/) [Survey]
- Lifetime profits: < $25k 47%; $25–50k 10%; $50–100k 6%; $100–500k 10%; $500k–1m 8%; $1–5m 5%; $5–10m 1% — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/how-much-do-amazon-sellers-make/)
- Profit margins: 1–5% 12%; 6–10% 16%; 11–15% 13%; 16–20% 15%; 21–25% 15%; 26–50% 13%; 51–100% 1%; "28% see profit margins above 20%" — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/how-much-do-amazon-sellers-make/)
- Etsy: "time to first sale 30 to 60 days; time to consistent income three to six months" — [Printify citing Sales Doe](https://printify.com/blog/how-much-can-you-make-on-etsy/) [Aggregator; no sample]
- Cleaning: "break even within 3 to 6 months" for a £3k–8k commercial launch — [Startup Costs UK](https://startupcosts.co.uk/articles/how-much-to-start-a-cleaning-business-uk) [Author estimate]

### Inferences
- Median time to first (any) profit among surviving Amazon sellers is roughly 6 months (cumulative 38% by 6 months, 58% by 12 months). Time to £50k cumulative profit: with 47% under $25k lifetime and the median survey respondent having sold for more than a year, the median seller never reaches £50k cumulative profit, let alone a £50k annual run-rate.

### Gaps
- No UK data on time to profit for services or distribution start-ups; no data anywhere on time to a £50k+ annual owner profit.

UK owner-hours surveys give means of 39–51 h/week (Tide 2026: 38h57m, n=500; Enterprise Nation 2022: 51 h; Opus Energy 2019: 45.5 h) with no year-1 vs year-3 split; Amazon sellers report far lower hours (50% ≤ 10 h/week), but heavily capitalised sellers (≥ $10k) mostly work > 20 h/week.

- Median year-one owner hours: for a full-time UK owner-operator, the surveys point to ~40–46 h/week median (Tide's distribution has 42% between 30 and 48 h). For a £30–60k-capitalised ecommerce start-up, the Jungle Scout ≥ $10k cohort implies > 20 h/week for most, i.e. a median plausibly in the 20–40 h band rather than the 4–10 h band of the typical Amazon side-seller.

### Gaps
- No survey splits owner hours by business age; no UK survey of new ecommerce or new cleaning/grounds owners' hours was found.

### Takeaway
Amazon's third-party sellers account for ~69% of Amazon GMV and the top 1.6% of sellers drive half of 3P GMV; Etsy's marketplace seller count fell in 2025 and UK revenue has declined two years running while its take rate reached 24.2%; online is 27–28% of UK retail. No primary source on Amazon account-suspension rates could be reached.

## KQ8. Resale value of a 2–3-year-old ecommerce or services business

### Takeaway
Broker data put small ecommerce businesses at ~1.4–1.9x annual profit (Flippa average 1.4x in 2025, 1.55x in H1 2026; Empire Flippers sub-$300k deals averaged $91k at 22.4x monthly profit) and services businesses at 1.2x on Flippa; only ~2% of businesses submitted to Empire Flippers were sold in 2025, and the average sold business was 5.7 years old. UK cleaning listings ask roughly 1.4–1.5x net profit but no sold-price data exists.

### Cited Findings
- Flippa 2025 recap (Jan–Dec 2025): average profit multiples — SaaS 2.7x (top quartile 5.8x), Content 2.6x (5.5x), Marketplace 2.5x, Apps 2.4x, YouTube 1.8x, **Ecommerce 1.4x (top quartile 2.7x), Services 1.2x (top quartile 2.1x)**; average transaction size $72,500 in the $50–100k band, $323,000 in $100–500k; 85% of deals cross-border; 37% of buyers repeat acquirers — [Flippa, 2025 Online Business M&A Insights](https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/) [Broker]

- Empire Flippers general guidance: "for any digital business, you will be looking at selling the business 2x to 5x your annual SDE or EBITDA... - UK cleaning businesses for sale (BusinessesForSale.com, Sep 2026): 373 listings. Examples with both price and profit: automotive detailing, Portsmouth — asking £95,000, turnover £363,000, net profit £63,000 (1.5x); premium cleaning, Cheltenham — asking £49,500, turnover £289,000, net profit £35,000 (1.4x); online cleaning businesses asking £9,950–9,995 on £100–200k turnover; Herefordshire commercial cleaning asking £29,000 on £37,136 turnover; Surrey relocatable asking £59,950 on £210,426 turnover — [BusinessesForSale UK](https://uk.businessesforsale.com/uk/search/commercial-cleaning-businesses-for-sale) [Asking prices, not sold prices; ages mostly 9–25 years]

So a business earning £50k profit at year three might list at roughly £70–95k and sell, if it sells at all, with ~30% deferred; Flippa's services average of 1.2x implies £60k for a £50k-profit cleaning business, consistent with the 1.4–1.5x asking multiples seen on BusinessesForSale.

### Gaps
- No UK sold-price dataset for small services businesses (BizBuySell, US, returned 403; BusinessesForSale does not publish sold prices). Empire Flippers' minimum listing criteria (profit and age thresholds) were not in the extracted text.
- Flippa does not publish sell-through rates or median (vs average) sale prices in the pages retrieved.

## KQ9. UK evidence on how many new businesses reach £70k+ owner profit at all

### Takeaway
No dataset measures owner profit by business age. The best proxies: HMRC shows 5.2% of self-employed individuals with profit ≥ £50k and 1.9% ≥ £100k in 2023-24; 45.8% report under £10k; 75% of UK businesses have no employees; and only 4.9% of employer businesses with 10+ staff are "high-growth".

### Cited Findings
- HMRC Table 3.10 (2023-24): 5.34m individuals with self-employment income; 276,000 (5.2%) at ≥ £50k; 104,000 (1.9%) at ≥ £100k; 2,446,000 (45.8%) under £10k; 633,000 (11.9%) with nil profit — [HMRC](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69f1f12d2fae53a03709682f/Collated_Tables_3_1_to_3_11_2324.ods)
- Mean self-employment income of SE taxpayers £29,300 (Table 3.6, taxpayers only; 3.67m individuals, £107bn) — [HMRC Table 3.6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69f1f12d2fae53a03709682f/Collated_Tables_3_1_to_3_11_2324.ods)
- Self-employment share of total income is lowest (4.0%) in the £70,000–100,000 total-income band and 35.2% above £1m — [HMRC Table 3.6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69f1f12d2fae53a03709682f/Collated_Tables_3_1_to_3_11_2324.ods)
- 14,330 high-growth businesses in 2024, a high-growth rate of 4.9% of businesses with 10+ employees — [ONS bulletin 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/businessdemography/2024)
- 4.27m of 5.69m UK businesses (75%) have no employees; 1.42m employers — [BPE 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release)
- Around one-fifth of all UK taxpayers (7.28m, 19.8%) have total income over £50,000 — [HMRC commentary 2023-24](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/personal-incomes-statistics-for-the-tax-year-2023-to-2024/personal-incomes-statistics-2023-to-2024-commentary)
- Earlier ONS/HMRC work: median SA self-employment income £8,788 (TYE 2018) — [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/articles/surveyandadministrativedatacomparisonsofselfemploymentincomeuk/taxyearsending2016to2018)

### Inferences
- The £70k owner-profit threshold sits inside HMRC's £50k–100k band; interpolating a declining density gives roughly 2–3% of all self-employed. Because a limited-company owner-director drawing £70k in salary plus dividends is not captured here, the true share of all business owners at £70k+ could be higher, but the IFS paper that would quantify this (owner-manager income distribution) returned 403.

### Gaps
- Companies House micro-entity accounts do not disclose profit; no official profit distribution for small limited companies exists.
- IFS "New trends in self-employment and top incomes" (owner-manager income) could not be fetched (403); FSB and Enterprise Nation surveys on new-business owner pay could not be searched after the quota ended.
- No UK data on owner profit by business age, so "how many *new* businesses reach £70k" remains unmeasured; the HMRC all-age distribution is an upper bound for 3-year-old businesses.
