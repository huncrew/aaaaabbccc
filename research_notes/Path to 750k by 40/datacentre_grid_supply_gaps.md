# Data-centre / AI-compute and electricity-grid build-out: supply lines and service niches a UK sole founder can actually reach (as of 29 September 2026)

Scope and method. Demand signals (Part A) come from primary reports and trade press dated 2025–26. Import lines (Part B) were pulled on 29 Sep 2026 from the HMRC UK Trade Info OData API (`https://api.uktradeinfo.com`), imports = FlowTypeId 1 (EU) + 3 (non-EU), GBP, Jan 2023 to latest month 202607 (July 2026), 27 HS4 headings, 108 aggregate calls plus 30 importer-name calls, 0 rate-limit rejections after pacing at 2.5 s/call. Duty came from the UK Integrated Online Tariff API (`https://www.trade-tariff.service.gov.uk/api/v2/headings/{hs4}` then `/api/v2/commodities/{10-digit}`, 28 headings, ~600 commodity calls). Query patterns (one set per HS4):
- Monthly value and net mass by CN8: `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/OTS?$apply=filter(Commodity/Hs4Code eq '8535' and MonthId ge 202301 and MonthId le 202607 and (FlowTypeId eq 1 or FlowTypeId eq 3))/groupby((CommodityId,MonthId),aggregate(Value with sum as V,NetMass with sum as M))`
- Source countries 2025: `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/OTS?$apply=filter(Commodity/Hs4Code eq '8535' and MonthId ge 202501 and MonthId le 202512 and (FlowTypeId eq 1 or FlowTypeId eq 3))/groupby((CommodityId,CountryId),aggregate(Value with sum as V))`
- Distinct importers 2025: `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/Import?$apply=filter(Commodity/Hs4Code eq '8535' and MonthId ge 202501 and MonthId le 202512)/groupby((CommodityId,TraderId))` (paged via `@odata.nextLink`; distinct TraderId counted in Python per line)
- Importer names (sample, 2025): `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/Import?$filter=CommodityId eq 85042100 and MonthId ge 202501 and MonthId le 202512&$expand=Trader&$top=400`
- Lookups: `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/Country`, `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/Commodity?$filter=Hs4Code eq '8535'`, `GET https://api.uktradeinfo.com/FlowType`
Caveats: importer details are names by commodity-month, not values, so importer counts measure breadth not concentration; "importers per £10m" is my fragmentation proxy, on the same log scale as the earlier scan (`import_line_candidates.md`); some CN8 lines are suppressed (SuppressionIndex) and read low; 9403 20 and 7326 90 are broad proxies (racks and containment have no dedicated CN8). Scoring weights are identical to the earlier scan: fragmentation 20, margin 20, repeat purchase 15, import growth 10, compliance ease 10, freight-friendliness 10, buyer reachable face-to-face 15 (0–100); sub-score formulas were back-fitted to the earlier scan's published sub-scores (frag_s = 0.438·ln(importers per £10m) − 1.32; growth_s = 0.5 + 0.0127·YoY% + 0.0129·3-yr%; margin_s = (GM% − 15)/30; freight_s = 0.27·ln(£/kg); all clamped 0–1; repeat, compliance and face-to-face are judgement inputs stated per line).

## Key Question 1 (Part A): How big and how dated are the UK data-centre and grid demand signals, and which shortages are documented?

### Takeaway
The UK committed data-centre pipeline reached 14.6 GW / 173 projects in September 2026 (Barbour ABI) against roughly 2.0 GW operational at end-2025 and only 242 MW physically under construction in H1 2026 (Savills), while ~50 GW of data-centre demand and 283 GW of generation/storage sit in NESO's reformed queue; the binding constraints are grid connections, long-lead electrical equipment (33 kV transformers up to 15 months, 132 kV two years, 400 kV four years, switchgear "doubled"; gensets 52–110 weeks; UPS 26–52 weeks) and MEP labour (100% of UK respondents reporting MEP shortages), all of which favour maintenance, spares and consumables sellers over new-equipment distributors.

### Inferences
- The pipeline-to-construction ratio (14.6 GW committed vs 0.24–0.87 GW under construction) means most 2026–28 spend lands with a small pool of Tier-1 GCs and MEP subcontractors, plus DNO/ICP works; a sole founder reaches this market through electrical contractors, panel builders, generator/UPS maintainers, cabling installers and regional colocation FM teams, not hyperscalers.
- Long-lead shortages are in transformers, HV switchgear, large gensets, UPS frames and HV cable, all capital-intensive lines with concentrated importers (see Part B: 262 transformer importers, 291 genset importers); they create demand for maintenance, life-extension and spares (load banks, oil testing, battery replacement, thermography) rather than for new small distributors.

### Gaps
- No UK-specific published lead-time series for distribution transformers or LV switchgear from BEAMA, Make UK or ENA was found; the UK figures rest on the Baringa 2024 study as reported by Energy UK. Most week-count figures are US-centric trade sources.
- DC Byte, CBRE and Knight Frank UK market reports were not retrievable (Knight Frank PDF unparseable; CBRE not found in search); Savills figures are snippet-only.
- NESO/Ofgem do not publish a separate data-centre GW figure; the 50 GW and 73 GW figures come from press reporting of Ofgem's February and July 2026 consultations.

### Inferences
- Lines where importer counts run above ~90 per £10m (earthing/LP, insulators/glands, patch cabling, containment, PPE, dry-type transformers, test instruments) are the ones where small trade-only importers already coexist with brands; lines below ~20 per £10m (oil transformers, gensets, HV cable, CRAC, Li-ion, LV cable) are OEM/DNO/hire-fleet direct imports where a £25–50k stock book cannot get a supplier agreement or a buyer's approval.
- Fibre connectors' +66% three-year growth and £41.8/kg value density fit a hand-carry/parcel model, but the −12% YoY dip and the presence of FS.com and Netceed (direct-to-installer online sellers) cap margins; pairing connectors with earthing/LP hardware or arc-flash PPE for the same electrical-contractor buyer is the more defensible mix.
- Generator spares (+14% YoY) and UPS batteries (−33% over three years, likely partial migration to lithium and price deflation) are the two lines whose buyers (generator/UPS maintainers) also appear as importers themselves, meaning a distributor must win on availability and pack size rather than price.

### Gaps
- Racks, aisle containment, busbar trunking, CDUs and cold plates have no dedicated CN8, so their import values cannot be isolated; 9403 20 and 7326 90 are proxies with unrelated goods.
- 3820 antifreeze is dominated by automotive importers; PG25 data-centre coolant volumes cannot be separated from the HMRC data.
- HMRC importer names for EU-sourced goods may be under-recorded (Intrastat covers NI–EU only since 2022), so EU-heavy lines (S13 pumps, S11 fluids) may have more importers than shown.

## Key Question 3 (Part C): Which service niches are reachable for a £200–450k acquisition or a small start, and what evidence exists on drivers, contracts, firms and listings?

### Takeaway
The niches with statutory or insurer-driven recurring demand and small-firm supply are critical-power maintenance (generator/UPS/load-bank/fuel polishing), electrical thermography and fixed-wire testing, data-hall cleaning to ISO 14644-1 Class 8, gas-suppression servicing and F-gas cooling maintenance; listings with disclosed numbers are scarce (Daltons: thermography £407k turnover/£142k EBITDA; electrical engineering & testing group £1.1m/£152k; NE electrical contractor with compliance revenue £1.52m/£308k; several refrigeration/AC contractors £357k–£993k turnover with £108k–£195k profit), and none was found specifically for generator/UPS, HV maintenance, BESS O&M or data-hall cleaning.

## Key Question 4 (Part D): For each top candidate, why could a £25–50k distributor or a £200–450k service firm enter, and what kills it?

### Takeaway
Entry is credible where the buyer is an electrical contractor, panel builder, maintainer or regional colocation FM team who buys in small, frequent lots (earthing/LP, glands and joint kits, fibre connectors, arc-flash PPE, generator spares, filters, suppression agents) or where a statutory cycle forces spend (load-bank tests, thermography, six-monthly gas checks); entry fails where the importer list is DNOs, OEMs and hire fleets (transformers, gensets, HV switchgear, CRAC, Li-ion), where certification is third-party (Cat III PPE approvals, BAFE, NERS, REFCOM) or where Amazon Business and FS.com already sell direct (patch leads, small UPS).
