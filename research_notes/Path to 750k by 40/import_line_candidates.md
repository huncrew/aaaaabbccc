### Inferences
- The "listing scan" hypotheses that survive the data are the MRO mechanical lines (bearings, power transmission, hydraulics, abrasives, welding consumables): mid-sized import pools (£30m–£490m), thousands of importers, 0–8% MFN, 0% from every FTA partner including India, no certification regime and positive 2026 momentum (+11% to +19% y/y Jan–Jul).
- The hypotheses the data argues against for a £25–50k probe: solar BOS (−17% 3-yr, Chinese aluminium under anti-dumping, Segen ~40% share), drones (tiny, class-marking, DJI-dominated), off-road tyres (319 importers, capital-like ticket sizes), pest control (356 importers, biocide authorisations and rodenticide point-of-sale rules), LED (−9% 3-yr, 56% China, wholesaler/Amazon price transparency) and EV accessories (no dedicated code; proxies are generic cable/connector codes).

### Gaps
- HMRC does not publish value per importer, so true concentration (share held by the top 5 importers) cannot be computed; importer counts include one-off and own-use importers (OEMs, end users), not only distributors.
- No dedicated commodity code exists for EV charging leads/wallboxes (classified within 8544 42 90 and 8537 10 / 8504 40); the EV line uses proxy codes and is therefore over-stated.
- Drone parts sit inside 8807 30 00 with all aeroplane/helicopter parts (£3.9bn) and cannot be isolated; the line uses complete UAVs (8806) only.

### Top-8 line notes: why it works / what kills it / how to run the five-buyer test

**1. Welding consumables (8311 electrodes, cored wire, brazing rods; 8515 90 torch parts).** Why it works: the smallest, most fragmented line in the set — only £30m of imports in 2025 but 484 distinct importers (160 per £10m, the highest ratio found), MFN 0%, no product regulation beyond SDS/EN classification, a true burn-and-replace consumable, and buyers (fabricators, garages, plant repairers, farms) who buy from a local welding counter and can be visited with a box of samples; imports were up 19% Jan–Jul 2026 y/y. What kills it: value density is poor (£3.04/kg, so freight and a pallet of wire ties up cash for little revenue), brand loyalty to ESAB/Lincoln/Sif is strong, Weldability Sif already runs a trade-only wholesale model with 2,200–2,900 lines feeding the same independent counters, and mixed gas/cylinder supply (which anchors accounts) is outside a £25–50k probe. Five-buyer test: pick five fabrication/engineering shops within 30 miles, take one pallet-mix (E6013 3.2 mm, ER70S-6 1.0 mm MIG wire, flux-cored 1.2 mm, contact tips/nozzles), offer a 20-kg trial at 10% under their current invoice with a same-day drop, and count how many reorder within 30 days without a price prompt; the line passes if three reorder and at least one asks for torch spares as well.

**2. Abrasives (6804 bonded wheels/cutting discs, 6805 coated discs/belts/rolls).** Why it works: £168m imports, 1,937 importers (116 per £10m), MFN 0% and 0% from all seven FTA partners, no product regime beyond EN 12413/EN 13743 marking, pure consumable, £8.51/kg, and every fabricator, garage, bodyshop and plant-hire yard uses cutting/grinding/flap discs daily. What kills it: the 3-year import trend is −9% (2023 £184m to 2025 £168m), premium brands (3M, Norton, Pferd, Klingspor) sell through the same broadline distributors (Cromwell, Hayley, Rubix), and generic 115 mm discs are a classic Amazon/Toolstation price-comparison item, so a probe wins only on a differentiated SKU (ceramic flap discs, zirconia belts) where a German/Austrian/Italian or Turkish source gives a quality story. Five-buyer test: give five workshops a 50-disc sample box and a written life-per-disc comparison versus their incumbent; the line passes if at least three switch a repeat order at a ≥35% gross margin on your landed cost.

**3. Hydraulic hose assemblies and steel fittings (4009 hose with fittings, 7307 99/7307 91/7307 19 fittings and adaptors).** Why it works: £487m imports (+3% 3-yr, +13% Jan–Jul 2026), 3,166 importers, MFN 0–2%, no product certification, high replacement frequency on plant, and buyers (plant hire, agricultural contractors, machinery repairers) who need the part identified and delivered to the yard the same day — the least Amazon-exposed category in the set, with NAHAD reporting 35% typical / 45% high-performer distributor gross margins. What kills it: the service model (Pirtek's 86 centres, Hydraquip, Hydrasun, Rubix) wins on crimping capability, not stock — a stock-only importer of adaptors/BSP-JIC fittings competes on price with Italian and Chinese makers already imported by 1,141 UK firms on 7307 99 80 alone; £7.82/kg is middling for freight. Five-buyer test: visit five plant-hire depots and agricultural engineers with a box of the 40 fastest-moving BSP/JIC/ORFS adaptors and quick-release couplings, offer a consignment rack; pass if three keep the rack and re-fill within a month.

**4. Power transmission (4010 V-belts, 7315 11 roller chain, 8483 50/60 pulleys, couplings).** Why it works: £387m imports, 2,792 importers (72 per £10m), +11% Jan–Jul 2026 y/y, 0–6% MFN and 0% from EU/India/Turkey/Vietnam/Japan/Korea/Australia, no product regime, wear-part demand, and the same workshop/food-plant/farm buyers as bearings so it stacks with line 5. What kills it: Germany and Italy supply 48% of UK imports (Optibelt, Gates, Regina, Tsubaki networks), and sizing/pitch mistakes turn cheap stock into dead stock; £8.87/kg. Five-buyer test: offer five maintenance engineers a size-matched replacement kit (SPZ/SPA belts, 08B/10B chain, connecting links) priced 15% under the branded list, then check whether they call you first on the next breakdown; pass if three do within 60 days.

**5. Bearings (8482).** Why it works: £419m imports, 2,896 importers with 1,417 on the single code 8482 10 90 alone, +15% Jan–Jul 2026 y/y, the highest value density of the mechanical lines (£23.11/kg), no product regulation, and universal demand across workshops, plant hire and food/packaging plants; MFN 8% but 0% from EU, India, Japan, Korea and the rest — the India FTA (in force 15 July 2026) makes Indian-made bearings duty-free, a real 8-point saving. What kills it: brand and counterfeit risk (SKF/NSK/FAG dominate specification; unbranded Chinese stock is what the incumbents already import at 5% share), Germany/US/France supply 63% of value, and a £25–50k probe cannot hold enough sizes to be a first call; distributor gross margin benchmarks are ~30% (Applied Industrial 30.3%). Five-buyer test: identify five buyers' 30 most-used part numbers from their stores lists, quote a second-brand (Indian/Japanese) price with certificates of conformity, and pass if three place a trial order and none rejects on brand policy.

**6. Hand tools and power-tool accessories (8202 saw blades, 8204 spanners, 8205 hand tools, 8207 bits/drills).** Why it works: £911m imports (+7% 3-yr), 9,532 importers (105 per £10m — the second most fragmented), MFN 0–2%, no regulation for accessories, £11.13/kg, and the broadest buyer base (contractors, joiners, garages). What kills it: this is the category most exposed to Amazon Business, Toolstation and Screwfix — 8205 59 80 alone has 3,027 importers and China supplies 31% — so a generic importer has no moat; distributor gross margins are ~30% and price transparency is total. Five-buyer test: pick a niche accessory family (e.g. SDS-max chisels, TCT blades for site saws, step drills) and put a counter-top display with five independent tool shops/hire depots; pass only if sell-through beats 50% in 30 days at ≥35% margin.

**7. Hygienic process components (7307 2x stainless fittings, 8481 80 ball/butterfly/diaphragm valves, 4016 93 gaskets, 8484 seals).** Why it works: £974m imports, 6,967 importers, MFN 0–2%, high value density (£21.66/kg), seals/gaskets are consumables and valves/fittings are specified by engineers — the least price-transparent category and the one where Diploma-style value-add distributors earn 22.5% operating margins. What kills it: buyers are few (775 meat processors, ~5,800 bakeries, plus dairies/breweries) and audit-gated (food-contact declarations, 3-A/EHEDG, material certs), incumbent specialists hold approvals, and a sole trader must fund certificates and traceability before the first sale. Five-buyer test: approach five food/drink plants' maintenance managers with a certified sample kit (tri-clamp ferrules/gaskets, EPDM seals, a DN25 butterfly valve) plus 3.1 material certs; pass if three put you on the approved supplier list within 90 days.

**8. Fire safety consumables (8424 10 extinguishers, 8531 10 30 building fire/smoke alarms, 8310 signs, 3813 charges).** Why it works: £633m imports growing +8% 3-yr and +9% Jan–Jul 2026, 3,288 importers, MFN 0–2%, a compliance-driven replacement cycle (5-year extended service, 10-year replacement, BS 5839 alarms), and buyers (fire-protection service companies, FM firms, landlords, plant hire) who cannot buy unbranded; London Security plc shows the economics (revenue £244.3m, ~73% gross margin) once servicing is attached. What kills it: BS EN 3 kitemark/BAFE expectations, pressure-equipment conformity and third-party certification put a certification bill in front of the probe; Ireland (25.6%) and the US supply the certified brands; and extinguishers are heavy/dangerous goods for parcel freight. Five-buyer test: approach five independent fire-protection service firms with certified extinguishers, signage packs and brackets at a price 15% under their current supplier; pass if three transfer a monthly order and none requires a certificate you cannot show.

### Inferences
- Because margin and repeat-purchase inputs are sourced from public comparables rather than line-specific UK trade price lists, the ranking is most robust on the data-driven criteria (fragmentation, growth, freight, tariff) and least robust on margin; a ±5-point margin change moves lines 3–9 by one or two places but does not displace welding/abrasives/hydraulics from the top three or solar/drones/tyres from the bottom three.
- The top five lines share the same buyer (workshop, plant hire, agricultural engineer, garage); a probe on one gives the founder a call list for the others, so the single-line probe should be chosen for lowest cash-to-first-sale (welding consumables or abrasives) and the follow-on line for margin (hydraulic adaptors, bearings).

### Gaps
- No systematic trade-list vs wholesale price comparison was run (see KQ4 gaps), so "typical trade gross margin" is an estimate per line; the five-buyer tests above are designed to measure it directly.

## Key Question 5: Compliance burden by line
