Scope note: all figures GBP, UK only. Every term is dated because rates and scheme rules move. Where a primary page (british-business-bank.co.uk) returned HTTP 403 on fetch, the content is reported from the search-engine excerpt of that URL and corroborated by secondary sources; this is flagged inline. ## 1. British Business Bank Growth Guarantee Scheme (GGS): status, end date, facility, guarantee, personal guarantees, PPR, lenders

### Takeaway
GGS is open, extended to at least 31 March 2030, offers up to £2m per business group with a 70% government guarantee to the lender (borrower stays 100% liable), permits personal guarantees at lender discretion but prohibits taking a principal private residence as security, and had 55 accredited lenders as at July 2026. A July 2026 Treasury package added capacity (£6.5bn), a 10-year maximum term for term loans/asset finance and a £54m turnover ceiling, but as at late September 2026 individual lender pages still quote 6 years and £45m.

### Cited Findings
- GGS was "further extended until 31 March 2030" in the 2025 Spending Review; on 12 July 2026 the Chancellor announced an increase in GGS capacity "unlocking a further £6.5bn of market lending over the next four years", helping "an estimated 33,000 businesses"; "GGS will offer greater flexibility to support facility terms up to ten years for Term Loans and Asset Finance" and "annual turnover eligibility will increase from £45m to £54m" — [British Business Bank press release, 12 July 2026 (page returned 403 on direct fetch; content from search excerpt of the URL)](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/news-and-events/news/british-business-bank-welcomes-chancellor-exchequers-announcement-package-new-measures-support)
- Scheme extension "announced 13 July 2026", "extended until 31 March 2030", maximum facility "up to £2 million" per business group, term loans "up to 6 years", personal guarantee "may be required. Private residence excluded from security", turnover cap "£45 million" — [Liquid Commercial Finance, GGS update, 2026](https://www.liquidcf.co.uk/growth-guarantee-scheme-update/)
- "The government supports facilities of up to £2 million ... with repayment terms of up to six years and longer terms being introduced from July 2026, and it has been extended to run until March 2030" — [Daltons Business, financing guide, 2026](https://www.daltonsbusiness.com/advice/uncategorized/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-your-funding-options)
- GGS "extended until 31 March 2030, providing a 70% guarantee to lenders on facilities up to £2 million"; borrower "100% of debt remains borrower's responsibility"; eligibility turnover up to £45m — [V4B Business Finance, June 2026](https://www.businessfinance-v4b.com/finance-options-for-buying-an-existing-business-in-2026/)
- Guarantee: "70% of the finance" guaranteed by UK government; term loans and asset finance "3 months to 6 years"; overdrafts, invoice finance and ABL "3 months to 3 years"; "You cannot use your main private residence as security for the loan"; "you will still be solely responsible for repaying the full debt"; eligible for "any legitimate business purpose, including growth and investment"; turnover "less than £45 million" — [Find Business Support (Scottish Government) GGS page](https://findbusinesssupport.gov.scot/service/funding/british-business-bank-growth-guarantee-scheme)
- NatWest GGS terms: minimum "£25,001 for term loans", max "£2m per business group (£1m for Northern Ireland Protocol borrowers)", "three months up to six years", "70% government-backed guarantee against the outstanding balance after the lender completes its standard recovery process", "Personal guarantees can be taken at NatWest's discretion, in line with our normal commercial lending practices", "Principal Private Residences cannot be taken as security within the Scheme", pricing "will vary and will depend on the specific lending proposal" — [NatWest GGS page](https://www.natwest.com/business/loans-and-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme.html)
- Virgin Money GGS: minimum £30,000, max £2m per group; six-year max for term loans; turnover £45m; borrower "100% liable for the debt"; "You can't use a main home (often called a principal private residence) as security for this scheme" — [Virgin Money GGS page](https://uk.virginmoney.com/business/finance/business-loans/government-loan-schemes/ggs/)
- Propel Finance (asset-finance accredited lender): minimum GGS facility £100,000; asset-finance GGS terms "12 to 72 months"; "Taking security over Principal Private Residences is strictly prohibited within the Scheme"; PGs "at Propel Finance's discretion" — [Propel Finance GGS page](https://propelfinance.co.uk/our-solutions/growth-guarantee-scheme)
- Scheme rules "leave personal guarantees at the discretion of each accredited lender"; PGs "may be capped at a fixed sum or a percentage of the facility", with the article citing "common negotiated outcomes of 20–50% of the facility amount, though full guarantees also occur"; the lender must "recover what it can from the company, then demand the shortfall from guarantors"; the 70% guarantee "does not reduce the borrower's liability"; the PPR rule "stops the lender taking a charge over your main home as security ... It does not make a personal guarantee meaningless: if a PG is enforced through the courts, your wider personal assets are still exposed" — [Connection Technologies, GGS PG rules, published 12 June 2026, updated 22 September 2026](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/growth-guarantee-scheme-personal-guarantee)
- "Accredited lenders: 55 listed as of July 2026"; cumulative GGS lending "by 31 March 2026: 21,194 facilities worth £3.64 billion across all eligible purposes"; Funding Circle offers a GGS product of "£25k–£250k" at "13.4% fixed" — [Palmstone Capital, Business Acquisition Loans UK guide, 17 July 2026](https://www.palmstone-capital.com/debt-advisory/business-acquisition-loans)
- GGS loan range "£25,001 to £2 million", "Terms: Up to 6 years", "Government guarantee: 70% to lender" — [Buy a Business Ltd, 4 May 2026](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)
- Older baseline (still consistent): "the lender may require personal guarantees, despite government backing"; "Principal private residences cannot be taken as security within the scheme" — [Hilton-Baird GGS guide, 12 August 2024](https://www.hiltonbairdfinancial.co.uk/the-growth-guarantee-scheme-guide-for-smes/)
- The official BBB scheme page and FAQ page exist but returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch — [BBB GGS page](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/finance-options/debt-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme); [BBB GGS FAQs](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/about/frequently-asked-questions/growth-guarantee-scheme); [BBB GGS performance data at 31 March 2026](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/about/research-and-publications/ggs-including-rls-iteration-3-performance-data-31-march-2026)

### Inferences
- The 10-year term and £54m turnover changes announced 12 July 2026 do not yet appear on NatWest, Virgin Money, Propel or Find Business Support pages as at late September 2026; a buyer should assume 6-year maximum terms unless a specific lender confirms it has implemented the new maximum.
- GGS does not eliminate PGs. Its practical value to a buyer without property is (a) the PPR cannot be charged, and (b) lenders have room to cap PGs (20–50% of facility is cited as a common negotiated range by one broker-side source, not by the BBB itself).
- The scheme's £25,001 floor (NatWest) and £30,000 floor (Virgin Money) mean a £150–250k acquisition loan is squarely within GGS range; acquisitions are a "legitimate business purpose", though no lender page explicitly lists "buying a business" as an example use.

### Gaps
- The official BBB accredited-lender list could not be fetched (403); the "55 lenders as of July 2026" count is from Palmstone Capital, not the BBB directly.
- The exact effective date for lenders to offer 10-year GGS terms and the £54m turnover cap was not found in any fetchable source.
- The BBB GGS FAQ wording on personal guarantees (e.g., any cap on PG recoveries at 20% of the facility as applied under the old RLS iteration 1) could not be verified for GGS.

## 2. Lenders active in sub-£1m acquisition finance: sizes, LTV, EBITDA multiples, rates, terms, fees, deposit, PG, debenture

### Takeaway
Most of the named "acquisition finance" specialists (Allica Growth Finance, ThinCats, Shawbrook corporate/ABL, Caple, Triple Point, Cynergy ABL) set minimums of £200k–£3m and target EBITDA of £500k+, so a £200–450k deal is served mainly by (i) unsecured/GGS-backed SME term loans (Funding Circle, Allica unsecured, iwoca, high-street business loans) at roughly 7–15% fixed with 1–3% fees, PGs and 1–6 year terms, and (ii) asset-based facilities. Broker guides in 2026 quote deposits of 10–50% (most commonly 20–40%), lender funding of 50–90% of price, senior debt at 1.5–3.0x adjusted EBITDA for small owner-managed deals, DSCR minimums of 1.25–1.5x, and margins of Bank Rate/SONIA + 2–6 points.

### Inferences
- For a £200–450k EV deal the realistic bank/lender universe is: GGS-backed term loans from high-street banks (HSBC/NatWest/Lloyds/Barclays via relationship managers), Funding Circle (standard or GGS product to £250k), Allica unsecured (to £150k, needs 3 years' filed accounts and 1.5x DSC on the borrowing entity), plus asset-based lenders (Cynergy from £200k; Close Brothers invoice finance from £25k). Allica Growth Finance, ThinCats, Shawbrook corporate lending, Caple (£500k floor) and Triple Point are above this ticket size.
- Debentures: no lender page fetched states debenture policy for sub-£250k loans; the market convention reported by Sprintlaw/Palmstone is that a secured lender takes a fixed and floating charge (debenture) over the acquiring company and requires seller notes to be subordinated. Unsecured products (Funding Circle, iwoca, Allica unsecured) rely on a PG rather than a debenture.
- "Three years of accounts" on the target satisfies Capitalise's "2–3 years of target accounts" expectation and Allica's three-year filed-accounts rule, but note Allica's unsecured product assesses the borrowing company, which for a newco buyer will be the target post-acquisition or a hive-up structure; lenders will look through to the target's historic EBITDA.

### Gaps
- No lender publishes an explicit LTV, EBITDA cap, or margin schedule for sub-£500k acquisitions; all figures above are broker/adviser ranges, not lender rate cards.
- Cynergy, Triple Point, Shawbrook, ThinCats and Close Brothers do not publish pricing.
- Funding Circle's GGS product page could not be fetched (404); the 13.4% fixed figure relies on Palmstone Capital (17 July 2026).
- Specific Lloyds/NatWest/Barclays/HSBC acquisition-loan (as opposed to general business loan) terms are not published online; they are relationship-managed.

## 3. Invoice finance and asset finance (stock, vehicles, plant): terms and advance rates

### Takeaway
Invoice finance in 2026 advances 70–95% of invoice value (80–90% typical) at a service fee of roughly 0.5–3% of invoice value plus a discount charge of about SONIA/Bank Rate + 1.5–7% depending on size; stock can be funded up to 100% of net orderly liquidation value by specialist ABL lenders, and plant/vehicle HP is priced from around 4–12% APR over 1–7 years with case-by-case deposits.

### Cited Findings
- Spark Finance invoice finance rate index (updated June 2026, next review September 2026): advance "70% to 90% of invoice value"; service fee "0.5% to 2.5% of invoice value" (prime 0.2–0.8%, standard SME 0.8–1.8%, specialist/small 1.8–3.0%); discount margin "SONIA + 1.5% to 2.5%" (prime), "SONIA + 2.5% to 4.5%" (standard), "SONIA + 4.5% to 7.0%" (specialist); minimum turnover "£100,000" (some lenders £50k) — [Spark Finance UK Invoice Finance Rate Index](https://www.sparkfinance.co.uk/data/uk-invoice-finance-rate-index)
- Advance "80–90%" of invoice value; service fee "0.5% to 3% of turnover"; discount charge "base rate plus 2–5% per annum"; example cost for £500k turnover on 60-day terms "between £5,000 and £15,000 per year" — [Growth Business Finance, invoice finance guide, 3 April 2026](https://growthbusinessfinance.com/invoice-finance-uk-a-complete-guide-for-smes-in-2026/)
- Close Brothers invoice finance: advance "typically 80% to 90%", "3.5 – 10% annually", min turnover £500k; asset finance from £5,000 per asset, HP/lease/refinance, priced case by case — [Funding Agent, 3 August 2026](https://www.fundingagent.co.uk/post/close-brothers-invoice-finance-and-asset-lending)
- Ultimate Finance advances "up to 95%" of invoice value; discount charges in the market "typically base rate + 1.5–3.5%" — [MarketInvoice, 25 September 2026](https://marketinvoice.co.uk/providers/ultimate-finance/)
- ABL collateral advance rates (one lender example): "Arbuthnot ABL – eligible receivables: up to 95%"; "Arbuthnot ABL – stock: up to 100% net orderly liquidation value"; ABL pricing "SONIA + 2.5%–5.5%" with "0.25%–1.5% service fee" (£1m+ facilities) — [Palmstone Capital, 17 July 2026](https://www.palmstone-capital.com/debt-advisory/business-acquisition-loans)
- Asset finance: "£5,000 to £500,000 per asset", "2–7 years", "Starts from 7%"; asset-based lending "£50,000 to £10m+" — [Capitalise, 3 August 2026](https://capitalise.com/gb/insights/business-loans/loan-to-buy-a-business)
- Asset finance "4% to 12% APR" — [V4B, June 2026](https://www.businessfinance-v4b.com/finance-options-for-buying-an-existing-business-in-2026/)
- Allica asset finance "£25,000–£1 million over 1–7 years ... 7–9%" — [Swoop review](https://swoopfunding.com/uk/lender-review/allica-bank/)
- UK Finance: invoice finance and ABL members advance "up to 90% of the invoice value"; outstanding facilities "well over £20 billion"; supported businesses' combined turnover "over £315 billion" (2024) — [UK Finance, Invoice Finance and ABL](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/our-expertise/commercial-finance/invoice-finance-and-asset-based-lending)
- A "refinance strategy" of valuing the target's "unencumbered machinery, equipment, or property" and refinancing it to raise the deposit is described as a common tactic — [search excerpt citing Daltons/Capitalise guides](https://www.daltonsbusiness.com/advice/uncategorized/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-your-funding-options)
- GGS asset-finance terms at an accredited asset-finance lender: "12 to 72 months depending on the equipment type", minimum £100,000 — [Propel Finance](https://propelfinance.co.uk/our-solutions/growth-guarantee-scheme)

### Inferences
- With Bank Rate at 3.75% (see section 6), a standard-SME invoice discounting line in September 2026 costs roughly 6.25–8.25% p.a. on drawn funds plus a 0.8–1.8% service fee on invoices; on a £350k-turnover business with 45-day debtors the drawable amount is small (tens of thousands), so invoice finance is a working-capital supplement rather than a purchase-price funder.
- Asset refinance (HP-style refinance of unencumbered vans/plant) is the main way asset finance contributes to the purchase price in a sub-£500k deal.

### Gaps
- No source gave explicit HP advance rates or deposit percentages for vehicles or plant in 2026 (typical "100% of asset less deposit" wording was not found in a citable 2026 source); Close Brothers and Cynergy do not publish them.
- Stock advance rates beyond the single Arbuthnot example (100% NOLV) were not found; most guides say stock and plant are funded but do not quantify.

## 4. Vendor/seller finance norms: share, term, interest, subordination

### Takeaway
UK sources agree seller finance (deferred consideration/vendor loan notes) is common in SME deals and runs 1–4 years at Bank Rate + 2–4 points, ranking behind any bank lender under an intercreditor deed; the share of price deferred is quoted anywhere from 20% (worked examples) to "over 50%" (one adviser's SME-under-£5m generalisation), with sellers typically wanting at least 20–40% (some 40–70%) paid at completion.

### Cited Findings
- For SME purchases "under £5m value, a typical deferred amount would be anything over 50%"; "typical deferred period could be between 2 and 4 years"; paid as "vendor loan notes at an interest rate of 2–4 percentage points over the bank base rate per annum"; with external debt the loan notes "would have a second charge over the company's assets and rank behind the providers of any external debt"; payments "often quarterly or annually ... in arrears" — [Valius, What is deferred consideration, 24 August 2026](https://www.valius.global/blog/what-is-deferred-consideration)
- Worked example: "£300,000 acquisition—£180,000 at completion, £120,000 over 3 years" (i.e. 40% deferred); funding-stack example for a £400,000 deal: "Personal contribution: 35% (£140,000); Commercial loan: 45% (£180,000); Seller finance: 20% (£80,000)" — [Buy a Business Ltd, 4 May 2026](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)
- "many sellers look for at least 20–40% upfront"; security "a first-ranking charge", "share pledges" or "all-assets fixed and floating charges", registered at Companies House "within 21 days"; where third-party finance exists, "settle priority early through an intercreditor deed ... without it, you can find yourself unsecured in practice" — [Sprintlaw UK, Seller financing a business, 22 December 2025](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/seller-financing-a-business-in-the-uk-how-to-structure-and-protect/)
- Initial payment at completion typically "40–70% of the price"; deferred "usually one to three years, often with interest" — [search excerpt from Sprintlaw/Taylor Rose set](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/seller-financed-business-sale-in-the-uk-legal-risks-and-contract-terms/)
- Deferred consideration "usually 1–3 years" and "can substitute for part of deposit requirement" — [NewOwner, 15 June 2026](https://newowner.co.uk/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-uk)
- In UK MBO structures vendor loan notes "typically represent 5–15% of the enterprise value", interest "5–10% range", maturity "5–7 years", "subordinated to senior debt and usually to mezzanine" — [FD Capital earn-out guide (undated, 2021–2024 site)](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/earn-out-guide/)
- Senior lenders "typically require subordination, payment-blocking during covenant breaches, and limited enforcement rights" on seller notes — [Palmstone Capital, 17 July 2026](https://www.palmstone-capital.com/debt-advisory/business-acquisition-loans)
- Earn-outs are "the most common form of deferred consideration in UK private company sales, particularly in the £2m–£20m range" — [Taylor Rose Solicitors](https://www.taylor-rose.co.uk/posts/deferred-consideration)
- ETA/search-fund norm (generic, not UK-specific): personal equity 0–20%, investor equity 5–30%, "seller note (10–30%) and senior bank debt (50–90%)" — [Darden Ideas to Action](https://ideas.darden.virginia.edu/eta-funding-models)

### Inferences
- The realistic seller-finance share in sub-£500k UK deals is 20–40% of price over 2–3 years; "over 50%" (Valius) reflects deals where bank debt is unavailable. At Bank Rate 3.75%, a "base + 2–4" vendor note prices at 5.75–7.75% (Valius formula), cheaper than any unsecured bank product.
- Any bank lender will insist the seller note is subordinated (second charge or unsecured, intercreditor deed, payment-blocking on covenant breach). Sellers who want a first charge effectively rule out senior bank debt.

### Gaps
- No survey data quantifying the average deferred percentage in completed UK sub-£500k deals was found; all figures are adviser generalisations or worked examples.
- No source addresses whether UK lenders count a fully subordinated seller note as "equity" for deposit purposes (as SBA lenders do in the US).

## 5. Typical total structure for a £350k deal (cash deposit / bank debt / seller note)

### Takeaway
Combining 2026 UK adviser norms, a £350k deal typically stacks as roughly 20–40% buyer cash (£70–140k), 40–55% bank/GGS debt (£140–190k) and 20–30% seller note (£70–105k), with the bank tranche capped by 1.5–3.0x EBITDA and a 1.25–1.5x DSCR test.

### Cited Findings

- Professional costs: legal "£2,000–£8,000+", accounting/DD "£1,500–£5,000+", stamp duty on shares 0.5% — [Buy a Business Ltd, 4 May 2026](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)

### Inferences
- Illustrative £350k structure consistent with the cited ranges: £90k buyer cash (26%), £175k GGS-backed term loan (50%; at ~£100k EBITDA this is 1.75x, inside the 1.5–3.0x band), £85k seller note (24%) over 3 years at Bank Rate + 3% (6.75%). Plus roughly £10–20k of fees/costs and a £35–70k working-capital reserve, so total cash need is nearer £135–180k unless the reserve is borrowed. This is a derived illustration, not a sourced deal.
- At 40% cash (£140k) the buyer with £60–110k cannot reach the Buy a Business Ltd "30–50%" norm without a larger seller note; at 20–26% cash the deal works only if a lender accepts the lower deposit, which Capitalise says usually costs "additional security, a personal guarantee, or a higher rate".

### Gaps

## 7. Government support for succession/ETA, including Employee Ownership Trust rules 2026

### Takeaway
There is no UK government scheme specifically for individual buyers of small businesses beyond GGS (and Start Up Loans up to £25k per person); the main 2026 policy change affecting sellers is that EOT sales from 26 November 2025 get only 50% CGT relief (effective ~12% rate) with no BADR on the taxable half, which narrows the tax advantage of EOTs over a trade sale to an individual and pushes sellers to compare the two more carefully.

### Cited Findings
- "the full CGT exemption will be replaced with a 50% relief for sales taking place on or after 26 November 2025"; "Business asset disposal relief and investor relief will no longer be available where the EOT CGT relief applies"; effective rate "approximately 12% on the full gain" for higher-rate taxpayers — [Tax Adviser magazine (search excerpt; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.taxadvisermagazine.com/article/employee-ownership-trusts-unexpected-reduction-capital-gains-tax-relief)
- Deloitte: old relief "100% relief from capital gains tax", new relief "50% of the gain" for "disposals on or after 26 November 2025"; "It will not be possible to claim business asset disposal relief or investors' relief on the 50% of the gain that remains taxable"; rationale: "reduce the cost of the relief, which is now significantly more than anticipated when it was introduced in 2014" — [Deloitte TaxScape, Autumn Budget 2025 EOT measure](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-autumn-budget-2025/cgt-relief-on-employee-ownership-trusts.aspx)
- Price Bailey: effective CGT "12% on the entire gain with no maximum cap"; "BADR (typically 10% for 2026/27)" cannot be layered; "With a 12% up front tax charge now in play, modelling the timings and amounts has become absolutely critical"; sellers "must now actively compare EOT structures against traditional trade sale alternatives more rigorously" — [Price Bailey EOT CGT guide](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/guides/capital-gains-tax-selling-business-employee-ownership-trust-eot/)
- Earlier changes from 30 October 2024 (Autumn Budget 2024) tightened conditions so "former owners cannot retain control of the company following disposal", trustees must be UK resident and must not pay more than market value — [House of Commons Library, Budget 2025: EOTs (PDF)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10437/CBP-10437.pdf); [Lewis Silkin, EOTs, 27 April 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/27/employee-ownership-trusts-a-different-business-model)
- Start Up Loans: "£500–£25,000 per person at fixed 6% per year over one to five years" — [NewOwner, 15 June 2026](https://newowner.co.uk/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-uk); another source states "fixed interest rate of 7.5 per cent per annum" and £5,000–£25,000 — [SmallBusiness.co.uk, 26 August 2026](https://smallbusiness.co.uk/best-small-business-loans-in-the-uk-2548223/) (sources conflict on the rate; verify on startuploans.co.uk)
- ThinCats lists "Employee ownership trusts (EOTs)" among funded transaction types — [ThinCats](https://www.thincats.com/)

### Inferences
- With EOT relief halved, a seller of a £350k business comparing a 12% effective EOT rate against BADR at 14% (2025/26) rising to 18% from April 2026 on a trade sale (BADR rate change per the Price Bailey note of "typically 10%" appears inconsistent with the announced 2025/26 = 14% and 2026/27 = 18% schedule; treat the BADR rate as unverified here) finds the gap has narrowed materially, so more owner-managed micro-businesses are likely to stay on the open market rather than go EOT. This is an inference about seller behaviour; no source quantifies the shift.

### Gaps
- BADR rate for 2026/27 was not verified from gov.uk in this research (Price Bailey's "typically 10%" conflicts with the Autumn 2024 announced schedule of 14%/18%); the report writer should check gov.uk before quoting.
- No UK government ETA/search-fund-specific programme was found.

## Key question A: Can a first-time buyer with £60–110k cash and no property pledge realistically borrow £150–250k against a business with three years of accounts, and on what terms?

### Takeaway
Yes at the lower end (£150–175k), tight at the upper end (£250k): sources put lender funding at 50–90% of price and deposits at 10–50%, so £60–110k cash supports a £200–350k price with a 20–30% seller note; the loan would be a GGS-backed or unsecured term loan at roughly 7–14% fixed (or Bank Rate + 3–6 variable) over 3–6 years, 1–3% fee, with a personal guarantee (possibly capped) but no charge on a main home.

### Cited Findings
- Deposits "10% to 30%"; lenders fund "70% up to a maximum of 90%"; higher-LTV lenders "offset this with additional security, a personal guarantee, or a higher rate" — [Capitalise, 3 August 2026](https://capitalise.com/gb/insights/business-loans/loan-to-buy-a-business)
- "Personal contribution 30–50% of purchase price required by most lenders", e.g. "£200,000 business requires £60,000–£100,000 personal contribution" — [Buy a Business Ltd, 4 May 2026](https://buyabusinessltd.com/blog/how-to-finance-buying-a-business-in-the-uk-a-practical-guide-for-2026)
- "First-time management or concentrated customers pushes requirement higher" than the 25–40% equity norm; SME unsecured/GGS-backed £25k–£250k priced "8%–18% fixed" with "2%–6% completion fee" — [Palmstone Capital, 17 July 2026](https://www.palmstone-capital.com/debt-advisory/business-acquisition-loans)
- Lenders expect "2–3 years of target accounts, management experience evidence" and "personal financial information" — [Capitalise, Acquisition finance](https://capitalise.com/gb/insights/business-loans/acquisition-finance)
- GGS: PPR cannot be taken as security; PGs at lender discretion — [NatWest GGS](https://www.natwest.com/business/loans-and-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme.html); [Find Business Support](https://findbusinesssupport.gov.scot/service/funding/british-business-bank-growth-guarantee-scheme)

- Allica unsecured £25,001–£150,000 at 9.90–13.75% fixed, 3% fee, PG, three years' filed accounts, 150% debt service cover — [Allica](https://www.allica.bank/business-loans)

### Inferences
- With £110k cash and a £350k price, a 31% deposit meets the mainstream 25–40% norm, leaving £240k to fund from a £150–190k loan plus £50–90k seller note. With £60k cash, the buyer needs a price nearer £200–250k or a seller note of 30%+.
- Property ownership is not a formal requirement (GGS forbids charging the main home), but a first-time buyer with no property offers the lender a PG of limited practical value, so expect pricing at the upper end of the ranges (10–14% fixed unsecured) or a lender insisting on GGS cover.

### Gaps
- No lender publishes a decline/approve statistic for first-time buyers; the feasibility view is inferred from deposit and DSCR norms.

## Key question C: Is a personal guarantee avoidable, or capped, under GGS or with any lender?

### Takeaway
Under GGS a PG is at lender discretion (not required by the scheme, not prohibited) and can be negotiated to a cap; the only lender found offering no PG at all is Caple (unsecured, but £500k minimum), while Funding Circle caps its flexible-loan PG at 2x the initial loan and Allica, iwoca and the high-street banks require director PGs.

### Cited Findings
- GGS: PGs "at the discretion of each accredited lender"; may be "capped at a fixed sum or a percentage of the facility", with "20–50% of the facility amount" cited as common negotiated outcomes; PPR cannot be charged — [Connection Technologies, updated 22 September 2026](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/growth-guarantee-scheme-personal-guarantee)
- "Personal guarantees can be taken at NatWest's discretion" — [NatWest](https://www.natwest.com/business/loans-and-finance/growth-guarantee-scheme.html); same wording at Propel Finance — [Propel](https://propelfinance.co.uk/our-solutions/growth-guarantee-scheme)
- Caple: "no personal guarantees, charges over company assets, fixed/floating charges, debentures, or warrants" — but minimum £500k — [Independent Banking Consultants](https://www.independentbankers.co.uk/caple-unsecured-business-loans/)
- Funding Circle: PG "Required for all loans"; flexible product "caps the guarantee at 2x the initial loan amount" — [Business Expert, 21 September 2026](https://www.businessexpert.co.uk/business-loans/funding-circle-business-loan-review/)
- Allica unsecured requires PG from UBO directors — [Allica](https://www.allica.bank/business-loans); iwoca "may also ask a company director for a personal guarantee" — [iwoca](https://www.iwoca.co.uk/small-business-loans); HSBC and Lloyds small business loans: PG required for limited companies/LLPs — [SmallBusiness.co.uk, 26 August 2026](https://smallbusiness.co.uk/best-small-business-loans-in-the-uk-2548223/)

### Inferences
- For a £150–250k loan a PG is effectively unavoidable; the negotiable items are the cap (aim for a percentage of the facility, citing the GGS 70% cover), joint-and-several terms, and release triggers. Without property, the PG's enforcement value is low, which lenders price in rather than waive.

### Gaps
- No lender page states a numeric PG cap policy for GGS loans; the 20–50% range is one adviser's observation.
