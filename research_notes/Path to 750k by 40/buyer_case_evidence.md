# Buyer case evidence: individuals buying £100k–£1m UK businesses, 2018–2026

Method note (read first). Web search budget for this session was exhausted part-way through, and several of the richest primary sources are blocked to automated fetching (Reddit, BusinessesForSale.com buyer case studies, Podtail, Podcast Addict, IMDb, and the Dealmakers/Dealmakers Academy sites, which are JavaScript-rendered and return empty pages). The result is that named, fully-documented UK cases in the £100k–£1m EV band are scarce below: most publicly indexed UK "I bought a business" stories are either (a) larger self-funded-search deals at £1m–£3m EV, (b) promotional course/broker testimonials with few numbers, or (c) forum threads by prospective rather than completed buyers. Where a case is above the target band or promotional it is flagged. Everything below is traceable to a URL; where a figure could not be traced it is listed under Gaps rather than stated.

## Key question 1: For a first-time UK buyer with sales strengths and no operating-manager appetite, what do the documented cases suggest about the likely first-24-months outcome?

### Takeaway
The documented cases point to a first 24 months dominated by operational firefighting rather than selling: the two best-documented deals (C1 UK, US1) both hit a liquidity or margin crisis inside 12–18 months despite competent, well-capitalised buyers, and both were rescued by outside equity or by the buyer personally grinding through customer attrition. No documented sub-£1m UK case shows a buyer who stayed genuinely hands-off in year one; the closest (C1) still spent roughly a day a week per business for 6–12 months and later hired a COO and CFO.

### Cited Findings
- Sam Turner's first UK acquisition (Dec 2021, ~£1.05m at 3x £350k EBITDA, £5m revenue) was financed with ~20% investor equity, a ~33% seller note repaid annually over 3 years and a cash-flow loan from an alternative lender (5-year, first year interest-only); he spent "approximately one day per week per business post-acquisition for the first 6-12 months" — [Acquiring Minds: Building an HVAC Holdco to £60m](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos)
- Within about 18 months that business's "wafer-thin margins" evaporated because fixed-price long-term contracts met 10–20% material-cost inflation and a housing slowdown; Turner "raised a little bit more equity to put into the business" because of insufficient liquidity, and group EBITDA only met its £1.4m budget because another acquisition "performed very well" while the first was "suffering a lot" — [Acquiring Minds: 4 Acquisitions In](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- Of the founders in Turner's first three acquisitions, "Two owners stayed; two departed. Promoted 'number twos' stepped up when founders left"; he now says "getting change with people if they're not that way of thinking" is "very, very difficult", is building a bench of potential MDs/GMs, and has hired a CFO and a COO rather than run the businesses himself — [Acquiring Minds: 4 Acquisitions In](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- Paul Quirk (ex-banker, no trade experience) bought a £4.5m-revenue Norwich window installer and was a "hands-on operator commuting weekly from Geneva to Norwich (Monday-Friday on-site)" who "heavily relies on retained owners' industry expertise" — two of the three sellers stayed as consultants for a minimum of 12 months — [Acquiring Minds: Paul Quirk / Amber Home Improvements](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- US comparator (flagged US, $1m deal): a sales-background buyer with "no experience in electronics distribution" saw revenue fall 65% year-on-year in year one after the retired seller left and "two of the top customers mentioned they were seeking his replacement"; the two largest customers ($300k+/yr) were lost, five more top customers retired, $25k of working capital proved "insufficient" against $12k+/month debt service, and the buyer suffered "severe anxiety/depression around month 6" before purchase orders recovered in months 9–10 — [Blake Harber, 12 months into our $1M acquisition](https://blakeharber.substack.com/p/12-months-into-our-1m-acquisition)
- The same buyer's stated lessons were to set "strict working boundaries" rather than "18 hour days", "play the long-term strategy", and accept that "small business is hard... many [years] can flat out suck" — [Blake Harber](https://blakeharber.substack.com/p/12-months-into-our-1m-acquisition)
- A Dealmakers Academy client ("Pete", promotional source) reports that a distressed "£1 deal" produced "unexpected £500k liabilities" and "key staff departed"; his first negotiated deal "took 2 years to negotiate" — [Business Buying Strategies podcast, Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/business-buying-strategies-from-the-dealmakers-academy/id1369703084)
- UK Business Forums members warned a 25-year-old considering a £10k hairdresser with £70k revenue that "customers go with the hairdresser so... you could see all the clients going elsewhere", that at that budget he would be "buying a job, not a business", and that "the failure rate for SMEs is around 80% in the first 5 years" (member claim, unsourced) — [UK Business Forums: Buying an existing business](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/buying-an-existing-business.413982/)
- Mike Finger's critique of the UK/US "buy a business for £1 / no money down" pitch argues it is "treasure for the well-capitalized, experienced buyer" but "trash" for "inexperienced, underfunded buyers", and cites HBR's 70–90% acquisition failure figure; Jeremy Harbour's comment on the post describes "inexperienced former students misrepresenting credentials" — [Mike Finger, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/buy-business-1-pitch-treasure-trash-mike-finger)
- Among international (non-US) traditional search funds, 79% of concluded funds acquired a company; of 146 acquirers, 15 had exited at a positive return and six "had exited their companies at a loss for investors"; the median fund returned 1.4x post-acquisition — [IESE International Search Funds 2024](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E) (note: excludes self-funded searchers and deals far larger than £1m; median purchase price $11.7m)

### Inferences
- For a sales-strength buyer who does not want to be the operating manager, the cases suggest the realistic year-one plan is: retain the seller or a number-two for a contracted 12 months minimum (C2), expect to be on site far more than planned, and hold working capital well above the deal-model number — the two best-documented cases were both pushed to an equity top-up or personal crisis by liquidity rather than by lack of sales.
- Owner-dependent trades (hairdressing, personal services) are the clearest "customers follow the seller" risk in the UK evidence; B2B distribution with retiring customer principals (US1) shows the same mechanism at £1m scale.
- The "profit vs plan" evidence is thin: only C1 reports against a budget (met at group level, missed at the individual first business). Expect year-one profit at or below the acquisition case rather than above it.

### Gaps
- No documented UK case in the £100k–£1m band reports owner hours in year one numerically; only C1 (~1 day/week per business) and C2 (Mon–Fri on site) give any indication, and both are above the band.
- No UK case found where the buyer explicitly installed a hired general manager from day one and reported the result; Turner's move to a COO/CFO structure came after 3–4 acquisitions.
- Reddit r/UKPersonalFinance, r/smallbusinessuk and r/EntrepreneurRideAlong could not be retrieved (blocked to automated fetching) so community-reported micro-deal outcomes are missing.

## Key question 2: Which sectors appear most often among the documented successes and failures at this size?

### Takeaway
The UK cases that reach the public record cluster in trades and building services (HVAC/M&E, plumbing, windows and doors, building energy management), B2B equipment supply, childcare, and services roll-ups (sports coaching); the documented failures or near-failures are fixed-price construction subcontracting (margin squeeze), owner-dependent personal services (client flight), distressed "£1" deals (hidden liabilities) and, in the US comparator, distribution with an ageing customer base.

### Cited Findings
- HVAC / heating and plumbing for new-build residential (UK, C1): 3x EBITDA entry; the subsector's fixed-price contracts made it "riskier than anticipated" once inflation hit — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos); Turner later diversified into other M&E subsectors because "the strong performer offset the weak one, potentially preventing total collapse" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- Window and door installation / conservatories (UK, C2): £4.5m revenue, £670k EBITDA, ~3x — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- Building energy management systems (UK, C5, £1m+ EBITDA), TV/film set storage and logistics (UK, C6), sports coaching (UK, C7, "7 acquisitions in 18 months"), doors manufacturing (UK, Will Hunnam, later a £30m-revenue acquisition — outside band) — [Buy and Build podcast, Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/buy-and-build/id1585550753) and [Podcast Republic listing](https://podcastrepublic.net/podcast/1585550753)
- NDT / testing and inspection equipment supply (UK, C3), acquired by an engineer + operations pairing intending to expand into logistics and manufacturing — [Business Sale Report success story](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/for-buyers/success-story-buying-a-business-directly-from-the-owner-218210) (promotional)
- Early-years nurseries (UK, C9): chosen because "growing, fragmented market" plus Ofsted "Outstanding" quality screen — [Cambridge Judge Business School](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/entrepreneurship-through-acquisition-a-cambridge-story/)
- Hairdressing (UK, C11): the one micro-deal in the forum evidence, with the explicit warning that clients follow the stylist — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/buying-an-existing-business.413982/)
- Electronics parts distribution (US1): customer attrition driven by retiring principals at customers, not by the buyer's competence — [Blake Harber](https://blakeharber.substack.com/p/12-months-into-our-1m-acquisition)
- Among the 57 completed UK ETA acquisitions tracked 2021–2026, the most frequent lenders were Shawbrook (12), HSBC UK (7), Arbuthnot ABL (4), Close Brothers (4) and Triple Point (3) — "predominantly specialist, challenger and private-credit names rather than high-street banks"; median UK ETA purchase price £5–10m at 4.5–6.0x EBITDA (i.e. the tracked ecosystem sits well above the £100k–£1m band) — [Exit Radar, UK ETA ecosystem 2026](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-eta-ecosystem)
- Business Sale Report's own "for buyers" insight pages currently carry sector pieces on HVAC M&A and facilities-management consolidation, consistent with trades being the most-marketed sub-£10m buy-and-build sectors — [Business Sale Report insights](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/for-buyers)
- Flagged composite (US): ClearlyAcquired's "10 SMB acquisition case studies" are anonymised/composite and show seller notes of 10–60% and an average 5.2-month close — [ClearlyAcquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/10-smb-acquisition-case-studies) — not to be treated as real cases.

### Inferences
- At sub-£1m EV the UK evidence is skewed towards trades/services with a workforce (so the buyer is not the sole producer) — the same pattern the ETA ecosystem shows one size up. Owner-as-producer businesses (salons, sole-trader consultancies) appear only as warnings.
- The failure signals cluster around contract structure (fixed price) and customer-relationship ownership, not around sector per se.

### Gaps
- No UK dataset was found giving success/failure rates by sector for individually-bought businesses under £1m; the Exit Radar and IESE data cover larger, investor-backed deals.
- Figures (EV, turnover, profit) are missing for C3, C6, C7, C8 because the primary episode pages/ transcripts were not retrievable.

## Key question 3: What handover and seller-finance structures appear in the good outcomes?

### Takeaway
The recurring pattern in the better-documented UK deals is roughly one-third seller/deferred consideration over 2–3 years, sellers retained for a contracted 12 months (as paid consultants rather than equity holders), and a warranty retention or deferred tranche of ~20% held for 24–48 months; buyers repeatedly report seller finance being stretched at the last minute to cover lender shortfalls.

### Cited Findings
- C1 structure: ~33% seller note "deferred over 3 years (annual installments)", ~20% investor equity, balance from an alternative lender on a 5-year cash-flow loan with one year interest-only; the same lender then "approved double the credit amount for future deals" although its "risk appetite subsequently reduced" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos)
- C1's fourth deal (2024/25) used equity in the group as part consideration ("gave him shares in our group"), with the founder staying on — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- C2 structure: alternative (P2P/private credit) lender + buyer savings; when "original lender fell short one week before close" the gap was bridged by "increased seller financing"; two of three sellers stayed as consultants "for minimum 12 months" without retaining equity; Quirk's lesson: "The debt market is completely different" in the UK — no SBA equivalent, higher rates, 5–6 year terms "requiring greater equity checks and seller financing to bridge gaps" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- C12 (seller side): sale to a German buyer where "payment took place over three calendar years and 13-14 months in time" and "their security was that I had to work during a hand-over period" — [UK Business Forums: instalments thread](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/if-you-were-selling-a-business-would-you-take-payment-in-instalments.391035/)
- Same thread: a member expects "a deferred amount (~20%) to be kept behind for warranties for 24-48 months"; another suggests a ~15% price premium for accepting instalments; security typically "fixed and floating charge; chattel mortgage or fixed charge over specific tangible assets; personal guarantees; suitable clauses/terms in the contract for default provisions" — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/if-you-were-selling-a-business-would-you-take-payment-in-instalments.391035/)
- Another UKBF thread states that for £1m–£10m deals "there is usually an element of deferred payment" and that offering it "generally means you can get a higher price" (search snippet; thread text not directly fetched) — [UK Business Forums search result](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/business-sale-advice.422831/)
- Lender marketing claims "approximately 90% of small business sales in the UK involve some form of seller financing" — this appeared in lender/legal marketing pages surfaced by search ([iwoca](https://www.iwoca.co.uk/finance-explained/seller-financing), [Swoop](https://swoopfunding.com/uk/business-loans/seller-financing/)); the underlying source was not located and the figure should be treated as unverified.
- Warranty protection in practice: SPAs commonly set de minimis thresholds "£5,000 or £10,000 for each head of claim", notification time limits "typically much shorter than any statutory limitation periods", and buyers must check "whether the seller will actually have the financial means to pay" — [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/warranty-claims-when-buying-a-business/)
- C3 (promotional): DD "kept to a minimum" on a "sold-as-seen" basis, two years' accounts reviewed, one site visit, contracts in nine weeks — [Business Sale Report](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/for-buyers/success-story-buying-a-business-directly-from-the-owner-218210)
- US comparator: SBA-backed deals in the anonymised ClearlyAcquired set combine 2–3 funding sources with seller notes of 10–60% — [ClearlyAcquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/10-smb-acquisition-case-studies) (composite, flagged)

### Inferences
- The "good outcome" structures share three features: (1) a seller note of roughly a third repaid over 2–3 years, which both bridges UK lender shortfalls and gives the buyer set-off leverage against warranty claims; (2) a paid, time-boxed (12-month) seller/founder consultancy rather than an open-ended earn-out; (3) a retention of ~20% for 24–48 months against warranties.
- Seller-note percentage in the UK cases is high partly because UK lenders (no SBA equivalent) size facilities conservatively; buyers should assume the lender number can drop in the final week and pre-agree a seller-finance fallback.

### Gaps
- No documented sub-£1m UK case gives a full capital stack in percentages; the only complete stacks (C1, C2) are at ~£1m–£2m EV.
- No UK case found that documents a formal earn-out (performance-linked) at this size; the cases use fixed deferred notes or consultancy retention instead.

## Additional collection point (1): Common structural patterns in successful sub-£1m UK owner-buyer deals

### Takeaway
Across the available UK cases the recurring structure is 3x EBITDA entry, ~20–35% of price deferred to the seller over 2–3 years, a 12-month retained-seller period, minimal formal DD on the smallest deals, and specialist/alternative lenders rather than high-street banks.

### Cited Findings
- 3x EBITDA entry in both C1 and C2 — [Acquiring Minds C1](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos); [Acquiring Minds C2](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- Alternative lenders with 5–6 year terms and interest-only first year (C1) — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos); UK ETA lender league table led by Shawbrook, HSBC, Arbuthnot, Close Brothers, Triple Point — [Exit Radar](https://www.exitradar.co.uk/blog/uk-eta-ecosystem)
- Turner's explicit "buy small to get in the game" thesis: a first deal below the lender's minimum "gets you in the door" and "improves credibility with lenders, investors, and sellers for future deals" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turn-hvac-advantos)
- Direct-to-owner sourcing ("Business Wanted" adverts, 70+ approaches over three years) produced C3's nine-week completion — [Business Sale Report](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/for-buyers/success-story-buying-a-business-directly-from-the-owner-218210) (promotional)
- Belgian micro-buyer's rule of thumb after walking away from a <€100k retail deal: examine "a minimum of 50 businesses, make an offer on 10" — [David Fonteyn](https://davidfonteyn.substack.com/p/the-business-buying-experiment)

### Inferences
- "Successful" at sub-£1m in the UK record mostly means "closed and still trading" rather than "hit the profit plan"; only C1 discloses performance against budget.

### Gaps
- No UK source found quantifying typical handover length for sub-£1m deals beyond the 12-month consultancy in C2 and the ~13-month worked handover in C12.

## Additional collection point (2): Documented failure modes

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: margin collapse from fixed-price contracts under inflation (C1); customers leaving with a retired seller or retiring customer principals (US1, warned in C11); hidden liabilities and staff exits in distressed "£1" deals (C4); lender shortfall in the final week before completion (C2); lenders refusing buyers without sector experience (C10); and working-capital shock from under-injecting cash against monthly debt service (US1). No documented UK case of a lender pulling a facility post-completion was found.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed-price contract margin collapse and need for emergency equity: C1 — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- Lender fell short one week before close; bridged by more seller finance: C2 — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- "Unexpected £500k liabilities surfaced, key staff departed" on a £1 distressed deal: C4 (promotional) — [Business Buying Strategies, Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/business-buying-strategies-from-the-dealmakers-academy/id1369703084)
- Course attendee spent >£20k plus ~£10k on data/targeting and "struggled to complete acquisitions due to unforeseen lending restrictions regarding sector experience"; the founder's reply positioned add-ons as optional and said direct-mail costs should be "under £900" — [Trustpilot: Dealmakers](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/dealmakers.co.uk)
- Seller retired and two top customers left; five more top customers retired; $25k working capital "insufficient" against $12k+/month debt service; revenue -65%: US1 — [Blake Harber](https://blakeharber.substack.com/p/12-months-into-our-1m-acquisition)
- Client flight in owner-dependent trades: "customers go with the hairdresser" — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/buying-an-existing-business.413982/)
- Seller's default risk on deferred consideration: "if your business fails, or you become incapacitated, you won't be generating any cash" — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/if-you-were-selling-a-business-would-you-take-payment-in-instalments.391035/)
- Partner/shareholder dispute as a failure mode (promotional, founder's own story): a partner "refused non-compete clause, withdrew contacts, later demanded £10.5m", costing "£50,000 in legal fees alone" — [Business Buying Strategies, Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/business-buying-strategies-from-the-dealmakers-academy/id1369703084)
- Marketplace quality: UKBF members report BusinessesForSale.com listings attract "timewasters and dreamers", "very few real businesses, and the ones that were IMO were overpriced" — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/your-experience-of-businessesforsale-com.216999/)
- Search-fund level: "nearly 40% of those who have not signed an LOI during the first 12 months did not acquire a business"; investors stress "the risk of seller retraction during due diligence, especially nearing the closure of a deal" — [IESE 2024](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)

### Inferences
- The two UK cases with the fullest disclosure both failed on the lender/liquidity axis rather than on customer loss; the customer-loss cases are US or hypothetical-UK. A UK buyer should model both.

### Gaps
- No UK case found of a hired manager quitting post-completion (Turner's founders leaving is the nearest).
- No UK case found of a lender withdrawing a facility after completion; Turner notes only that his lender's "risk appetite subsequently reduced".
- No UK first-hand account found of a buyer defaulting on a vendor loan.

## Additional collection point (3): Data on how many UK "acquisition entrepreneur" course attendees complete a deal

### Takeaway
No independent completion-rate data exists in the public record for UK course attendees; the only numbers are the provider's own headline ("13,000 dealmakers" trained), a 4.8/5 Trustpilot score with no review confirming a completed acquisition, and one detailed 1-star review of a non-completer who spent ~£30k. For investor-backed search funds, ~65% of UK searchers acquire within two years (Exit Radar estimate) and 79% of concluded international search funds acquired (IESE) — but these populations are not comparable to course attendees.

### Cited Findings

- Provider-side case study: "Pete" went "from earning £50k annually" to co-owning a "£6 million" revenue group, with the first deal taking two years and later ones "6-7 months using Jonathan's approach" — [Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/business-buying-strategies-from-the-dealmakers-academy/id1369703084) (promotional)

### Inferences
- With 13,000+ attendees claimed and only a handful of named case studies recycled across the provider's own channels, the implied completion rate is very low, but this cannot be quantified from public data.

### Gaps
- No survey, regulator finding or journalist investigation giving a completion percentage for Dealmakers Academy, Dan Bradbury, Carl Allen or similar UK programmes was found. Searches for Reddit threads on these programmes could not be executed (budget exhausted / Reddit blocked).

## Additional collection point (4): Time from starting to look → offer → completion, and DD costs actually paid

### Takeaway
UK buyer accounts range from 9 weeks (direct-from-owner, minimal DD) to 18 months–3 years of searching; the legal/advisory norm quoted is 3–5 months from heads of terms to completion for deals under £2m; search-fund data puts median start-to-close at 21 months. Not one UK case discloses the DD fees actually paid.

### Cited Findings
- C3: "approximately three years actively searching... approaching more than 70 businesses", then "9 weeks from initial contact to contract signing" — [Business Sale Report](https://www.business-sale.com/insights/for-buyers/success-story-buying-a-business-directly-from-the-owner-218210) (promotional)
- C2: "Searched approximately 18 months (end of 2020 to September 2022 close)"; deal costs "not specifically disclosed" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/paul-quirk-amber-home-improvements)
- C1: fourth deal "slipped two months due to seller availability alone"; lesson "pad acquisition timelines by 1-2 months" — [Acquiring Minds](https://acquiringminds.co/articles/sam-turner-advantos)
- C4 (promotional): first deal "took 2 years to negotiate"; later deals "6-7 months" — [Apple listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/business-buying-strategies-from-the-dealmakers-academy/id1369703084)
- US2: search launched March, LOI May, closed September — ~6 months — [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/self-funded-acquisition-a-few-things-i-learned)
- Legal norm: "A deal can take three to five months from heads of terms to completion" for uncomplicated deals under £2m; DD report "typically within two weeks" of review; SPA negotiation "one week to several months"; post-completion admin "2-3 weeks"; no fee figures given — [Zoppi & Co](https://www.zoppi.co.uk/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-buy-a-business-uk-heads-of-terms-to-completion)
- Search funds: median 21 months from start of search to close (12% under 11 months, 37% 11–20, 35% 21–30, 16% 31+); successful searchers signed first LOI on average in month 7 vs month 9 for those who failed — [IESE 2024](https://www.iese.edu/media/research/pdfs/ST-0658-E)
- Generic DD cost benchmark (not UK-specific, not a paid case): "0.2% and 4% of deal value"; "$25,000–$75,000 for small business acquisitions" — [datarooms.org](https://datarooms.org/vdr-blog/due-diligence-costs/)
- Course-related search costs actually paid: ~£10k on "data and targeting" on top of >£20k tuition (non-completer) — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/dealmakers.co.uk)

### Inferences
- The UK pattern is a long search (12–36 months) followed by a short close (2–5 months) once a motivated direct seller is found; broker-listed deals appear to take longer and attract more time-wasting on both sides.

### Gaps
- No UK buyer in any retrieved source states the legal, financial DD or lender arrangement fees actually paid on a sub-£1m deal.
- Buy and Build episode pages for C5–C8 and the Acquiring Minds UK episodes beyond C1/C2 could not be retrieved for timeline detail.
